# Russian Grammar from A1 to C2

## A complete learner's reference with examples and English translations

**Scope:** the grammar needed to move from first sentences to precise, idiomatic and stylistically controlled Russian.  
**Standard:** modern standard Russian. Colloquial, formal and literary differences are marked where relevant.  
**Example format:** Russian appears first; the English meaning follows after an em dash. Stress marks are added only when pronunciation or contrast matters.

---

## How to use this guide

CEFR levels describe what a learner can do, not a single official grammar syllabus. The placement of a few topics therefore varies between courses. This guide uses a practical sequence:

- **A1:** build correct simple sentences in the most frequent cases and tenses.
- **A2:** complete the core case system; introduce aspect and motion.
- **B1:** connect clauses, narrate, explain and choose aspect more reliably.
- **B2:** compress information with participles and gerunds; control government, particles and register.
- **C1:** handle fine meaning differences, formal syntax and stylistic alternatives.
- **C2:** manipulate grammar deliberately for emphasis, genre, rhythm and idiomatic effect.

Do not wait to “finish” one level before reading the next. Learn each rule in three passes: recognise it, produce it with a model, then use it spontaneously. Every noun should be learned with gender and an example of its genitive; every verb with aspect, government and a natural sentence.

## Notation

- **N, G, D, A, I, P** = nominative, genitive, dative, accusative, instrumental and prepositional.
- **SG / PL** = singular / plural.
- **IPFV / PFV** = imperfective / perfective aspect.
- An asterisk marks a non-standard or incorrect form: `*Я есть студент`.
- Parentheses show an optional element or a predictable form.
- `кто? / что?` and similar labels give the traditional Russian case questions. They are memory aids, not complete definitions.

# Part I. Foundations used at every level

## 1. Alphabet, sound, stress and spelling

Russian uses 33 Cyrillic letters. Grammar is visible in word endings, so accurate spelling matters from the beginning.

### 1.1 Hard and soft consonants

Most consonants have a hard and a soft version. The following vowel normally signals the distinction:

- hard series: **а, о, у, э, ы**
- softening series: **я, ё, ю, е, и**
- **ь** softens or separates but has no sound of its own; **ъ** separates a consonant from a following iotated vowel.

Compare **брат** “brother” and **брать** “to take”; **угол** “corner” and **уголь** “coal”. Softness can distinguish words and endings.

### 1.2 Stress

Russian stress is mobile and usually unwritten. It can distinguish forms:

- **ру́ки** — hands; **руки́** — of a hand
- **о́кна** — windows; **окна́** — of a window
- **пи́сать** — to urinate; **писа́ть** — to write

Unstressed **о** is pronounced closer to [a], and unstressed **е/я** are reduced. Learn stress with every word. The letter **ё** is always stressed, although printed Russian often writes it as **е**.

### 1.3 The spelling rules

These rules control many endings:

1. After **г, к, х, ж, ч, ш, щ**, write **и**, not **ы**: **книги, русские**.
2. After **ж, ч, ш, щ, ц**, do not write unstressed **о**; write **е**: **свежего, хорошего**. Under stress, **о** may occur: **большо́й**.
3. After **ж, ч, ш, щ**, write **а/у**, not **я/ю**: **чашка, чудо**.
4. After **ц**, **ы** occurs in endings and a few roots (**улицы, цыган**), but **и** is usual elsewhere (**цирк**).

## 2. The architecture of a Russian sentence

Russian is highly inflected: endings show the role of a word. Word order is flexible, but not meaningless.

- Neutral order: **Маша читает книгу.** — Masha is reading a book.
- The end often carries new or contrastive information: **Книгу читает Маша.** — It is Masha who is reading the book.
- Subject pronouns may be omitted when context is clear: **Работаю дома.** — I work / am working at home.

Russian has no articles. Context, word order, aspect and demonstratives express meanings often carried by English *a/the*:

- **На столе лежит книга.** — There is a book on the table.
- **Книга очень старая.** — The book is very old.
- **Эта книга очень старая.** — This particular book is very old.

## 3. The six cases at a glance

| Case | Core jobs | Frequent cues | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nominative | subject; dictionary form; identification | кто? что? | **Это новый студент.** — This is a new student. |
| Genitive | possession, absence, source, quantity | кого? чего?; у, из, с, без, для | **У меня нет времени.** — I have no time. |
| Dative | recipient, experiencer, destination toward | кому? чему?; к, по | **Я звоню сестре.** — I call my sister. |
| Accusative | direct object, destination into/onto | кого? что?; в, на | **Я вижу сестру.** — I see my sister. |
| Instrumental | means, companion, role, complement | кем? чем?; с, над, под, перед | **Я говорю с сестрой.** — I speak with my sister. |
| Prepositional | location, topic; only after prepositions | о ком? о чём?; в, на, о | **Я думаю о сестре.** — I think about my sister. |

The meanings above are centres, not one-to-one translations. Case choice is also governed by particular verbs, adjectives and prepositions.

## 4. Gender, number and animacy

### 4.1 Gender

- Masculine nouns usually end in a consonant or **-й**: **стол, музей**.
- Feminine nouns usually end in **-а/-я**: **книга, неделя**.
- Neuter nouns usually end in **-о/-е**: **окно, море**.
- Nouns ending in **-ь** must be learned: **словарь** is masculine; **дверь** is feminine.
- Natural gender can override shape: **папа, дядя, мужчина** are masculine.

Past-tense verbs and adjectives agree with gender: **Он устал. Она устала. Окно было открыто.**

### 4.2 Number

Common nominative plural endings are **-ы/-и** for masculine/feminine and **-а/-я** for neuter, but many common plurals are irregular: **друг — друзья, брат — братья, человек — люди, ребёнок — дети**.

### 4.3 Animacy

Animacy affects the accusative of masculine singular nouns and all plural nouns/adjectives:

- inanimate A = N: **Я вижу новый стол / новые столы.**
- animate A = G: **Я вижу нового студента / новых студентов.**

Grammatical animacy is lexical. **Кукла** “doll” and playing-card **туз** “ace” can behave as animate; **труп** “corpse” is normally inanimate.

# Part II. A1 — building the core sentence

## A1 outcomes

By the end of A1, you should be able to identify people and things, describe them, speak about routines and simple past/future events, ask questions, express possession, location, destination, likes, ability and basic quantities.

## 5. “To be,” identification and existence

### 5.1 Present-tense identity

The present tense of **быть** “to be” is normally omitted:

- **Я студент.** — I am a student.
- **Она дома.** — She is at home.
- **Москва — столица России.** — Moscow is the capital of Russia.

Use a dash especially when both sides are nouns, the sentence is formal, or the pause is contrastive. **есть** is not a general replacement for English *am/is/are*. It stresses existence or availability:

- **У вас есть паспорт?** — Do you have a passport?
- **В городе есть метро.** — There is a metro in the city.
- **Он врач.**, not normally `*Он есть врач.`

Past and future use **быть** normally: **Я был дома. Она будет врачом.**

### 5.2 Possession: у + genitive

Russian normally says that something exists “at” a possessor:

- **У меня есть машина.** — I have a car.
- **У Анны новая работа.** — Anna has a new job.
- **У нас нет машины.** — We do not have a car.

Omit **есть** when the point is the description, location or number of a known possession. Use it when asserting existence or asking whether something exists.

## 6. Personal, possessive and demonstrative pronouns

| Person | Nominative | Genitive/Accusative | Dative | Instrumental | Prepositional |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| I | я | меня | мне | мной/мною | мне |
| you SG | ты | тебя | тебе | тобой/тобою | тебе |
| he/it M | он | его | ему | им | нём |
| she | она | её | ей | ею/ей | ней |
| we | мы | нас | нам | нами | нас |
| you PL/formal | вы | вас | вам | вами | вас |
| they | они | их | им | ими | них |

After most prepositions, third-person pronouns add **н-**: **у него, к ней, с ними, о нём**. No **н-** appears when the pronoun is a direct object: **Я вижу его.**

Possessives **мой, твой, наш, ваш, свой** agree like adjectives. **его, её, их** never change:

- **моя книга, в моей книге** — my book, in my book
- **их дом, в их доме** — their house, in their house

Use **свой** when the possessor is the grammatical subject:

- **Анна любит свою работу.** — Anna loves her own job.
- **Анна любит её работу.** — Anna loves another woman's job.

Demonstratives **этот** “this” and **тот** “that” agree: **эта книга, это окно, те люди**. The neuter **это** also acts as an invariable identifier: **Это мои друзья.**

## 7. Nominative agreement: nouns and adjectives

Long adjectives agree with their noun in gender, number and case.

| Form | Hard stem model **новый** | Soft-type model **синий** |
|---|---|---|
| M SG | новый | синий |
| F SG | новая | синяя |
| N SG | новое | синее |
| PL | новые | синие |

- **новый русский фильм** — a new Russian film
- **интересная большая книга** — an interesting big book
- **Это кафе новое.** — This café is new.

Adjectives usually precede nouns. A postposed adjective can sound predicative, contrastive or literary: **Мне нужен билет обратный, не обычный.** — I need a return ticket, not a standard one.

## 8. Present-tense verbs

Russian verbs have an infinitive, usually in **-ть**, and conjugate for person and number. There are two main conjugations.

| Person | First conjugation: читать | Second conjugation: говорить |
|---|---|---|
| я | читаю | говорю |
| ты | читаешь | говоришь |
| он/она | читает | говорит |
| мы | читаем | говорим |
| вы | читаете | говорите |
| они | читают | говорят |

Consonant changes are common: **писать — пишу**, **любить — люблю**, **платить — плачу**. Learn the **я**, **ты** and **они** forms with every unpredictable verb.

Important irregulars:

- **быть:** future **буду, будешь, будет, будем, будете, будут**
- **есть** “eat”: **ем, ешь, ест, едим, едите, едят**
- **дать** “give”: **дам, дашь, даст, дадим, дадите, дадут**
- **хотеть:** **хочу, хочешь, хочет, хотим, хотите, хотят**
- **мочь:** **могу, можешь, может, можем, можете, могут**

The Russian present can translate both English simple and continuous forms: **Я читаю** = “I read / I am reading.” Context supplies the distinction.

## 9. Negation, questions and conjunctions

### 9.1 Negation

Put **не** before the word being negated:

- **Я не знаю.** — I do not know.
- **Он не врач.** — He is not a doctor.
- **Это не новая, а старая книга.** — This is not a new book but an old one.

Russian allows and normally requires negative concord:

- **Я ничего не понимаю.** — I do not understand anything.
- **Никто не пришёл.** — Nobody came.

### 9.2 Questions

Yes/no questions use intonation, not inversion: **Вы говорите по-русски?** Question words normally stand near the front:

- **Кто это? Что вы читаете? Где Анна? Когда урок? Почему? Как?**

Useful conjunctions are **и** and, **а** and/but/whereas, **но** but, **или** or, **потому что** because, **что** that:

- **Я живу в Лондоне, а брат живёт в Москве.** — I live in London, while my brother lives in Moscow.

## 10. Past and future

### 10.1 Past

Remove infinitive **-ть** and add **-л** plus agreement in most verbs:

| M | F | N | PL |
|---|---|---|---|
| читал | читала | читало | читали |

- **Вчера я работал / работала дома.** — Yesterday I worked at home.
- **Дети играли.** — The children were playing.

Some masculine forms lack **-л**: **нести — нёс**, **идти — шёл**, **умереть — умер**.

### 10.2 Compound future

For an imperfective verb, use future **быть + infinitive**:

- **Я буду работать завтра.** — I will be working / will work tomorrow.
- **Мы будем читать.** — We will read / be reading.

Perfective verbs use their conjugated “present-looking” forms for future meaning: **Я прочитаю книгу.** — I will read the book through. Aspect is developed from A2 onward.

## 11. The accusative: objects and destinations

### 11.1 Nouns

- Feminine **-а → -у**: **книга → книгу**.
- Feminine **-я → -ю**: **неделя → неделю**.
- Neuter and inanimate masculine usually do not change.
- Animate masculine uses the genitive form: **Я вижу брата.**

### 11.2 Adjectives

- feminine **-ая → -ую**, **-яя → -юю**: **новую книгу, синюю рубашку**
- masculine animate: **нового студента**
- masculine inanimate/neuter: same as nominative: **новый стол, новое окно**

### 11.3 Motion into or onto

Use **в/на + A** for destination and **в/на + P** for static location:

- **Я иду в школу. Я учусь в школе.** — I am going to school. I study at school.
- **Мы едем на работу. Мы на работе.** — We are going to work. We are at work.

## 12. The prepositional: location and topic

Common singular endings are **-е**, with feminine **-ия** and neuter **-ие** giving **-ии**:

- **в городе, в школе, на море**
- **в России, в здании**

Use **о/об + P** for “about”: **Мы говорим о новом фильме.** Use **об** before a vowel sound: **об Анне**; **обо мне** is fixed.

Some masculine nouns take stressed **-у/-ю** for location after **в/на**: **в лесу́, на мосту́, в шкафу́**, but use ordinary **-е** for topic: **о ле́се, о мо́сте**.

## 13. Basic genitive: possession, absence and quantity

Common singular patterns:

- masculine consonant: **стол → стола**
- masculine **-й/-ь**: **музей → музея; словарь → словаря**
- feminine **-а/-я**: **книга → книги; неделя → недели**
- feminine **-ь**: **дверь → двери**
- neuter **-о/-е**: **окно → окна; море → моря**

Use the genitive:

- after **нет**: **Нет времени.** — There is no time.
- for possession/relationship: **книга Анны, центр города**
- after **из, с, от, до, без, для, около**: **из России, без сахара**
- after **два/три/четыре** in the basic counting pattern: **два студента, три книги, четыре окна**
- after **пять** and higher: **пять студентов, десять книг**

## 14. Basic dative, likes and necessity

The dative marks a recipient or experiencer:

- **Я даю книгу другу.** — I give the book to a friend.
- **Мне нравится Москва.** — I like Moscow; literally, Moscow is pleasing to me.
- **Мне нравятся русские фильмы.** — I like Russian films. The verb agrees with what is liked.
- **Мне двадцать лет.** — I am twenty years old.
- **Мне холодно / трудно / интересно.** — I am cold / find it difficult / am interested.

With **нужно/надо/можно/нельзя + infinitive**, the experiencer is dative:

- **Мне нужно работать.** — I need to work.
- **Здесь нельзя курить.** — Smoking is not allowed here.

## 15. Basic instrumental and “with”

Use **с + I** for accompaniment and the bare instrumental for means:

- **Я говорю с новым преподавателем.** — I speak with the new teacher.
- **Я пишу ручкой.** — I write with a pen.

After **быть, стать, работать** and similar verbs, a profession, role or resulting state often takes instrumental:

- **Она была врачом. Он хочет стать инженером. Я работаю переводчиком.**

Do not confuse **с + G** “from/off” with **с + I** “with”: **с работы** from work; **с коллегой** with a colleague.

## 16. Numbers, dates and time at A1

- **один** agrees: **один стол, одна книга, одно окно**.
- **два** has **два** M/N and **две** F: **два окна, две книги**.
- Clock time: **Сейчас три часа. Урок в пять часов.**
- Age: **Мне двадцать один год / двадцать два года / двадцать пять лет.**
- Dates are ordinal: **Сегодня первое мая.** — Today is the first of May. **Я родился пятого мая.** — I was born on the fifth of May.

## A1 pitfalls

- Do not insert **есть** into every present-tense identity sentence.
- Make every adjective agree with its noun.
- Distinguish location (**в школе**) from destination (**в школу**).
- Remember animacy in masculine accusative (**вижу брата**, but **вижу стол**).
- In **мне нравится книга**, the liked item—not the person—controls verb number.
- Learn stress and genitive with every noun; learn conjugation and aspect labels with every verb.

## A1 can-do check

You are ready for A2 when you can produce sentences such as:

1. **У моей сестры есть маленькая квартира в центре.** — My sister has a small flat in the centre.
2. **Вчера мы были в музее, а завтра будем работать.** — Yesterday we were at the museum, and tomorrow we will work.
3. **Мне нравится этот фильм, потому что он интересный.** — I like this film because it is interesting.
4. **Я вижу нового преподавателя и говорю с ним.** — I see the new teacher and speak with him.

# Part III. A2 — completing the everyday grammar system

## A2 outcomes

At A2, you consolidate all six cases, distinguish processes from completed events, describe directed and habitual motion, compare things, give commands, talk about people and places in connected sentences, and manage everyday quantities and dates.

## 17. The case system in working detail

### 17.1 Genitive meanings and prepositions

The genitive expresses:

- **absence:** **У меня нет билета.** — I do not have a ticket.
- **possession/whole:** **машина брата; конец фильма** — my brother's car; the end of the film
- **origin or movement away:** **из дома, с работы, от врача** — from the house, from work, from the doctor
- **limit/distance:** **до станции, около дома** — as far as the station, near the house
- **purpose/benefit:** **для детей** — for children
- **exclusion/lack:** **без ошибок** — without mistakes
- **comparison after a comparative:** **Анна старше брата.** — Anna is older than her brother.
- **quantity:** **много работы, чашка чая** — much work, a cup of tea

Genitive plural is one of the least predictable forms. Common patterns are:

- many masculine nouns: **-ов/-ев/-ей** — **столов, музеев, словарей**
- feminine **-а** often zero ending — **книг, школ**, sometimes with stem changes: **девушка — девушек**
- feminine **-я/-ь** often **-ь/-ей** — **недель, дверей**
- neuter **-о** often zero — **окон, мест**; **-е** may give **-ей** or another form — **морей, полей**

Learn frequent irregulars: **людей, детей, друзей, братьев, сестёр, имён, времён, денег**.

### 17.2 Dative meanings and prepositions

Singular endings: M/N often **-у/-ю**; F **-е** or **-и**. Plural is **-ам/-ям**.

- recipient: **Я написал другу.** — I wrote to a friend.
- experiencer/state: **Ей весело.** — She is having fun.
- age: **Сыну пять лет.** — The son is five.
- obligation: **Нам надо уйти.** — We have to leave.
- direction toward with **к**: **подойти к окну** — approach the window
- distribution/movement along with **по**: **идти по улице; дать по книге** — walk along the street; give one book each

Many verbs govern dative: **помогать кому**, **звонить кому**, **советовать кому**, **мешать кому**, **верить кому/чему**.

### 17.3 Instrumental meanings and prepositions

Singular endings: M/N **-ом/-ем**; F **-ой/-ей** (formal/literary **-ою/-ею**). Plural **-ами/-ями**.

- accompaniment: **с друзьями** — with friends
- means/instrument: **ехать автобусом; резать ножом** — go by bus; cut with a knife
- profession/status: **стать учителем** — become a teacher
- agent in a passive: **дом построен архитектором** — the house was designed/built by the architect
- spatial prepositions: **над столом, под мостом, перед домом, за школой, между городами**
- interests/control after governed verbs: **интересоваться музыкой, заниматься спортом, управлять компанией**

### 17.4 Prepositional meanings

- location after **в/на**: **в университете, на почте**
- topic after **о/об/обо**: **о путешествии, обо мне**
- time with **в** plus months/years/centuries: **в июле, в 2026 году, в двадцатом веке**
- certain fixed contexts after **при**: **при Петре Первом; при хорошей погоде** — under Peter the Great; in good weather

### 17.5 Case contrasts with one preposition

| Contrast | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| в/на + A vs P | destination vs location | **в комнату / в комнате** |
| за + A vs I | fetch/behind or duration vs position behind | **идти за хлебом; сесть за стол; быть за столом** |
| под + A vs I | movement under vs location under | **положить под стол / лежать под столом** |
| с + G vs I | from/off vs with | **с полки / с другом** |
| по + D vs A/P | along/by vs up to/after in fixed uses | **по дороге; по средам; по окончании** |

## 18. Adjectives and possessives in all cases

The adjective normally carries a highly visible case ending. The most useful singular pattern is:

| Case | Masculine/Neuter | Feminine | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| N | новый / новое | новая | **новый дом** |
| G | нового | новой | **около нового дома** |
| D | новому | новой | **к новому дому** |
| A | N or G / новое | новую | **вижу новый дом / нового друга / новую школу** |
| I | новым | новой | **перед новым домом** |
| P | новом | новой | **в новом доме** |

Plural: N **-ые/-ие**; G/P **-ых/-их**; D **-ым/-им**; I **-ыми/-ими**; A follows animacy.

Possessives **мой, твой, свой** follow a pronominal pattern: **моего, моему, моим, моём; моей; моих**. **наш, ваш** use **нашего, вашему** and so on.

## 19. Verbal aspect: first working system

Most Russian verbs belong to an aspectual pair:

- IPFV **читать** — read, be reading, read habitually
- PFV **прочитать** — read through and reach a boundary/completion

Aspect is not simply “long versus short” or “imperfect versus perfect tense.” It presents an event from inside (IPFV) or as a bounded whole/result (PFV).

### 19.1 Use the imperfective for

- an action in progress: **Я читал, когда она позвонила.** — I was reading when she called.
- repetition/habit: **Я каждый день читаю.** — I read every day.
- general ability/activity: **Ты читаешь по-русски?** — Can/do you read in Russian?
- an action with no focus on result: **Вчера я читал эту статью.** — I spent time reading / had experience of this article.
- compound future: **Я буду читать.**

### 19.2 Use the perfective for

- a completed result: **Я прочитал статью.** — I finished/read the article.
- a single bounded event in a sequence: **Он открыл дверь, вошёл и сел.** — He opened the door, came in and sat down.
- a future result: **Я прочитаю статью вечером.** — I will read the article tonight.

Perfective verbs have no true present. **Я прочитаю** refers to the future.

### 19.3 Common pairs

| Imperfective | Perfective | Core contrast |
|---|---|---|
| делать | сделать | do / get done |
| писать | написать | write / produce in writing |
| читать | прочитать | read / read through |
| покупать | купить | buy/shop for / make the purchase |
| смотреть | посмотреть | watch/look / watch or look for a bounded time/result |
| говорить | сказать | speak/say / say once |
| брать | взять | take / take once, obtain |
| решать | решить | work on/decide repeatedly / solve or decide |

Pairs can have different roots. Learn them as vocabulary.

## 20. Reflexive verbs in -ся/-сь

Attach **-сь** after a vowel and **-ся** after a consonant: **учусь, учится**. Main meanings include:

- true reflexive: **Он моется.** — He washes himself.
- reciprocal: **Мы встретились.** — We met (each other).
- intransitive/change of state: **Дверь открывается.** — The door opens/is opening.
- passive-like/general: **Дом строится.** — The house is being built.
- lexical meaning to learn whole: **бояться** fear (+ G), **смеяться** laugh, **надеяться** hope (+ на A), **пользоваться** use (+ I).

Not every English reflexive is Russian reflexive and vice versa: **Я чувствую себя хорошо** — I feel well; **Я интересуюсь музыкой** — I am interested in music.

## 21. Verbs of motion without prefixes

Russian distinguishes movement in one direction/on one trip from multidirectional, repeated or general movement.

| One direction / underway | Multidirectional / habitual | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| идти | ходить | go on foot |
| ехать | ездить | go by transport |
| бежать | бегать | run |
| лететь | летать | fly |
| плыть | плавать | swim/sail |
| нести | носить | carry on foot |
| везти | возить | transport |
| вести | водить | lead/drive |

- **Сейчас я иду в магазин.** — I am going to the shop now (on foot).
- **Я часто хожу в этот магазин.** — I often go to this shop.
- **Вчера я ходил в музей.** — I went to the museum and came back / made a trip.
- **Когда я шёл домой, шёл дождь.** — While I was walking home, it was raining.

Mode matters: **идти/ходить** on foot; **ехать/ездить** by vehicle. English *go* does not encode this.

## 22. Imperatives, requests and prohibitions

Common imperative endings are **-й**, **-и** or a bare softened stem; plural/formal adds **-те**:

- **читай(те), говори(те), встань(те), дай(те)**
- **Будьте добры, повторите.** — Please repeat.

For a negative instruction, imperfective is the neutral choice, especially “do not do this activity”: **Не курите здесь. Не открывай окно.** Perfective negative warns against an accidental/unwanted result: **Не упади! Не забудьте!** This distinction deepens at B1/B2.

First-person plural suggestions use **давай(те)**:

- **Давай пойдём домой.** — Let's go home.
- **Давайте читать.** — Let's read / start reading.

## 23. Comparatives and superlatives

The simple comparative is invariable, often **-ее/-ей**:

- **быстрый → быстрее; интересный → интереснее**
- irregular: **хороший → лучше; плохой → хуже; большой → больше; маленький → меньше**

- **Москва больше Лондона.** — Moscow is larger than London. (comparison noun in G)
- **Этот текст легче, чем тот.** — This text is easier than that one.

The compound comparative uses **более/менее + adjective**: **более важный вопрос**. Superlatives use **самый + adjective**: **самый интересный фильм**, or more formal/literary **наиболее важный**.

## 24. Adverbs, impersonal predicates and modal meaning

Many adverbs derive from adjectives: **быстрый → быстро; хороший → хорошо**. Distinguish:

- adjective with noun: **интересная книга**
- adverb with verb: **Он интересно говорит.**
- impersonal predicate with dative experiencer: **Мне интересно.**

Common predicatives: **можно** permitted/possible, **нельзя** forbidden/impossible, **нужно/надо** necessary, **жаль** regrettable, **пора** time to, **видно/слышно** visible/audible.

- **Отсюда видно море.** — The sea is visible from here.
- **Нам пора уходить.** — It is time for us to leave.

## 25. Connecting everyday sentences

### 25.1 Object and reported-thought clauses

- **Я знаю, что он дома.** — I know that he is at home.
- **Она сказала, что придёт завтра.** — She said that she would come tomorrow.

Russian does not normally backshift tense in reported speech. The tense remains relative to the actual situation.

### 25.2 Time, cause and condition

- **Когда я работаю, я слушаю музыку.** — When I work, I listen to music.
- **Я остался дома, потому что заболел.** — I stayed home because I became ill.
- **Если будет тепло, мы пойдём гулять.** — If it is warm, we will go for a walk.

### 25.3 Purpose with чтобы

Use **чтобы + past-form verb** when the subjects differ or when expressing a desired action:

- **Я хочу, чтобы ты пришёл / пришла.** — I want you to come.

When the subject is the same, an infinitive is usually simpler: **Я пришёл поговорить.** — I came to talk.

## 26. Indefinite and negative pronouns: introduction

- **кто-то, что-то, где-то** = someone/something/somewhere known or factual but unidentified
- **кто-нибудь, что-нибудь, где-нибудь** = anyone/anything/anywhere non-specific, especially in questions, conditions and commands
- **никто, ничто, нигде, никогда** = nobody, nothing, nowhere, never, used with **не**

Examples:

- **Кто-то звонил.** — Someone called.
- **Кто-нибудь знает ответ?** — Does anyone know the answer?
- **Я никогда там не был.** — I have never been there.

## 27. Quantities, dates and the genitive

After a nominative/accusative cardinal number:

- **один** + matching singular
- **два/три/четыре** + genitive singular
- **пять–двадцать**, and numbers ending 5–9 or 0, + genitive plural
- compound numbers follow the final word: **двадцать одна книга, двадцать два студента, двадцать пять книг**

After other cases, both numeral and noun decline, a B1/B2 topic: **с двумя друзьями**.

Approximation can reverse order: **два часа** exactly/about two hours; **часа два** about two hours.

## A2 pitfalls

- Case follows the governing word, not an English preposition: **помогать другу**, not a translation of “help to.”
- A round trip in the past is usually **ходил/ездил**, not **шёл/ехал**.
- Do not treat perfective as “past”; it also forms a simple future.
- Learn reflexive verbs with their government: **интересоваться чем**, **бояться кого/чего**.
- Distinguish **из/в**, **с/на** and **от/к** as paired routes: **в школу — из школы; на работу — с работы; к врачу — от врача**.

## A2 can-do check

1. **Вчера я ходил в библиотеку и взял две новые книги.** — Yesterday I went to the library and borrowed two new books.
2. **Когда я прочитаю статью, я позвоню преподавателю.** — When I finish the article, I will call the teacher.
3. **Мне нужно встретиться с коллегами, чтобы обсудить проект.** — I need to meet my colleagues to discuss the project.
4. **Этот маршрут короче, но по нему труднее идти.** — This route is shorter, but it is harder to walk along it.

# Part IV. B1 — narration, explanation and reliable choices

## B1 outcomes

At B1, you should tell a coherent story, distinguish process, repetition and result, handle prefixed motion, express wishes and hypothetical conditions, use relative and purpose clauses, and vary word order without losing clarity.

## 28. Aspect in narrative and interaction

### 28.1 Process, fact and result

Compare three common past meanings:

- process: **В восемь часов я ещё писал письмо.** — At eight I was still writing the letter.
- fact/experience: **Я уже писал ему.** — I have written to him before / the act occurred.
- result: **Я написал письмо.** — I wrote and completed the letter.

The “general factual” imperfective reports whether an event occurred without foregrounding its result:

- **Ты читал «Войну и мир»?** — Have you read / do you know *War and Peace*?
- **Кто открывал окно?** — Who opened/has been opening the window? (It is now closed or the identity/action matters more than a present result.)
- **Кто открыл окно?** — Who opened the window? (It is open; resulting state matters.)

### 28.2 Repeated bounded events

Repeated action is usually imperfective: **Каждое утро он покупал кофе.** But a series can contain individually completed perfectives when their results are counted or distributive: **Он несколько раз перечитал письмо.** — He reread the letter several times.

### 28.3 Aspect after phase and modal verbs

After **начать, продолжать, кончить/закончить**, the infinitive is normally imperfective because it names an activity: **начал читать, продолжал работать**.

After **хотеть, мочь, надо**, aspect depends on meaning:

- **Я хочу читать по-русски.** — I want to be able to/read in Russian generally.
- **Я хочу прочитать эту книгу.** — I want to finish/read this book.

### 28.4 Imperatives and aspect

- IPFV positive invites or starts an activity, or sounds hospitable: **Заходите! Садитесь! Рассказывай.**
- PFV positive asks for one result: **Откройте окно. Позвоните завтра.**
- IPFV negative is a neutral prohibition: **Не открывайте окно.**
- PFV negative warns against an unwanted result: **Не открой случайно не тот файл.** — Don't accidentally open the wrong file.

## 29. Prefixed verbs of motion

With motion verbs, prefixes add path and often create perfective one-way verbs. The corresponding habitual/process form is usually built from the multidirectional stem.

| Prefix | Path idea | Perfective example | Imperfective partner |
|---|---|---|---|
| по- | set off / limited trip | **пойти, поехать** | — (unprefixed IPFV supplies process) |
| при- | arrive | **прийти, приехать** | **приходить, приезжать** |
| у- | leave | **уйти, уехать** | **уходить, уезжать** |
| в-/во- | enter | **войти, въехать** | **входить, въезжать** |
| вы- | exit | **выйти, выехать** | **выходить, выезжать** |
| под- | approach | **подойти, подъехать** | **подходить, подъезжать** |
| от- | move away | **отойти, отъехать** | **отходить, отъезжать** |
| до- | reach | **дойти, доехать** | **доходить, доезжать** |
| пере- | cross/transfer | **перейти, переехать** | **переходить, переезжать** |
| про- | pass through/by | **пройти, проехать** | **проходить, проезжать** |
| об-/обо- | go around | **обойти, объехать** | **обходить, объезжать** |
| за- | stop by / go behind | **зайти, заехать** | **заходить, заезжать** |

- **Мы подошли к станции, вошли в здание и поднялись на второй этаж.** — We approached the station, entered the building and went up to the second floor.
- **Я часто захожу к бабушке после работы.** — I often drop in on my grandmother after work.
- **Поезд уже пришёл.** — The train has arrived.

Government is part of the construction: **войти в + A**, **выйти из + G**, **подойти к + D**, **дойти до + G**, **перейти через + A**.

## 30. Relative clauses and the pronoun который

**который** agrees in gender and number with its antecedent, but its case depends on its job inside the relative clause:

- **Это книга, которая лежит на столе.** — This is the book that is lying on the table. (subject, N)
- **Это книга, которую я читаю.** — This is the book that I am reading. (object, A)
- **Это студент, с которым я работаю.** — This is the student with whom I work. (after **с**, I)
- **Это город, в котором я родился.** — This is the city in which I was born. (after **в**, P)

The antecedent keeps the case required by the main clause: **Я говорю о книге, которую ты дал мне.** — I am talking about the book you gave me.

## 31. Purpose, wishes, commands and чтобы

**чтобы** combines with a verb morphologically shaped like the past. Its agreement matches the intended subject:

- **Я хочу, чтобы он пришёл, а она пришла.**
- **Преподаватель попросил, чтобы студенты открыли книги.** — The teacher asked the students to open their books.

For a same-subject purpose, use an infinitive or **чтобы + infinitive**:

- **Я пришёл (чтобы) поговорить с вами.** — I came to speak with you.

After verbs of command/request, Russian also uses an infinitive: **Он попросил меня подождать.** — He asked me to wait.

## 32. Conditional and hypothetical constructions

Use past-form verb + the particle **бы** (often attached to **если** as **если бы**). **бы** does not change form and may move for emphasis.

- **Я бы поехал, но у меня нет времени.** — I would go, but I have no time.
- **Если бы было тепло, мы бы пошли гулять.** — If it were warm, we would go for a walk.
- **На вашем месте я бы подождал.** — In your place, I would wait.

Russian does not use a special conditional tense. Present/future hypothetical meaning still takes past morphology.

## 33. Indefinite, negative and emphatic pronouns

The suffixes convey perspective:

- **-то:** a real but unidentified entity — **Кто-то стучит.**
- **-нибудь:** any non-specific choice — **Возьмите что-нибудь почитать.**
- **кое-:** the speaker knows but withholds/plays down details — **Я кое-что узнал.** — I found out a thing or two.
- **-либо:** formal version of **-нибудь** — **какие-либо изменения** any changes

With a preposition, it goes inside: **с кем-то, о чём-нибудь, кое с кем**.

Negative pronouns with unstressed **ни-** require sentential **не**: **Никому ничего не сказал.** Under a preposition: **ни с кем, ни о чём**. Stressed **не-** without a finite verb expresses absence of a possible participant/object:

- **Мне не с кем поговорить.** — I have nobody to talk to.
- **Нам нечего терять.** — We have nothing to lose.

## 34. Word order and information structure

Russian neutral order is subject–verb–object, but context determines what is “given” and “new.” New/focal material tends to come later.

- Question: **Кто купил билеты?** Answer: **Билеты купила Анна.** — Anna bought the tickets.
- Question: **Что купила Анна?** Answer: **Анна купила билеты.** — Anna bought the tickets.
- **Только Анна поняла вопрос.** — Only Anna understood the question.
- **Анна только поняла вопрос.** — Anna only understood the question (did not answer it, for example).

Adjectives normally precede nouns; possessors normally follow: **новая книга брата**. Unusual order can add contrast, evaluation or a literary tone.

## 35. More clause relationships

### 35.1 Time and sequence

- **Пока я готовил ужин, она работала.** — While I was cooking dinner, she was working.
- **После того как он пришёл, мы начали.** — After he arrived, we began.
- **Прежде чем отвечать, подумайте.** — Before answering, think.

### 35.2 Cause, result and concession

- **Так как было поздно, мы ушли.** — Since it was late, we left.
- **Было так холодно, что река замёрзла.** — It was so cold that the river froze.
- **Хотя шёл дождь, мы гуляли.** — Although it was raining, we walked.

### 35.3 Indirect questions

- **Я не знаю, где он живёт.** — I do not know where he lives.
- **Скажите, пожалуйста, когда начинается фильм.** — Please tell me when the film begins.
- **Я спросил, придёт ли она.** — I asked whether she would come. **ли** normally follows the focused word.

## 36. Numerals in oblique cases

Outside nominative/accusative counting phrases, both the numeral and noun normally take the required case:

- **с двумя новыми студентами** — with two new students (I)
- **к трём часам** — by/toward three o'clock (D)
- **о пяти книгах** — about five books (P)
- **нет двадцати рублей** — there are not twenty roubles (G)

**один** declines like an adjective. **два/две, три, четыре** have distinct oblique forms; 5–20 and 30 decline like third-declension nouns. Full tables appear in the appendices.

## 37. Short-form adjectives: first use

Short adjectives are normally predicative and describe a state, judgement or sufficiency:

- **Дом большой.** — The house is large. (neutral property; long form)
- **Дом велик.** — The house is large/grand. (more categorical, formal or expressive)
- **Я готов / готова.** — I am ready.
- **Мы согласны.** — We agree.
- **Эта задача трудна для меня.** — This task is difficult for me.

Some meanings strongly prefer short forms: **должен** must/owe, **рад** glad, **готов** ready, **согласен** in agreement, **нужен** needed. Agreement remains: **Мне нужна помощь. Нам нужны билеты.**

## 38. Passive meaning at B1

Russian often avoids the passive by using an active sentence, a **-ся** construction or a short passive participle:

- active: **Рабочие строят дом.** — Workers are building the house.
- process/passive-like: **Дом строится.** — The house is being built.
- result/state: **Дом построен.** — The house has been built / is built.

The agent, if expressed, takes instrumental: **Дом построен известным архитектором.**

## B1 pitfalls

- Do not choose aspect from tense alone; decide whether the sentence presents activity/fact or boundary/result.
- In a relative clause, determine **который**’s case from the clause that contains it.
- Keep every negative pronoun with its required **не**: **никто не...**
- A prefixed motion verb includes a path; learn its paired prepositions and cases.
- Flexible word order is not random. Put old/contextual material earlier and focal/new material later unless deliberately marking style.

## B1 can-do check

1. **Когда я вышел из дома, я понял, что забыл телефон, и вернулся.** — When I left the house, I realised that I had forgotten my phone and went back.
2. **Если бы у меня было больше времени, я бы чаще ходил в театр.** — If I had more time, I would go to the theatre more often.
3. **Это коллега, с которым я познакомился на конференции.** — This is the colleague whom I met at the conference.
4. **Я уже смотрел этот фильм, но так и не понял, почему герой уехал.** — I have already seen this film, but I still never understood why the hero left.

# Part V. B2 — grammatical compression and nuance

## B2 outcomes

At B2, you should understand and produce participles and gerunds, manage complex case government, use aspect in negation and repeated events, decline numerals in formal contexts, and recognise how particles and impersonal/passive constructions shape stance.

## 39. Participles: adjective-like verb forms

Participles express a verb-based property and agree like adjectives with the noun they modify. Russian has four principal types, although not every verb forms every type naturally.

| Type | Typical formation | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|---|
| Present active | IPFV 3PL stem + **-ущ-/-ющ-** or **-ащ-/-ящ-** | one who is doing | **читающий студент** — the student who is reading |
| Past active | past/infinitive stem + **-вш-**; consonant stem + **-ш-** | one who did/has done | **прочитавший статью студент** — the student who read the article |
| Present passive | IPFV 1PL stem + **-ем-/-им-** | being done | **обсуждаемый вопрос** — the issue being discussed |
| Past passive | usually PFV stem + **-нн-/-енн-/-т-** | done/completed | **написанное письмо** — the letter that was written |

Participial phrases usually follow the noun in neutral formal prose and are set off by commas when postposed and expanded:

- **Студенты, изучающие русский язык, сдали экзамен.** — The students studying Russian passed the exam.
- **Написанное вчера письмо уже отправлено.** — The letter written yesterday has already been sent.

### 39.1 Tense is relative

The participle’s tense is relative to the main verb:

- **Я встретил человека, живущего в Казани.** — I met a person who was/is living in Kazan.
- **Мы обсуждали данные, полученные ранее.** — We discussed data obtained earlier.

### 39.2 Case inside a participial phrase

The participle agrees with its head noun, while its own objects keep the case governed by the base verb:

- **с человеком, хорошо знающим город** — with a person who knows the city well (**знающим** I to agree with **человеком**; **город** A as object of **знать**)

### 39.3 Participles versus relative clauses

Participles are dense and frequent in written, academic and official Russian. Relative clauses are often clearer and more natural in conversation:

- formal: **документы, представленные комиссией**
- neutral spoken: **документы, которые представила комиссия**
- both: the documents that the commission submitted

Avoid creating an awkward participle when the verb is reflexive, highly idiomatic or has no established form. Usage matters more than theoretical possibility.

## 40. Short passive participles and result states

Short passive participles agree with the subject: M zero, F **-а**, N **-о**, PL **-ы/-и**.

- **Документ подписан. Дверь закрыта. Письмо отправлено. Работы закончены.**
- **Документ был подписан директором.** — The document was signed by the director.
- **Документ будет подписан завтра.** — The document will be signed tomorrow.

Distinguish a result/state from a lexical adjective when relevant:

- **Дверь закрыта охранником.** — The door was/has been closed by the guard. (event/result)
- **Магазин закрыт по воскресеньям.** — The shop is closed on Sundays. (regular state; adjectival reading)

Only participles normally take a direct agent in instrumental. Spelling of **н/нн** depends partly on whether a form is a full participle, short participle or adjective; see the spelling appendix.

## 41. Gerunds (деепричастия)

A gerund adds a secondary action whose understood subject must be the same as the main clause’s subject.

### 41.1 Imperfective gerund

Usually formed from the present stem with **-а/-я**; it presents a simultaneous or background action:

- **Читая статью, я делал заметки.** — While reading the article, I made notes.
- **Она шла, улыбаясь.** — She walked, smiling.

### 41.2 Perfective gerund

Usually formed with **-в/-вши/-ши**; it presents a prior or bounded secondary action:

- **Прочитав статью, я написал ответ.** — Having read the article, I wrote a reply.
- **Вернувшись домой, он сразу уснул.** — Having returned home, he fell asleep immediately.

### 41.3 The same-subject rule

Correct: **Подъезжая к станции, я увидел реку.** — As I approached the station, I saw the river.

Incorrect: `*Подъезжая к станции, у меня слетела шляпа.` This literally makes the hat the one approaching. Rewrite: **Когда я подъезжал к станции, у меня слетела шляпа.**

Impersonal sentences allow a gerund only when the implied actor is the same and is recoverable, commonly with an infinitive: **Готовя доклад, нужно проверять источники.** — When preparing a report, one must check sources. Even then, a full clause may be clearer.

Gerunds are much more common in writing than spontaneous conversation.

## 42. Advanced aspect at B2

### 42.1 Negation and failed results

Imperfective negation often denies that an action occurred or rejects the activity; perfective negation says a result was not reached:

- **Я не читал эту книгу.** — I have not read / did not engage with this book.
- **Я не прочитал книгу.** — I did not finish the book.
- **Он не решал задачу.** — He did not work on the problem.
- **Он не решил задачу.** — He failed to solve the problem.

### 42.2 Annulled result

When an action occurred but its result has been reversed, Russian often uses the imperfective general-factual question/statement:

- **Ты открывал окно?** — Did you open the window? (It is closed now; asking about the occurrence.)
- **Я давал тебе ключ.** — I did give/once gave you the key. (Current possession may differ.)

### 42.3 Limited duration and inception

Prefixes can add Aktionsart, a lexical mode of action rather than simply form a neutral aspect pair:

- **поработать** — work for a while
- **запеть** — begin singing
- **закричать** — start shouting
- **проработать пять лет** — work for a total of five years
- **перечитать все книги** — read/re-read all the books exhaustively
- **написать много писем** — write a large quantity of letters

Do not assume every prefix only means “completion.” It adds its own boundary or lexical meaning.

### 42.4 Delimited repetition

- **Он обычно вставал рано.** — He normally got up early. (habit, IPFV)
- **Он несколько раз встал и снова сел.** — Several times he stood up and sat down again. (counted complete transitions)
- **В течение часа он то вставал, то садился.** — For an hour he kept standing up and sitting down. (alternating process)

## 43. Verb and adjective government

Fluency depends on learning which case follows a word. Common patterns include:

| Governor | Case/construction | Example |
|---|---|---|
| бояться, избегать, достигать | G | **избегать ошибки; достичь цели** |
| ждать | A or G | **ждать поезд; ждать поезда** (individual/defined vs anticipated/non-specific nuance) |
| помогать, мешать, следовать | D | **помочь коллеге; следовать совету** |
| благодарить | A + за A | **благодарить друга за помощь** |
| интересоваться, пользоваться, владеть | I | **пользоваться словарём** |
| зависеть | от + G | **зависеть от погоды** |
| влиять | на + A | **влиять на результат** |
| участвовать | в + P | **участвовать в проекте** |
| похожий | на + A | **похож на отца** |
| богатый/бедный | I | **богат ресурсами** |
| свойственный | D | **свойственно этому жанру** |

Government may shift with meaning: **верить другу** trust a friend; **верить в успех** believe in success; **говорить о проекте** talk about a project; **говорить правду** tell the truth.

## 44. Preposition nuances

### 44.1 Cause

- **из-за + G** usually undesirable or neutral external cause: **из-за дождя** because of the rain
- **благодаря + D** favourable/enabling cause (though irony is possible): **благодаря помощи** thanks to the help
- **от + G** physical/emotional cause: **дрожать от холода; плакать от радости**
- **по + D** formal cause/category: **по ошибке, по привычке** — by mistake, out of habit

### 44.2 Despite and according to

- **несмотря на + A** — despite: **несмотря на трудности**
- **вопреки + D** — contrary to: **вопреки совету**
- **согласно + D** — according to: **согласно договору**, not `*согласно договора`

### 44.3 Time

- **через + A** after an interval: **через неделю** in a week
- **за + A** time taken to complete: **сделать за час** do it in an hour
- **на + A** planned duration: **уехать на неделю** leave for a week
- bare A duration: **работать весь день** work all day
- **в течение + G** throughout/within: **в течение недели**

### 44.4 “About” and approximation

**около + G, примерно, приблизительно** give neutral approximation. **с + A** can express approximate size in conversational comparisons: **ребёнок ростом с меня** — a child about my height.

## 45. Numerals and quantified agreement

### 45.1 Verb agreement

A quantified subject may take singular neuter to present the group as an amount/event, or plural to foreground individuals:

- **Пришло двадцать человек.** — Twenty people arrived. (count as a whole; neutral reporting)
- **Двадцать человек пришли раньше.** — Twenty people came earlier. (individual participants foregrounded)

With **большинство, ряд, часть**, singular is formal/collective; plural is possible when separate actions or animate members are salient.

### 45.2 Collective numerals

**двое, трое, четверо...** are used especially with male persons, children/young ones, plural-only nouns and paired objects:

- **двое друзей, трое детей, двое суток, трое ножниц**

They are normally avoided with female-only human nouns in standard neutral usage: **две студентки**, not usually **двое студенток**. Oblique forms: **двоих, двоим, двоими; троих...**

### 45.3 Fractions and decimals

- **одна вторая, две трети, пять восьмых**
- **одна целая пять десятых** — 1.5 in careful speech
- decimal notation uses a comma: **3,14 — три целых четырнадцать сотых**

## 46. Particles and small words with large effects

Particles organise stance and emphasis:

- **же** insists, contrasts or links to shared context: **Я же говорил!** — I told you!
- **ведь** reminds the listener of a reason/fact: **Ведь уже поздно.** — After all, it is already late.
- **ли** marks an embedded yes/no question: **Не знаю, придёт ли он.**
- **бы** marks hypothetical/wish: **Если бы знать!** — If only one knew!
- **только** only; **даже** even; their position controls focus.
- **именно** precisely: **Именно это я имел в виду.** — That is exactly what I meant.
- **как раз** exactly/just: **Он пришёл как раз вовремя.**
- **уж/уже** can intensify beyond temporal “already”: **Уж он-то знает.** — He of all people knows.
- **-то** attached to a focused word can mark contrast: **Я-то согласен.** — As for me, I agree.

## 47. Impersonal, indefinite-personal and general-personal sentences

Russian can suppress an agent in several ways:

- impersonal state: **Темнеет. Мне не спится.** — It is getting dark. I cannot sleep.
- impersonal necessity: **Нужно закончить работу.** — The work must be finished.
- indefinite-personal 3PL: **Здесь говорят по-русски.** — Russian is spoken here / people speak Russian here.
- general-personal 2SG: **Без труда не вытащишь и рыбку из пруда.** — You cannot even pull a fish from a pond without effort.
- infinitival label/order: **Не входить. Быть по сему.** — Do not enter. So be it. (the latter is archaic/formulaic)

These are not merely missing subjects; they are normal Russian sentence types used to adjust agency and register.

## 48. Coordination, punctuation and paired conjunctions

Common paired structures:

- **не только..., но и...** — not only..., but also...
- **как..., так и...** — both... and...
- **либо..., либо...** — either... or...
- **ни..., ни...** — neither... nor...
- **то..., то...** — now... now... / alternately...

Keep parallel grammatical forms:

- **Он не только прочитал статью, но и написал ответ.** — He not only read the article but also wrote a reply.

A comma normally separates subordinate clauses and participial/gerundial phrases, but punctuation follows syntactic boundaries, not pauses alone.

## B2 pitfalls

- A gerund’s actor must equal the main clause subject.
- A participle agrees with its head noun, not with a nearby noun inside the phrase.
- Do not overuse participles in conversation; convert them to **который** clauses.
- Learn government as part of a lexical entry: **влиять на что**, **управлять чем**.
- In formal standard Russian, use **согласно чему**, **вопреки чему**, **благодаря чему** with dative.
- Place focus particles immediately before (or in some uses after) what they modify.

## B2 can-do check

1. **Получив данные, исследователи сравнили их с результатами, опубликованными годом ранее.** — Having received the data, the researchers compared them with results published a year earlier.
2. **Я не закончил отчёт, хотя работал над ним весь день.** — I did not finish the report, although I worked on it all day.
3. **Согласно новому правилу, документы, поданные после пятницы, рассматриваться не будут.** — According to the new rule, documents submitted after Friday will not be considered.
4. **Именно из-за того, что решение приняли поздно, проект пришлось перенести.** — It was precisely because the decision was made late that the project had to be postponed.

# Part VI. C1 — precision, formal syntax and register

## C1 outcomes

At C1, grammar becomes a system of alternatives. You should choose among synonymous case constructions, aspectual framings, passive/active forms, nominal and verbal styles, and particles according to genre, emphasis and the speaker’s stance.

## 49. Aspect as viewpoint and discourse structure

### 49.1 Foreground and background

Perfectives commonly advance a narrative; imperfectives set the scene, describe simultaneous processes or reopen an event internally:

- **Снег шёл, ветер усиливался. Вдруг дверь открылась, вошёл человек и положил на стол письмо.** — Snow was falling and the wind was growing stronger. Suddenly the door opened, a person entered and put a letter on the table.

Aspect guides narrative attention, not just completion.

### 49.2 Expected, attempted and achieved action

- **Я уговаривал его уйти.** — I tried/was persuading him to leave; outcome unstated.
- **Я уговорил его уйти.** — I succeeded in persuading him to leave.
- **Он поступал в университет.** — He applied/took entrance steps; admission not entailed.
- **Он поступил в университет.** — He was admitted/enrolled.

Many imperfectives pragmatically cancel the result implicature even where the action had a goal.

### 49.3 Conative and resultative readings

The same lexical pair may map to English in different ways:

- **Я решал задачу, но не решил её.** — I tried/worked on the problem but did not solve it.
- **Он сдавал экзамен, но не сдал.** — He took/attempted the exam but did not pass.

### 49.4 Historic present and performatives

The present can make past events vivid: **И тут он входит и говорит...** — And then he comes in and says...

Some imperfective presents perform the action they name in formal speech: **Объявляю заседание открытым. Благодарю вас. Прошу учесть...** — I declare the meeting open. I thank you. I ask you to take into account...

## 50. Prefixes, suffixes and lexical aspect

Prefixes contribute recurring spatial/abstract meanings but do not guarantee predictable translation.

| Prefix | Frequent abstract extensions | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| в-/вс- | insertion, upward onset | **включить** switch on; **вскрикнуть** cry out |
| вы- | extraction, exhaustive completion | **вытащить** pull out; **выучить** learn thoroughly |
| до- | reach endpoint, supplementary action | **дописать** finish writing; **доработать** refine/finish |
| за- | onset, covering, diversion, excessive involvement | **засмеяться, закрасить, зайти, заработаться** |
| на- | accumulation/sufficient quantity | **написать много; наесться** eat one’s fill |
| от- | separation, completion/cessation | **отделить; отработать смену** |
| пере- | crossing, repetition, excess | **перейти; переписать; пересолить** |
| под- | approach, slight/additional action | **подойти; подправить** touch up |
| про- | passage, duration, omission/loss | **пройти; проработать; пропустить** |
| раз-/рас- | dispersal, development, intensive onset | **раздать; разогреться; рассмеяться** |
| с- | together/down/one complete trip | **собрать; спуститься; сходить** |

Secondary imperfectives in **-ывать/-ивать/-вать** restore an internal/repeated view after prefixation: **переписать → переписывать; решить → решать; открыть → открывать**. Avoid treating all morphology as mechanically productive; check actual usage and stress.

## 51. Fine-grained case meanings

### 51.1 Genitive under negation

The direct object under negation may be accusative or genitive. Accusative is common for definite, concrete or contrastive objects; genitive strengthens absence, non-specificity or formal/literary tone:

- **Я не прочитал эту книгу.** — I did not finish this book. (definite A)
- **Я не читал этой книги.** — I have not read this book / any of it. (G, more bookish or absence-focused)
- **Он не сказал ни слова.** — He did not say a word. (fixed emphatic G)

Modern usage often prefers accusative, especially with animate or strongly definite objects. Do not apply a blanket “negative verb takes genitive” rule.

### 51.2 Partitive genitive and second genitive

Mass nouns may use a genitive/partitive form for an indefinite amount:

- **выпить чаю, добавить сахару, купить сыру** — have some tea, add some sugar, buy some cheese

The ordinary genitive (**чая, сахара, сыра**) is always broadly available and often neutral. **-у/-ю** partitives are lexical, colloquial or stylistically marked.

### 51.3 Second prepositional (locative)

A limited group of masculine nouns use stressed **-у/-ю** for concrete location after **в/на**:

- **в лесу́, в снегу́, на берегу́, в аэропорту́**
- but topic/ordinary P: **о ле́се, о бе́реге, об аэропо́рте**

Some nouns allow variants with meaning or style differences: **в це́хе / в цеху́**.

### 51.4 Instrumental predicative versus nominative

After **быть**, nominative can identify or characterise, especially in the present or as a stable identity; instrumental presents a role, status, phase or classification, especially in past/future:

- **Он был талантливый ребёнок.** — He was a talented child. (identity/description; conversational)
- **Он был талантливым ребёнком.** — He was a talented child. (role/classification; standard neutral)
- **Она стала директором.** — She became director.

After infinitival **быть**, instrumental is standard: **Хочу быть врачом.**

### 51.5 Distributive по

- **по одному билету** — one ticket each
- **по два билета** — two tickets each (in modern neutral Russian, **по + A** for two/four patterns)
- **по пяти билетов / по пять билетов** — five tickets each; variation exists, with accusative increasingly neutral

In careful formal writing, check house style because prescriptive and contemporary patterns sometimes differ.

## 52. Long and short adjectives; predicative nuance

Long predicative adjectives often describe a characteristic or observed state; short forms are more categorical, temporary, evaluative, formal or complement-taking. The contrast is lexical and contextual:

- **Он спокойный.** — He is a calm person / he seems calm.
- **Он спокоен.** — He is calm (now / in judgement).
- **Платье короткое.** — The dress is short.
- **Платье коротко для неё.** is non-standard for ordinary agreement; say **Платье коротко́ ей?** only in restricted predicative/stylistic uses, more naturally **Платье ей коротко.** — The dress is too short for her.
- **Этот стол слишком мал для шести человек.** — This table is too small for six people.

Short forms readily take complements: **богат чем, готов к чему/сделать что, достоин чего, верен кому/чему**.

Not all qualitative adjectives have a normal short form; relational adjectives generally do not: **деревянный** wooden, **московский** Moscow-related.

## 53. Comparative syntax and standards of comparison

- simple comparative + G: **Она опытнее коллеги.**
- simple comparative + **чем** with a parallel form: **Она опытнее, чем её коллега.**
- **чем** before a clause: **Результат оказался лучше, чем мы ожидали.**
- **тем..., чем... / чем..., тем...**: **Чем раньше начнём, тем быстрее закончим.** — The earlier we start, the sooner we will finish.
- intensifiers: **гораздо/намного лучше, чуть выше, всё труднее**

Avoid the non-standard blend `*более лучше`; use **лучше** or **более хороший** (the latter is usually clumsier).

## 54. Complex subordination and correlatives

Formal and analytical Russian uses multiword conjunctions:

- **в связи с тем что** — because/in connection with the fact that
- **вследствие того что** — owing to the fact that
- **несмотря на то что** — despite the fact that
- **в то время как** — whereas/while
- **для того чтобы** — in order that/to
- **при условии что** — provided that
- **по мере того как** — as/proportionally as

Comma placement can depend on meaning, emphasis and the presence of particles:

- **Он ушёл, потому что устал.** — neutral cause
- **Он ушёл потому, что устал, а не потому, что обиделся.** — the causal basis itself is contrasted; the conjunction is split

Correlative pronouns organise dense sentences:

- **Тот, кто знает контекст, поймёт намёк.** — Whoever knows the context will understand the hint.
- **То, что казалось случайностью, оказалось закономерностью.** — What seemed accidental turned out to be a pattern.
- **Насколько я понимаю, решение ещё не принято.** — As far as I understand, no decision has been made yet.

## 55. Participial style at C1

Use participles to control information density, not merely to sound advanced.

- clause, more explicit: **Исследователи проанализировали данные, которые были собраны в трёх регионах.**
- participle, more compact: **Исследователи проанализировали данные, собранные в трёх регионах.**
- nominal style, most abstract: **Был проведён анализ данных, собранных в трёх регионах.**

The active version is usually clearer about agency. Russian official writing often overuses passive and nominal structures; good C1 style knows when to simplify them.

Preposed expanded participles usually lack commas when restrictive/integrated, but may take them with added causal/concessive meaning:

- **Опубликованные вчера данные вызвали спор.** — The data published yesterday caused debate.
- **Уставшие после долгой дороги, туристы сразу легли спать.** — Tired after the long journey, the tourists went straight to bed. (causal circumstance)

## 56. Nominalisation and official style

Formal Russian often replaces verbs with verbal nouns and light verbs:

- verbal/direct: **Комиссия проверила документы.**
- nominal/formal: **Комиссия провела проверку документов.** — The commission conducted a review of the documents.
- passive/impersonal: **Была проведена проверка документов.** — A review of the documents was carried out.

Common light-verb patterns: **провести анализ**, **принять решение**, **оказать помощь**, **осуществить контроль**, **внести предложение**.

Use them when the genre requires institutional focus, classification or a stable technical term. In ordinary prose, the direct verb is clearer: **помочь** rather than **оказать помощь**, **проверить** rather than **осуществить проверку**.

## 57. Stance, evidentiality and modal particles

Russian has no grammatical evidential tense, but stance is richly expressed lexically and syntactically:

- **говорят / как говорят** — they say / as they say
- **якобы** — allegedly/supposedly, often distancing or sceptical
- **будто / словно** — as if
- **оказывается** — it turns/turned out; speaker discovers
- **по-видимому, вероятно, должно быть** — apparently, probably, presumably
- **вряд ли** — hardly/unlikely
- **неужели** — really? can it be?
- **разве** — surely/not? challenges an assumption

Examples:

- **Он якобы ничего не знал.** — He allegedly knew nothing.
- **Неужели вы не получили письмо?** — Did you really not receive the letter?
- **Разве это не очевидно?** — Isn't this obvious?
- **Он, должно быть, уже уехал.** — He must have left already.

## 58. Dash, colon and asyndetic complex sentences

Russian punctuation can encode logical relations without a conjunction:

- colon for explanation/cause/content: **Я понял одно: спор продолжать бессмысленно.** — I understood one thing: continuing the argument was pointless.
- dash for result, contrast or rapid consequence: **Ещё шаг — и лёд треснет.** — One more step and the ice will crack.
- semicolon separates substantial parallel clauses: **Слева темнел лес; справа тянулись поля.**
- comma between closely linked simultaneous/sequential clauses is possible, but formal writing may prefer an explicit conjunction or stronger mark.

Punctuation is partly semantic. Compare **Я знал: он придёт** (the second clause explains what I knew) and **Я знал — он придёт** (confident inference/intonational assertion).

## 59. Reference, ellipsis and cohesion

Avoid unnecessary pronoun repetition, but keep reference clear:

- **Анна вошла, сняла пальто и села.** — Anna came in, took off her coat and sat down.
- Ambiguous: **Когда Анна встретила Ольгу, она улыбнулась.** Who smiled?
- Clear: **Увидев Ольгу, Анна улыбнулась.** or **Ольга улыбнулась, когда Анна подошла.**

Demonstratives and resumptive words structure argument:

- **это** refers to a proposition: **Он отказался. Это нас удивило.**
- **такой** classifies by type/degree: **Такое решение рискованно.**
- **данный** means “given/the present” in official or technical style; it is often pompous in everyday prose.

## C1 pitfalls

- Do not overgeneralise genitive under negation; definiteness and style matter.
- Prefix meaning and aspect interact with lexical meaning. Verify actual pairs.
- Use nominalisations for a reason, not as decoration.
- A short adjective is not merely a shorter long adjective; it may change predicate type or register.
- In dense sentences, ensure every participle, pronoun and gerund has an unmistakable controller.
- Treat punctuation as syntax and meaning, not just breathing.

## C1 can-do check

1. **Хотя переговоры продолжались несколько месяцев, сторонам так и не удалось достичь соглашения.** — Although the negotiations continued for several months, the parties never managed to reach an agreement.
2. **Представленные комиссией данные, по-видимому, не учитывают факторов, влияющих на спрос.** — The data presented by the commission apparently do not account for factors affecting demand.
3. **Чем дольше откладывается решение, тем выше риск того, что проект потеряет финансирование.** — The longer the decision is delayed, the greater the risk that the project will lose funding.
4. **Он поступал в университет дважды и лишь на третий раз поступил.** — He applied to the university twice and was admitted only on the third attempt.

# Part VII. C2 — idiomatic, stylistic and editorial control

## C2 outcomes

C2 does not mean memorising a final list of endings. It means selecting, bending and occasionally withholding expected structures with native-like awareness of genre, implication and rhythm. You should recognise marked grammar, edit competing variants, and explain why two grammatical sentences do not mean quite the same thing.

## 60. Marked word order, inversion and focus

Russian word order combines syntax, information structure, rhythm and genre. A C2 reader distinguishes neutral placement from deliberate marking.

### 60.1 Theme and rheme

- neutral answer to “What happened?”: **Директор подписал договор.** — The director signed the contract.
- object as theme, agent as new contrast: **Договор подписал директор.** — It was the director who signed the contract.
- event/result as theme, object focus: **Директор подписал договор, а не заявление.** — The director signed the contract, not the application.

### 60.2 Separated noun phrases

Adjective/postmodifier separation can create afterthought, evaluation or spoken immediacy:

- **Он принёс книгу — старую, потрёпанную, всю в пометках.** — He brought a book—old, battered, covered in notes.

### 60.3 Fronting and inversion

- **Странное это было решение.** — It was a strange decision. (evaluative, reflective)
- **Хорошо же вы подготовились!** — Well, you certainly prepared! (can be sincere or ironic)
- **Никогда ещё не видел он такого города.** — Never before had he seen such a city. (literary inversion)

Marked order is powerful precisely because a neutral order exists. Reproduce it only when the intended focus/register warrants it.

## 61. Ellipsis, incompleteness and parcellation

Spoken and literary Russian routinely omits recoverable material:

- **Ты куда? — Домой.** — Where are you going? — Home.
- **Я — за, он — против.** — I am for it; he is against.
- **Хотел позвонить, да забыл.** — I wanted to call, but forgot.

Parcellation breaks one syntactic whole into separate sentences for emphasis:

- neutral: **Он согласился сразу, без колебаний.**
- parcellated: **Он согласился. Сразу. Без колебаний.** — He agreed. Immediately. Without hesitation.

This is normal in fiction, journalism and advertising, but can look theatrical or careless in formal analytical prose.

## 62. Register transformations

A C2 user can express the same proposition in different grammatical registers.

### 62.1 Colloquial

**Они всё-таки решили отложить запуск, потому что не успевали всё проверить.** — They decided after all to postpone the launch because they did not have time to check everything.

Features: active agents, common verbs, particles, relatively direct clause linkage.

### 62.2 Neutral written

**Поскольку завершить проверку в установленный срок не удалось, было решено отложить запуск.** — Since it proved impossible to complete the review by the deadline, it was decided to postpone the launch.

Features: causal conjunction, impersonal construction, controlled information order.

### 62.3 Official-administrative

**В связи с невозможностью завершения проверки в установленный срок принято решение о переносе даты запуска.** — Owing to the impossibility of completing the review within the established period, a decision has been adopted to move the launch date.

Features: derived preposition, chains of genitives, nominalisation, institutional suppression of agent.

The official version is not “better”; it suits a record or decision notice. In explanatory prose, it may conceal responsibility and burden the reader.

## 63. Colloquial grammar and discourse particles

### 63.1 Truncated and analytic tendencies

Conversation tolerates structures avoided in edited formal prose:

- **Я к тому, что...** — My point is that...
- **Он такой: «Не пойду».** — He was like, “I’m not going.”
- **В смысле?** — What do you mean?
- **Есть кто дома?** — Is anyone home?
- **Мне кофе, пожалуйста.** — A coffee for me, please.

### 63.2 Particle clusters

Particles combine pragmatically:

- **ну вот** — there we are / and so
- **да нет** — no, actually (not “yes no”)
- **вот именно** — exactly
- **ну уж** — emphatic disbelief/concession
- **так ведь** — but, as you know / the thing is
- **что ли** — or something? tentative identification: **Он обиделся, что ли?**
- **как бы** — as if; colloquially a hedge/filler, often stigmatised when overused

Meaning depends on intonation and context. Dictionaries provide cores, not full conversational force.

## 64. Concession, counterexpectation and “however” systems

Russian distributes concessive meaning across conjunctions, particles and pronouns:

- **хотя** although: **Хотя было поздно, он продолжал работать.**
- **несмотря на то что** despite the fact that: more explicit/formal
- **пусть/пускай** even if/let: **Пусть он и ошибся, намерения у него были добрые.** — Even if he made a mistake, his intentions were good.
- **как ни** however: **Как ни старался, он не успел.** — However hard he tried, he did not make it.
- **что бы ни / кто бы ни / где бы ни** whatever/whoever/wherever: **Что бы ни случилось, звони.**
- **зато** but on the positive compensating side: **Дорого, зато надёжно.** — Expensive, but reliable.
- **всё же / всё-таки** nevertheless / after all

Note the spelling and grammar contrast:

- **чтобы** conjunction: **Я пришёл, чтобы поговорить.**
- **что бы** pronoun + particle: **Что бы вы выбрали?** — What would you choose?
- **тоже/также** also versus **то же/так же** the same/in the same way.

## 65. Idiomatic aspect and competing viewpoints

At C2, both aspects can be grammatical, with different assumptions:

- **Вы читали договор?** — Have you read/looked at the contract? (experience/fact; tactful inquiry)
- **Вы прочитали договор?** — Have you finished reading the contract? (expected result)
- **Я буду просить его помочь.** — I will ask/keep asking him; process or repeated attempt.
- **Я попрошу его помочь.** — I will make one request (often also a softened near-future intention).
- **Не буду спорить.** — I will not argue / I decline the activity.
- **Не стану спорить.** — I will not proceed to argue; deliberate refusal.

Perfective present forms can soften a suggestion or make an interaction less durative:

- **Я вам посоветую другой вариант.** — I would advise / let me recommend another option.
- **Не скажете, который час?** — Could you tell me the time? (negated perfective politeness formula)

## 66. Lexicalised grammar and collocation

Advanced correctness includes selecting the expected grammatical frame:

- **обратить внимание на + A**, but **уделить внимание + D**
- **преимущество перед + I**, but **превосходство над + I**
- **сомневаться в + P**, but **подвергать сомнению + A**
- **отзыв о + P**, but **рецензия на + A**
- **характерный для + G**, but **свойственный + D**
- **контроль над + I / контроль за + I**, while technical/institutional usage may prefer one by domain

Collocations can resist plausible synonyms: standard **одержать победу**, **потерпеть поражение**, **принять меры**, **играть роль**, **иметь значение**. Grammatical mastery includes these predicate–noun packages.

## 67. Difficult noun categories and agreement

### 67.1 Indeclinable nouns

Nouns such as **метро, пальто, такси, жюри, интервью** do not change. Gender is lexical and semantic:

- usually neuter objects: **новое пальто, московское метро**
- persons follow natural sex where relevant: **опытная атташе** (female attaché), **известный маэстро** (male maestro)
- many animal names default masculine unless female sex is foregrounded: **быстрый кенгуру**

### 67.2 Common-gender nouns

Words in **-а/-я** can agree according to the person: **Он ужасный неряха. Она ужасная неряха.** Examples include **умница, сирота, коллега** (the last is often treated by natural gender with ordinary adjectival agreement).

### 67.3 Abbreviations

Gender often follows the head word in the expanded phrase: **ООН приняла** (организация, F), **МГУ объявил** (университет, M). But lexicalised or phonetic usage can compete; edited style should be consistent.

### 67.4 Plural-only and singular-only nouns

- plural-only: **ножницы, сутки, деньги, каникулы**
- primarily singular/mass/abstract: **молодёжь, молоко, смелость**

Counting plural-only nouns often needs collective numerals (**двое суток**) or classifier phrases (**две пары ножниц**).

### 67.5 Animacy edge cases

Animacy may depend on meaning:

- **вижу спутник** for an inanimate satellite (A=N), but **вижу спутника** for a companion (A=G).
- **вызвать персонажей** — summon/select characters; fictional persons normally animate.
- food names derived from animals vary by whether the creature or dish is conceptualised: usage must be learned.

## 68. Difficult numeral constructions

### 68.1 Compound oblique numerals

In formal standard Russian, every component declines:

- N: **две тысячи пятьсот сорок три рубля**
- G: **двух тысяч пятисот сорока трёх рублей**
- I: **двумя тысячами пятьюстами сорока тремя рублями**

Spontaneous speech often simplifies large numerals, but formal presentation, legal text and broadcasting expect full control.

### 68.2 Year expressions

- **в две тысячи двадцать шестом году** — in 2026
- **с тысяча девятьсот девяносто первого года** is common? In careful standard grammar, decline the relevant ordinal ending in a compound year: **с тысяча девятьсот девяносто первого года**; the cardinal components preceding the ordinal remain in their citation-like form.
- **к две тысячи тридцатому году** — by 2030

### 68.3 Agreement around 21, 31, etc.

Compounds ending in **один** take singular: **двадцать один участник пришёл**. Compounds ending in **два–четыре** take the paucal noun form but plural adjective is standard in many animate phrases: **двадцать два новых участника**.

### 68.4 Half- forms

- **полчаса, полдня, пол-лимона, пол-Москвы**: spelling varies by following capital, vowel or **л**.
- **полтора часа, полторы недели**; oblique **полутора часов/недель**.

## 69. Productive word formation and grammatical category shifts

Russian derives aspect, agency, quality and abstraction through morphology:

- agent nouns: **читать → читатель; переводить → переводчик**
- action/result nouns: **решить → решение; развивать → развитие**
- abstract qualities: **точный → точность**
- relational adjectives: **наука → научный; город → городской**
- diminutives and expressive nouns: **дом → домик; книга → книжка; рука → ручка**

Diminutives are not merely about size. They can signal affection, informality, politeness, irony or mitigation:

- **Подождите минуточку.** — Wait just a moment. (mitigation)
- **Ваш документик.** can sound friendly, belittling or bureaucratically patronising depending on context.

Word formation changes government or register. Learn derivatives as lexical items, not guaranteed formulas.

## 70. Authorial punctuation and editorial judgement

At C2, distinguish rule-governed punctuation from optional authorial marking.

### 70.1 Dash between subject and predicate

The dash is common with nominal/infinitival predicates: **Цель проекта — снизить расходы.** It may be omitted with a pronoun, particle, conversational flow or when the predicate precedes: **Москва столица России** is possible in simple/educational text, but a dash is standard in careful isolated formulation; **Мой брат врач** normally has no required dash.

### 70.2 Parenthetical words

True parentheticals are comma-set: **конечно, вероятно, по-моему, во-первых**. Some lookalikes are sentence members and are not automatically parenthetical: **будто, как будто, именно, даже, примерно, якобы**.

Compare:

- **Он, верно, уже ушёл.** — He has probably left. (**верно** parenthetical = probably)
- **Он верно решил задачу.** — He solved the problem correctly. (adverb)

### 70.3 Optionality changes meaning

- **Он может быть дома.** — He may be at home. (**может быть** predicate)
- **Он, может быть, дома.** — Perhaps he is at home. (parenthetical)

Use reference works and editorial house style for genuinely variable cases. “Authorial” does not mean arbitrary.

## 71. Historical, literary and socially marked forms

Recognise forms outside neutral contemporary conversation:

- remnant vocatives/interjections: **Боже! Господи!**; modern colloquial truncated vocatives **Мам! Пап! Саш!**
- elevated instrumental endings **-ою/-ею**: **рукою, землею**
- archaic/plural forms in quotations or high style: **сыны**, **очи**, **уста** beside neutral **сыновья, глаза, губы/рот**
- Church Slavonic/high-style participial and lexical elements can signal solemnity or parody.
- polite pre-revolutionary or stylised formulas may use unusual capitalization and address; they should not be copied into neutral modern business Russian.

Recognition is part of C2; productive use requires a clear genre reason.

## 72. Prescriptive pressure points and variant norms

Advanced speakers encounter variation between colloquial frequency and codified edited norms. High-value checks include:

- **по приезде, по окончании, по возвращении** = upon arrival/completion/return (P-like fixed ending), not colloquial `*по приезду` in careful standard usage when meaning “after.”
- **согласно приказу, вопреки ожиданиям, благодаря помощи** + D.
- **оплатить проезд** (A) but **заплатить за проезд**.
- **одеть ребёнка** dress a person; **надеть пальто** put on clothing.
- **представить доказательства** present; **предоставить возможность** provide.
- **их**, not non-standard **ихний**, in neutral standard Russian.
- **езжай(те)** is the standard imperative of **ехать**; colloquial forms vary, but `*едь` is stigmatised in careful speech.
- stress may be normative: **звони́т, включи́т, догово́р, догово́ры, катало́г**.

Norms change and dictionaries sometimes accept variants. C2 competence includes checking a current authoritative dictionary when the stakes are editorial, academic, legal or public.

## 73. Editing for clarity: a C2 grammar workflow

When revising advanced Russian, inspect in this order:

1. **Predication:** Who does what? Is agency deliberately present or suppressed?
2. **Government:** Does every verb, adjective, noun and preposition assign the correct case?
3. **Aspect:** Is the event viewed as process, fact, repetition, attempt or result?
4. **Reference:** Does each pronoun, participle and gerund have one clear controller?
5. **Information structure:** Does word order place context before focus, unless marked?
6. **Register:** Are nominalisations, passive forms and particles appropriate to the genre?
7. **Parallelism:** Do coordinated elements have matching grammatical shape?
8. **Punctuation:** Do marks express the intended syntax and semantic relation?
9. **Sound and rhythm:** In speeches or literary prose, do repeated endings and clause lengths help rather than obscure?
10. **Norm check:** Verify stress, variant endings and contested constructions in an up-to-date dictionary.

## C2 can-do check

1. Explain the difference between **Вы читали отчёт?** and **Вы прочитали отчёт?**, then choose the more tactful question for a colleague.
2. Convert **В связи с невозможностью осуществления проверки...** into clear neutral Russian without losing legal meaning.
3. Interpret the focus differences among **Только он решил задачу**, **Он только решил задачу** and **Он решил только задачу**.
4. Edit a dangling gerund, an ambiguous pronoun and an inappropriate genitive under negation.
5. Retell one event colloquially, in neutral analytical prose and as an official record.

## 83. Spelling and punctuation essentials

### 83.1 не and ни

- **не** negates: **не знаю**.
- **ни** strengthens negation or forms negative pronouns: **ни разу не видел; никто не пришёл**.
- **не один** = more than one/many; **ни один не** = not a single one.

### 83.2 не with nouns/adjectives/adverbs

Joined when forming a single concept or synonym without contrast: **неправда** falsehood, **небольшой** small. Separate with explicit opposition or intensifiers of negation: **не большой, а маленький; вовсе не простой**. Context can change the analysis.

### 83.3 -н- and -нн-

This is a lexical-morphological system with exceptions. Broadly, full past passive participles often have **-нн-** (**написанный**), short passive participles one **-н-** (**написан**), and adjectives follow their suffix patterns (**деревянный**, exception-rich). Dependents and prefixes can help distinguish participles from adjectives, but use a dictionary for uncertain forms.

### 83.4 Commas

Use commas for subordinate clauses, gerundial phrases, many postposed expanded participles, parenthetical words, direct address and coordinated clauses without a single shared element. Do not automatically place a comma before every **и**; two homogeneous predicates share no comma: **Он вошёл и сел.**

### 83.5 Direct speech

Russian typographic convention commonly uses «ёлочки»:

- **Он сказал: «Я вернусь».** — He said, “I will return.”
- **«Я вернусь», — сказал он.** — “I will return,” he said.

Publishing conventions vary in nested quotations and final punctuation.

## 85. How to study a grammar point

1. **Notice:** underline the ending and identify the governing word.
2. **Contrast:** build a minimal pair, such as **читал / прочитал** or **в школу / в школе**.
3. **Substitute:** replace one noun through all genders/numbers.
4. **Personalise:** write three true sentences about your life.
5. **Transform:** make the sentence negative, past, future, a question and a subordinate clause.
6. **Retrieve:** reproduce it after one day, one week and one month.
7. **Verify:** check stress, aspect and government in a reliable dictionary.

Example transformation chain:

- **Я читаю статью.** — I am reading an article.
- **Я не читаю статью.** — I am not reading the article.
- **Вчера я читал статью.** — Yesterday I was reading/read the article.
- **Я прочитал статью.** — I finished the article.
- **Когда я прочитал статью, я написал заметку.** — When I finished the article, I wrote a note.

## 87. Final perspective

Russian grammar is not a ladder of exceptions. Its central systems reinforce one another:

- **case** assigns relationships;
- **agreement** makes those relationships visible;
- **aspect** frames events;
- **word order and particles** organise attention;
- **clause structure** packages reasoning;
- **register** determines which correct option is appropriate.

At A1, accuracy means choosing a recognisable ending. At B1, it means building a coherent event. At C1, it means choosing among alternatives. At C2, it means controlling what the grammar implies—not only what the words literally state.
