## 74. Noun declension: singular endings

The table gives regular prototypes. Spelling rules and stress can alter the visible ending.

| Type | N | G | D | A | I | P |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| M hard **стол** | стол | стола | столу | стол | столом | столе |

| F **-а книга** | книга | книги | книге | книгу | книгой | книге |
| F **-я неделя** | неделя | недели | неделе | неделю | неделей | неделе |
| F **-ь дверь** | дверь | двери | двери | дверь | дверью | двери |

Special neuters in **-мя** insert **-ен-**: **имя — имени — именем; время — времени — временем**. **путь** is irregular masculine: **пути, путём, о пути**. **мать, дочь** add **-ер-** in oblique forms: **матери, дочерью**.

## 75. Noun declension: plural endings

| Case | Common endings | Examples |
|---|---|---|
| N | **-ы/-и, -а/-я** | столы, книги, окна, моря |
| G | **-ов/-ев/-ей** or zero; irregular | столов, музеев, дверей, книг, окон |
| D | **-ам/-ям** | столам, неделям |
| A | N if inanimate; G if animate | вижу столы / студентов |
| I | **-ами/-ями** | столами, морями |

High-frequency irregular plurals: **человек — люди — людей; ребёнок — дети — детей; друг — друзья — друзей; брат — братья — братьев; стул — стулья; дерево — деревья; имя — имена; время — времена; мать — матери**.

## 78. Verb endings and core irregulars

| Person | First conjugation | Second conjugation |
|---|---|---|
| я | -у/-ю | -у/-ю |
| ты | -ешь | -ишь |
| он/она | -ет | -ит |
| мы | -ем | -им |
| вы | -ете | -ите |
| они | -ут/-ют | -ат/-ят |

Most **-ить** verbs are second conjugation, with famous exceptions **брить, стелить** (first). Traditional second-conjugation exceptions not in **-ить** include **гнать, держать, дышать, слышать, смотреть, видеть, ненавидеть, обидеть, зависеть, терпеть, вертеть**. In practice, learn principal parts rather than relying only on lists.

## 79. Aspect selection quick map

| Communicative view | Usual aspect | Example |
|---|---|---|
| process now/then | IPFV | **Я писал письмо.** |
| repeated/habitual | IPFV | **Я часто писал.** |
| ability/activity/experience | IPFV | **Вы читаете по-русски?** |

| ordered foreground events | PFV | **Вошёл, сел, заговорил.** |

| future result | conjugated PFV | **Прочитаю.** |

## 80. Motion verbs quick map

| Meaning | One-direction IPFV | Multi/habitual IPFV | Common PFV departure |
|---|---|---|---|
| go on foot | идти | ходить | пойти |
| go by transport | ехать | ездить | поехать |
| run | бежать | бегать | побежать |
| fly | лететь | летать | полететь |
| swim/sail | плыть | плавать | поплыть |
| carry on foot | нести | носить | понести |
| transport | везти | возить | повезти |
| lead/drive | вести | водить | повести |

Key route pairs:

- **в + A ↔ из + G:** into/from inside
- **на + A ↔ с + G:** onto/to an activity ↔ off/from
- **к + D ↔ от + G:** toward a person/place ↔ away/from
- **до + G:** as far as
- **через + A:** across/through

## 83. Spelling and punctuation essentials

### 83.1 не and ни

- **не** negates: **не знаю**.
- **ни** strengthens negation or forms negative pronouns: **ни разу не видел; никто не пришёл**.
- **не один** = more than one/many; **ни один не** = not a single one.

### 83.2 не with nouns/adjectives/adverbs

Joined when forming a single concept or synonym without contrast: **неправда** falsehood, **небольшой** small. Separate with explicit opposition or intensifiers of negation: **не большой, а маленький; вовсе не простой**. Context can change the analysis.

### 83.3 -н- and -нн-

This is a lexical-morphological system with exceptions. Broadly, full past passive participles often have **-нн-** (**написанный**), short passive participles one **-н-** (**написан**), and adjectives follow their suffix patterns (**деревянный**, exception-rich). Dependents and prefixes can help distinguish participles from adjectives, but use a dictionary for uncertain forms.

### 83.4 Commas

Use commas for subordinate clauses, gerundial phrases, many postposed expanded participles, parenthetical words, direct address and coordinated clauses without a single shared element. Do not automatically place a comma before every **и**; two homogeneous predicates share no comma: **Он вошёл и сел.**

### 83.5 Direct speech

Russian typographic convention commonly uses «ёлочки»:

- **Он сказал: «Я вернусь».** — He said, “I will return.”
- **«Я вернусь», — сказал он.** — “I will return,” he said.

Publishing conventions vary in nested quotations and final punctuation.

# Part IX. A practical learning path

## 84. What to master at each level

### A1 checklist

- gender, plural, animacy and basic agreement
- present, past and compound future
- nominative, accusative and prepositional; basic G/D/I
- possession with **у**, likes with **нравиться**, modal predicatives
- basic questions, negation, numbers and time

### A2 checklist

- all cases in common singular/plural patterns
- common case-governing prepositions and verbs
- first systematic aspect choices
- unprefixed motion verbs
- reflexive verbs, imperatives, comparison
- everyday subordinate clauses and pronouns in **-то/-нибудь/ни-**

### B1 checklist

- aspect in narration, negation and interaction
- prefixed motion verbs and route government
- relative clauses with **который**
- conditionals and **чтобы** clauses
- oblique numerals, short adjectives and first passive patterns
- information-structure word order

### B2 checklist

- active/passive participles and short passive results
- imperfective/perfective gerunds with the same-subject rule
- advanced aspectual meaning and lexical modes of action
- verb/adjective government and nuanced prepositions
- collective/oblique numerals; particle focus
- impersonal, indefinite-personal and formal clause patterns

### C1 checklist

- aspect as discourse viewpoint
- prefix/suffix semantics and secondary imperfectivisation
- genitive under negation, partitive and locative variants
- long/short predicate nuances and formal comparison
- complex conjunctions, participial compression and nominalisation
- stance markers, asyndetic relations and semantic punctuation

### C2 checklist

- marked word order, ellipsis and parcellation
- colloquial, neutral, academic and official transformations
- clusters of particles and concessive systems
- idiomatic aspect and collocational government
- difficult gender, animacy, indeclinables and numerals
- variant norms, authorial punctuation and editorial verification

## 85. How to study a grammar point

1. **Notice:** underline the ending and identify the governing word.
2. **Contrast:** build a minimal pair, such as **читал / прочитал** or **в школу / в школе**.
3. **Substitute:** replace one noun through all genders/numbers.
4. **Personalise:** write three true sentences about your life.
5. **Transform:** make the sentence negative, past, future, a question and a subordinate clause.
6. **Retrieve:** reproduce it after one day, one week and one month.
7. **Verify:** check stress, aspect and government in a reliable dictionary.

Example transformation chain:

- **Я читаю статью.** — I am reading an article.
- **Я не читаю статью.** — I am not reading the article.
- **Вчера я читал статью.** — Yesterday I was reading/read the article.
- **Я прочитал статью.** — I finished the article.
- **Когда я прочитал статью, я написал заметку.** — When I finished the article, I wrote a note.
- **Статья, которую я прочитал, была полезной.** — The article I read was useful.
- **Прочитав статью, я написал заметку.** — Having read the article, I wrote a note.

## 86. Recommended grammar notebook entry

For a noun, record: **сло́варь (M, animate? no) — G слова́ря — PL словари́, G.PL словаре́й**, plus one governed phrase: **пользоваться словарём**.

For a verb, record: **реша́ть (IPFV) / реши́ть (PFV); реша́ю, реши́шь; решать/решить что (A)**, then one contrast: **Я решал задачу, но не решил её.**

For a preposition or predicate, record the question and case: **благодаря чему? + D: благодаря помощи**.

## 87. Final perspective

Russian grammar is not a ladder of exceptions. Its central systems reinforce one another:

- **case** assigns relationships;
- **agreement** makes those relationships visible;
- **aspect** frames events;
- **word order and particles** organise attention;
- **clause structure** packages reasoning;
- **register** determines which correct option is appropriate.

At A1, accuracy means choosing a recognisable ending. At B1, it means building a coherent event. At C1, it means choosing among alternatives. At C2, it means controlling what the grammar implies—not only what the words literally state.
