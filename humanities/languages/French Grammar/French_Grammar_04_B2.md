# B2: controlling complex clauses and viewpoint

> *At B2, grammar becomes a system of choices rather than isolated forms. You should justify why a clause is indicative, subjunctive or conditional; compress repeated information; and adjust emphasis without losing clarity.*

## 1. Indicative or subjunctive after opinion and certainty

> **Learning goal:** Choose mood according to whether the proposition is asserted, rejected, doubted or sought.

Affirmative certainty/opinion normally takes the indicative: *Je pense qu'il vient; il est certain qu'elle réussira*. Negated or interrogative opinion often takes the subjunctive when doubt is genuine: *Je ne pense pas qu'il vienne; pensez-vous qu'elle puisse réussir ?* The indicative is possible when the speaker still asserts the fact.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je crois qu'il a raison.
>
> *English: I think he is right.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je ne crois pas qu'il ait raison.
>
> *English: I do not think he is right.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Croyez-vous vraiment qu'il est coupable ?
>
> *English: Do you really believe he is guilty? (The question focuses on the belief while treating the proposition as asserted/likely.)*

> **Usage note:** Mood is not a mechanical synonym for certainty versus uncertainty. It reflects how the clause is presented: asserted as information (indicative) or placed under evaluation, will, doubt or non-identification (subjunctive).

## 2. Subjunctive in relative clauses and superlatives

> **Learning goal:** Use the subjunctive when the antecedent is desired, non-specific, denied or subjectively evaluated.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je cherche un assistant qui puisse travailler le soir.
>
> *English: I am looking for an assistant who can work evenings. (No identified assistant.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** J'ai un assistant qui peut travailler le soir.
>
> *English: I have an assistant who can work evenings. (Identified fact.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** C'est le meilleur film que j'aie vu cette année.
>
> *English: It is the best film I have seen this year.*

After *le seul, le premier, le dernier, le meilleur, le pire* and similar evaluations, the subjunctive is common but not automatic. The indicative is possible when the speaker foregrounds a factual identification rather than a subjective judgment.

> **Watch out:** Do not assume every negative antecedent requires the subjunctive. Compare *Il n'y a personne qui puisse m'aider* (non-existence) with *La personne qui peut m'aider n'est pas ici* (identified person's ability is asserted).

## 3. The past subjunctive

> **Learning goal:** Place the subordinate action before the main action while preserving a subjunctive trigger.

> **Pattern:** **avoir/être in the present subjunctive + past participle**: *que j'aie compris, qu'elle soit partie, que nous nous soyons trompés*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je suis ravi que vous ayez accepté.
>
> *English: I am delighted that you accepted/have accepted.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Bien qu'elle soit arrivée tard, elle a tout compris.
>
> *English: Although she arrived late, she understood everything.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il doute qu'ils se soient parlé.
>
> *English: He doubts that they spoke to each other.*

Agreement follows the same principles as other compound forms. The choice between present and past subjunctive is about relative time: simultaneous/later versus earlier/completed.

## 4. Advanced subjunctive conjunctions and alternatives

> **Learning goal:** Select mood and structure after concession, prevention, condition and temporal boundaries.

- Subjunctive: *à moins que, pourvu que, à condition que, de peur que, de crainte que, jusqu'à ce que, sans que, avant que, bien que, quoique*.
- Indicative: *après que, alors que, tandis que, parce que, puisque, dès que, pendant que*.
- Conditional meaning can also be expressed with *au cas où + conditional*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Attendez jusqu'à ce qu'elle revienne.
>
> *English: Wait until she comes back.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Après qu'elle est partie, nous avons fermé la porte.
>
> *English: After she left, we closed the door.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Prenez un parapluie au cas où il pleuvrait.
>
> *English: Take an umbrella in case it rains.*

> **Watch out:** Careful standard French prescribes the indicative after *après que*, even though the subjunctive is common in actual usage. In formal writing, use *après qu'il est parti / après qu'il sera parti* or recast as *après son départ / après être parti*.

## 5. Sequence of tenses and relative time

> **Learning goal:** Select a subordinate tense relative to the main verb rather than copying English mechanically.

With a present main verb, ordinary tense meanings remain: *Je sais qu'il vient / est venu / viendra*. With a past reporting or thinking verb, French commonly backshifts: present -> imperfect; past compound -> plus-que-parfait; future -> conditional; future anterior -> past conditional.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle affirme qu'elle finira. -> Elle a affirmé qu'elle finirait.
>
> *English: She says she will finish. -> She said she would finish.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je sais qu'il a compris. -> Je savais qu'il avait compris.
>
> *English: I know he understood. -> I knew he had understood.*

Modern French normally keeps the present or past subjunctive after a past main verb: *Je voulais qu'il vienne / qu'il soit venu*. Literary imperfect/pluperfect subjunctives appear at C1-C2.

## 6. Indirect speech: pronouns, time and place

> **Learning goal:** Re-anchor a report to the reporter's person, time and location.

Besides tense, change pronouns and deictic expressions when the viewpoint changes: *ici -> là*, *aujourd'hui -> ce jour-là*, *hier -> la veille*, *demain -> le lendemain*, *la semaine prochaine -> la semaine suivante*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** « Je viendrai ici demain », a dit Léa. -> Léa a dit qu'elle viendrait là le lendemain.
>
> *English: "I will come here tomorrow," Léa said. -> Léa said she would come there the next day.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** « Pourquoi es-tu parti ? » -> Elle m'a demandé pourquoi j'étais parti.
>
> *English: "Why did you leave?" -> She asked me why I had left.*

> **Usage note:** If the report is made in the same time/place frame, some expressions may remain unchanged. The aim is coherent reference, not automatic substitution.

## 7. Compound relative pronouns with prepositions

> **Learning goal:** Choose among *lequel* forms, *qui* and *dont/duquel* after a preposition.

After a simple preposition, use *qui* for people and a *lequel* form for things: *la collègue avec qui je travaille; l'outil avec lequel je travaille*. With compound prepositions ending in *de*, use *duquel/de laquelle/desquels/desquelles* when the *de* belongs to the prepositional phrase: *près duquel, à côté de laquelle*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les raisons pour lesquelles il a refusé restent floues.
>
> *English: The reasons why he refused remain unclear.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** La maison à côté de laquelle j'habite est vide.
>
> *English: The house next to which I live is empty.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le sujet dont nous discutons est complexe.
>
> *English: The subject we are discussing is complex.*

> **Watch out:** Use *dont* for a simple *de* relationship, but *duquel* when *de* is embedded in a larger preposition: *le projet dont je parle* versus *le bâtiment près duquel je travaille*.

## 8. Passive alternatives and se faire

> **Learning goal:** Choose a natural French way to background or omit an agent.

French has several options:

- True passive: *La loi a été adoptée.*
- Indefinite *on*: *On a adopté la loi.*
- Pronominal passive/middle: *Ce produit se vend bien.*
- *Se faire + infinitive*: often an event affecting the subject, voluntary or not: *Elle s'est fait livrer un repas; il s'est fait voler son sac*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Cette expression ne s'emploie plus.
>
> *English: This expression is no longer used.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous nous sommes fait expliquer la procédure.
>
> *English: We had the procedure explained to us.*

> **Watch out:** Before an infinitive, *fait* remains invariable: *elles se sont fait arrêter*. With *laisser + infinitive*, modern spelling also permits or favours invariable *laissé*; usage guides may vary, so maintain one editorial standard.

## 9. Infinitive and participial clause reduction

> **Learning goal:** Compress a subordinate clause when the subject relationship is clear.

- Same subject: *avant de partir; sans répondre; après avoir fini; pour réussir*.
- Different explicit subject after perception: *J'ai vu Paul sortir.*
- Present participle: *Les personnes souhaitant participer...*
- Compound participle: *Ayant terminé, elle est partie; étant arrivé tôt, il a attendu*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Après avoir relu le contrat, elle l'a signé.
>
> *English: After rereading the contract, she signed it.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les candidats ayant obtenu la note requise seront contactés.
>
> *English: Candidates who obtained the required mark will be contacted.*

> **Watch out:** A reduced clause normally shares its understood subject with the clause it modifies. If not, state the subject: *Le travail terminé, nous sommes sortis* is an absolute construction; *Ayant terminé, nous sommes sortis* means that **we** had finished.

## 10. Advanced comparison and proportion

> **Learning goal:** Express difference, progression and correlation precisely.

Useful structures include *de plus en plus*, *de moins en moins*, *plus ... plus*, *moins ... moins*, *d'autant plus/moins ... que*, and *autrement que*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Plus on pratique, plus on progresse.
>
> *English: The more one practises, the more one improves.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Cette solution est d'autant plus utile qu'elle coûte peu.
>
> *English: This solution is all the more useful because it costs little.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il y a deux fois plus de participants que l'an dernier.
>
> *English: There are twice as many participants as last year.*

> **Watch out:** *Plus* is pronounced with or without its final *s* depending on syntax and meaning; pronunciation belongs to phonology, but listening carefully prevents confusion between *plus* meaning **more** and conversational *ne ... plus* meaning **no longer**.

## 11. Indefinite and distributive reference

> **Learning goal:** Distinguish forms such as *chacun, certain, n'importe lequel* and *quelconque*.

- *chacun/chacune* = each one; *chaque* must precede a singular noun.
- *certains/certaines* = some people/things; *un certain* = a certain/some particular.
- *n'importe qui/quoi/où/quand/lequel* = anyone/anything/anywhere/any time/any one.
- *quelconque* after a noun often means any/ordinary; before or in *un quelconque* it may sound dismissive.
- *autrui* = other people/others, formal and invariable.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Chacun doit présenter son passeport.
>
> *English: Each person must show their passport.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Choisissez n'importe laquelle.
>
> *English: Choose any one of them. (feminine reference)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Certains y voient un avantage; d'autres, un risque.
>
> *English: Some see an advantage in it; others, a risk.*

## 12. Governed prepositions and verb patterns

> **Learning goal:** Learn the preposition as part of a verb's grammar.

Meaning can change with the construction: *penser à* (think about) versus *penser de* (have an opinion of); *manquer de* (lack) versus *manquer à quelqu'un* (be missed by someone); *servir à* (be used for) versus *servir de* (serve as).

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je pense souvent à ce voyage.
>
> *English: I often think about that trip.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Que pensez-vous de cette proposition ?
>
> *English: What do you think of this proposal?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Mes amis me manquent.
>
> *English: I miss my friends. (Literally: my friends are missing to me.)*

> **Usage note:** Record a verb as a frame: *convaincre quelqu'un de faire*, *empêcher quelqu'un de faire*, *encourager quelqu'un à faire*, *permettre à quelqu'un de faire*.

## 13. Complex negation and the expletive ne

> **Learning goal:** Distinguish genuine negation from a stylistic *ne* that carries no negative meaning.

The **ne explétif** can appear in careful language after expressions of fear/prevention and before a clause following certain comparisons or *avant que/à moins que*: *Je crains qu'il ne parte; avant qu'il ne soit trop tard; plus difficile qu'on ne le pense*. It does not make the clause negative.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle a peur qu'il ne découvre la vérité.
>
> *English: She is afraid that he may discover the truth.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** La situation est moins grave qu'on ne le croyait.
>
> *English: The situation is less serious than people thought.*

For actual negative meaning, include another negative word: *Je crains qu'il ne vienne pas* = I am afraid he will not come.

## 14. Inversion and formal questions

> **Learning goal:** Form formal questions and stylistic inversion without agreement errors.

With a noun subject, French often uses complex inversion: **noun + verb-pronoun**: *Le directeur viendra-t-il ?* With *qui* as subject, no inversion: *Qui viendra ?* With *que* as object, inversion is possible: *Que voulez-vous ?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Pourquoi cette mesure a-t-elle été adoptée ?
>
> *English: Why was this measure adopted?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** À quelle heure les invités arriveront-ils ?
>
> *English: What time will the guests arrive?*

Literary or formal declarative inversion can follow an initial adverb or reporting clause: *Peut-être viendra-t-il; ainsi commence l'histoire; « Je refuse », répondit-elle.* Avoid imitating it in ordinary conversation.

## 15. Articles, professions and abstract reference

> **Learning goal:** Make article choices in classifications, lists, labels and abstract statements.

- After *être*, an unmodified profession/status usually has no article: *elle est ingénieure*. Modification normally brings an article: *c'est une excellente ingénieure*.
- Abstract or generic nouns usually take the definite article: *La patience est utile; j'aime la musique*.
- Lists and headings may omit articles: *Objectifs : qualité, rapidité, simplicité*.
- After *sans*, both zero article and an article occur depending on whether the noun is viewed generically or specifically: *sans sucre; sans le sucre prévu*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il est devenu président de l'association.
>
> *English: He became president of the association.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** La liberté implique des responsabilités.
>
> *English: Freedom entails responsibilities.*

## 16. Nominalisation

> **Learning goal:** Repackage actions and qualities as nouns for denser formal discourse.

Common suffixes include *-tion/-ation* (*transformer -> transformation*), *-ment* (*développer -> développement*), *-age* (*recycler -> recyclage*), *-ité* (*stable -> stabilité*) and *-ance/-ence* (*résister -> résistance*).

> **Example**
>
> **French:** L'entreprise a réduit ses coûts. -> La réduction des coûts par l'entreprise...
>
> *English: The company reduced its costs. -> The company's reduction of costs...*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Parce que la demande augmente... -> En raison de l'augmentation de la demande...
>
> *English: Because demand is increasing... -> Because of the increase in demand...*

> **Watch out:** Nominalisation can clarify relations and vary style, but too many noun chains make prose heavy. Alternate with finite verbs and make agents explicit when responsibility matters.

## 17. Passé simple: recognition and core formation

> **Learning goal:** Recognise the main literary narrative tense even if you normally speak with the passé composé.

The passé simple carries completed foreground events in literature, history and some formal narratives. Common patterns are *-a-* for *-er* verbs (*il parla, ils parlèrent*), *-i-* for many *-ir/-re* verbs (*il finit, ils finirent; il prit, ils prirent*), and *-u-* for many irregulars (*il eut, fut, put, voulut, reçut*). *Venir/tenir* use *-in-* (*il vint, il tint*).

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il ouvrit la porte, entra et regarda autour de lui.
>
> *English: He opened the door, entered and looked around.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle fut surprise mais ne dit rien.
>
> *English: She was surprised but said nothing.*

> **Usage note:** At B2, receptive control is the priority. In ordinary modern communication, use the passé composé unless genre and register clearly call for the literary tense.

## 18. Difficult agreement points

> **Learning goal:** Check agreement where sound does not reveal the written form.

- Colour adjectives from nouns are often invariable: *des chaussures marron, des yeux orange*. Simple colour adjectives agree: *des robes vertes*. Compound colours are generally invariable: *des yeux bleu clair*.
- Adjectives modifying several nouns are plural; traditional generic/mixed agreement is masculine plural, though inclusive reformulations can avoid ambiguity.
- *Possible* is invariable in *le plus de solutions possible* when it modifies the implicit degree, but agrees when it directly describes a noun: *toutes les solutions possibles*.
- *Demi* is invariable before a noun (*une demi-heure*) and agrees only in gender after it (*deux heures et demie*).

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elles sont restées seules pendant deux heures et demie.
>
> *English: They remained alone for two and a half hours.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous avons examiné le plus de dossiers possible.
>
> *English: We examined as many files as possible.*

## B2 mastery checklist

- I can explain mood choices after opinion, relative clauses and conjunctions.
- I can use the past subjunctive and maintain relative time in reported speech.
- I can choose among passive, *on*, pronominal and *se faire* constructions.
- I can reduce clauses safely with infinitives and participles.
- I can use complex relatives, formal inversion, governed prepositions and advanced negation.
- I can write a structured 250-300 word argument with qualification and cohesive reference.

> **Check yourself:** Explain the contrast: *Je cherche le rapport qui explique le problème* / *Je cherche un rapport qui explique ou qui puisse expliquer le problème.*

> **Answer:** The first clause points to an identified report whose explanatory role is asserted, so the indicative is natural. The second asks for a non-identified report; *explique* can present the desired property as expected, while *puisse expliquer* emphasises possibility/non-identification and therefore uses the subjunctive.
