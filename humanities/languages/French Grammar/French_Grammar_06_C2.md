# C2: stylistic command of the whole system

> *C2 does not mean using the rarest form whenever possible. It means understanding the full range, including literary and historically layered structures, then choosing the simplest form that achieves the exact rhetorical effect.*

## 1. The literary past-tense system

> **Learning goal:** Read and, where genre requires, sustain the relationship among passé simple, imperfect, plus-que-parfait and passé antérieur.

In literary narration, the passé simple advances bounded foreground events while the imperfect supplies background or internal duration. The plus-que-parfait marks prior background. The **passé antérieur** (*eut/fut + past participle*) marks immediate completion before another literary past event, often after *quand, lorsque, dès que, aussitôt que*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Dès qu'il eut refermé la porte, il comprit son erreur.
>
> *English: As soon as he had closed the door, he understood his mistake.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle marchait depuis une heure lorsqu'elle aperçut la maison qu'on lui avait décrite.
>
> *English: She had been walking for an hour when she saw the house that had been described to her.*

> **Usage note:** A coherent literary passage keeps its narrative plane stable. Randomly mixing passé simple and passé composé can sound like an accidental register shift, although deliberate mixtures occur in modern literature and journalism.

## 2. Imperfect and pluperfect subjunctive

> **Learning goal:** Recognise the literary sequence of subjunctive tenses and use it only in an appropriate elevated or historical register.

Traditional sequence after a past main verb uses the **imperfect subjunctive** for simultaneous/later action and the **pluperfect subjunctive** for prior action. Modern French ordinarily substitutes present and past subjunctive.

| Verb | Imperfect subjunctive (3rd singular) | Pluperfect subjunctive (3rd singular) |
| --- | --- | --- |
| parler | qu'il parlât | qu'il eût parlé |
| finir | qu'il finît | qu'il eût fini |
| venir | qu'il vînt | qu'il fût venu |
| être | qu'il fût | qu'il eût été |
| avoir | qu'il eût | qu'il eût eu |

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il fallait qu'elle partît avant qu'il ne fût trop tard.
>
> *English: She had to leave before it was too late.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il regrettait qu'elle eût refusé.
>
> *English: He regretted that she had refused.*

> **Watch out:** Forms such as *que nous partissions* are ambiguous in appearance with the present subjunctive/imperfect indicative pattern; context and the verb stem matter. Productive use outside literary pastiche, historical prose or very formal rhetoric is usually marked.

## 3. Ne littéraire and layered negation

> **Learning goal:** Distinguish omitted *pas* licensed by literary usage from the non-negative *ne explétif*.

In elevated language, *pas* may be omitted with certain verbs and constructions: *je ne saurais vous répondre; il ne cesse de protester; je n'ose le dire; elle ne peut accepter* (the last can be ambiguous outside context). This **ne littéraire** is genuinely negative. The **ne explétif** is not: *je crains qu'il ne vienne* means fear that he will come.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nul ne saurait nier l'évidence.
>
> *English: No one could deny the evidence.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il ne cesse de se plaindre.
>
> *English: He never stops complaining.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** À moins qu'il ne refuse...
>
> *English: Unless he refuses... (*ne* is expletive; refusal is not negated.)*

> **Usage note:** Negative concord can be cumulative or distributive: *Il ne dit jamais rien à personne* = He never says anything to anyone. The placement of *jamais, rien,* and *personne* helps show their grammatical role.

## 4. Fine points of past-participle agreement

> **Learning goal:** Resolve or responsibly recast the most difficult agreement environments.

- With *en*, the participle is generally invariable: *Des lettres, j'en ai écrit beaucoup.*
- With an impersonal verb, no agreement: *Les chaleurs qu'il a fait*; *les efforts qu'il a fallu*.
- A preceding object may not govern agreement if it is the logical object of a following infinitive: *les chansons que j'ai entendu chanter*.
- *Coûter, valoir, peser, mesurer, courir, vivre* can take adverbial measures (no agreement) or true direct objects (agreement depending on sense): *les cent euros que cela a coûté*; *les épreuves qu'elle a vécues*.
- With *avoir l'air*, agreement depends on whether the adjective describes the subject or the noun *air*: *elles ont l'air heureuses*; *elle a l'air sérieux* can describe her serious expression, though recasting is often clearer.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les trois heures qu'a duré la réunion ont paru longues.
>
> *English: The three hours the meeting lasted seemed long.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les difficultés qu'il a fallu surmonter étaient considérables.
>
> *English: The difficulties that had to be overcome were considerable.*

> **Watch out:** Some traditional rules are contested or simplified by modern spelling recommendations and editorial practice. For publication, follow the chosen house style consistently; when no meaning is gained from a spectacular agreement problem, rewrite.

## 5. Scope, attachment and deliberate ambiguity

> **Learning goal:** Detect which constituent an adverb, negation, relative clause or supplement modifies.

Compare *Il n'a probablement pas accepté* (probably did not accept) with *Il n'a pas probablement accepté* (awkward, potentially: did not "probably accept"). Compare *J'ai seulement présenté Paul à Léa* with placements that restrict the person, recipient or action.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Seuls les membres peuvent voter.
>
> *English: Only members may vote.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les membres peuvent seulement voter.
>
> *English: Members may only vote (not perform another action).*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il a parlé à la directrice de l'école qui venait d'ouvrir.
>
> *English: He spoke to the head of the school that had just opened. (Which noun does *qui* modify? Context or revision is needed.)*

> **Usage note:** Expert writing does not eliminate every possible ambiguity; it distinguishes productive ambiguity (irony, suspense, poetic resonance) from accidental ambiguity in instructions, law, science or argument.

## 6. Ellipsis, substitution and parallelism

> **Learning goal:** Omit recoverable material without breaking grammatical parallelism.

French permits ellipsis of a repeated verb or complement, often marked by a comma: *Paul préfère le thé; Léa, le café.* Substitution uses *le faire, en faire autant, celui/celle, tel, ainsi*: *Il avait promis de venir et il l'a fait*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Certains approuvent la réforme; d'autres, non.
>
> *English: Some approve the reform; others do not.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle travaille plus vite que je ne le pensais.
>
> *English: She works faster than I thought.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il souhaite partir, mais pas seul.
>
> *English: He wishes to leave, but not alone.*

> **Watch out:** Keep coordinated structures parallel: *Il souhaite réduire les coûts et que la qualité s'améliore* is understandable but uneven. Prefer *Il souhaite réduire les coûts et améliorer la qualité* or *Il souhaite que les coûts diminuent et que la qualité s'améliore*.

## 7. Relative clauses: restriction, supplementation and stacking

> **Learning goal:** Control the semantic effect of punctuation and embedded relative chains.

A restrictive relative identifies the referent; a supplementary relative adds parenthetical information. Nested relatives can be grammatical yet hard to process. French can use *lequel* forms, repetition or sentence division to repair a chain.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les candidats qui maîtrisent deux langues seront prioritaires.
>
> *English: Candidates who know two languages will receive priority. (Only that subset.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les candidats, qui maîtrisent deux langues, seront prioritaires.
>
> *English: The candidates, who know two languages, will receive priority. (All of them do.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le règlement prévoit une procédure dont les modalités, qui restent à préciser, devront être approuvées.
>
> *English: The regulation provides for a procedure whose arrangements, which remain to be specified, must be approved.*

> **Usage note:** At C2, grammaticality is only the first test. Processing load, referential distance and prose rhythm determine whether a formally correct relative chain should survive editing.

## 8. Determiners and reference as rhetorical choices

> **Learning goal:** Exploit definite, indefinite, demonstrative and zero determination to build a discourse world.

An indefinite noun introduces a discourse referent; a definite noun treats it as identifiable; a demonstrative can reclassify it with a speaker stance: *un projet -> le projet -> ce projet ambitieux*. Generic readings vary: *Le tigre est menacé; Les tigres sont menacés; Un tigre est un félin*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Une inquiétude demeure. Cette inquiétude concerne le financement.
>
> *English: One concern remains. This concern relates to funding.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** L'homme est mortel.
>
> *English: Humankind/man is mortal. (Generic singular.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Hommes et femmes ont réagi différemment.
>
> *English: Men and women reacted differently. (Zero article in a balanced categorical expression.)*

> **Watch out:** Demonstratives can imply distance, familiarity, criticism or shared recognition. *Ce fameux rapport* may mean renowned, notorious or the report everyone keeps mentioning; context supplies the stance.

## 9. Word order, cadence and marked syntax

> **Learning goal:** Manipulate clause weight and rhythm without losing the grammatical anchor.

French tends to place heavier or newer material later, but marked fronting can frame a topic or create suspense. Right dislocation can release a heavy noun phrase; detached adjective/participle phrases can compress evaluation.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Restait une question, la plus difficile de toutes.
>
> *English: One question remained, the most difficult of all.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il est remarquable, ce résultat obtenu avec si peu de moyens.
>
> *English: It is remarkable, this result achieved with so few resources.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Convaincue de l'urgence, la présidente prit la parole.
>
> *English: Convinced of the urgency, the chair took the floor.*

> **Usage note:** Rhythm emerges from syntax, punctuation and information weight. A series of identical *subject + verb + object* clauses is clear but monotonous; constant fronting and parenthesis is vivid but tiring. C2 control balances both.

## 10. Grammar of stance and evidential distance

> **Learning goal:** Calibrate certainty, attribution, evaluation and politeness.

French distributes stance across modal verbs, adverbs, conditional forms, impersonal constructions and reporting frames: *devoir* (inference/obligation), *pouvoir* (possibility/capacity), *sembler/paraître*, *il se peut que + subjunctive*, *selon/d'après*, *à en croire*, and journalistic conditional.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il doit être chez lui.
>
> *English: He must be at home. (Inference.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il se pourrait que les chiffres aient été sous-estimés.
>
> *English: The figures may have been underestimated.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** À en croire plusieurs témoins, la réunion aurait été interrompue.
>
> *English: According to several witnesses, the meeting was reportedly interrupted.*

> **Watch out:** The journalistic conditional distances the writer from a claim; it is not proof of uncertainty by itself. Attribute high-stakes claims explicitly and avoid a chain of vague conditionals.

## 11. Idiomatic valency and construction choice

> **Learning goal:** Select the construction French conventionally uses even when another is logically possible.

Near-synonyms impose different grammar: *se rappeler quelque chose* but *se souvenir de quelque chose*; *aider quelqu'un à faire*; *assister à un événement*; *participer à*; *dépendre de*; *informer quelqu'un de*; *prévenir quelqu'un que/de*; *s'attendre à ce que + subjunctive*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je me rappelle cette conversation. / Je me souviens de cette conversation.
>
> *English: I remember that conversation.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous nous attendons à ce que les prix augmentent.
>
> *English: We expect prices to increase.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il a pallié cette difficulté.
>
> *English: He remedied this difficulty. (Traditional direct construction.)*

> **Usage note:** Corpora and current dictionaries are valuable at this level because valency and register evolve. C2 mastery includes sensitivity to established norm, widespread contemporary usage and audience expectations.

## 12. Register switching and voice

> **Learning goal:** Recast one message across genres without merely swapping vocabulary.

Conversational French uses dislocation, *on*, reduced negation, fragments and discourse markers. Administrative prose favours impersonal structures, nominalisation and explicit logical framing. Literary prose may exploit tense alternation, inversion and ellipsis. Journalistic prose combines compressed attribution with varied sentence rhythm.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** *On peut pas le faire maintenant.*
>
> *English: We cannot do it now. (Informal speech.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** *Nous ne pouvons pas le faire maintenant.*
>
> *English: We cannot do it now. (Neutral standard.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** *Sa mise en oeuvre immédiate ne saurait être envisagée.*
>
> *English: Its immediate implementation cannot be contemplated. (Formal administrative style.)*

> **Watch out:** A formal passive can conceal agency; a conversational *on* can do the same. Register choice is also an ethical choice when it affects who appears responsible.

## 13. Editing for grammatical economy

> **Learning goal:** Diagnose advanced prose for redundancy, over-subordination and hidden reference.

Common C2 editing moves include replacing a noun chain with a verb, splitting a centre-embedded sentence, restoring an agent, removing a redundant pronoun, and converting a vague demonstrative into a lexical noun.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** *La réalisation de l'évaluation de la mise en oeuvre du dispositif...* -> *L'équipe évaluera la mise en oeuvre du dispositif...*
>
> *English: The team will evaluate how the scheme was implemented.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** *C'est une mesure dont on peut dire qu'elle est utile.* -> *Cette mesure est utile.*
>
> *English: This measure is useful.*

> **Usage note:** Sophistication is not maximum density. The best advanced sentence often results from deleting a structure the writer was capable of producing.

## 14. Productive variation without loss of meaning

> **Learning goal:** Paraphrase a proposition through verbal, nominal, passive, impersonal and cleft structures while tracking shifts in focus.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le comité a refusé la proposition.
>
> *English: The committee rejected the proposal. (Agent-focused active.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** La proposition a été refusée par le comité.
>
> *English: The proposal was rejected by the committee. (Patient-focused passive.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le refus de la proposition par le comité a surpris les auteurs.
>
> *English: The committee's rejection of the proposal surprised its authors. (Event nominalised as theme.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** C'est le comité qui a refusé la proposition.
>
> *English: It was the committee that rejected the proposal. (Contrastive agent focus.)*

Each version has a different information structure. A C2 user chooses by discourse purpose, not by a general belief that one voice is superior.

## C2 mastery checklist

- I can read literary past and subjunctive systems without confusing them with modern neutral usage.
- I can resolve or intelligently recast exceptional agreement and attachment problems.
- I can control scope, ellipsis, parallelism, reference and clause stacking.
- I can use tense, mood, modality and attribution to calibrate stance.
- I can switch grammatical register across conversation, essay, report, journalism and literary prose.
- I can edit for economy and explain how each syntactic alternative changes focus or responsibility.

> **Check yourself:** Recast formally and then plainly: *On dit qu'ils auraient peut-être sous-estimé le coût.*

> **Answer:** Formal/evidential: *D'après plusieurs sources, le coût aurait été sous-estimé.* Plain with a named agent, if known: *Les analystes ont peut-être sous-estimé le coût.* The versions do not make identical evidential commitments; the writer must choose an attribution that the evidence supports.
