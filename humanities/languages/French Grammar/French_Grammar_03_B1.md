# B1: narrating, hypothesising and connecting clauses

> *B1 grammar turns separate sentences into connected discourse. You should control the time line of a story, express conditions and reactions, report another person's words and avoid ambiguity when several pronouns or clauses interact.*

## 1. The plus-que-parfait

> **Learning goal:** Mark an event that was already complete before another past reference point.

> **Pattern:** **avoir or être in the imperfect + past participle**: *j'avais parlé, elle était partie, nous nous étions levés*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Quand je suis arrivé, le train était déjà parti.
>
> *English: When I arrived, the train had already left.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle connaissait Rome parce qu'elle y avait vécu.
>
> *English: She knew Rome because she had lived there.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous ne nous étions jamais rencontrés.
>
> *English: We had never met.*

> **Usage note:** The plus-que-parfait is relative: it looks backward from a past point. It often explains a past result or supplies earlier background.

## 2. Building a past narrative

> **Learning goal:** Coordinate the imperfect, passé composé and plus-que-parfait.

- **Imperfect**: scene, state, habit or action in progress.
- **Passé composé**: main bounded events and changes.
- **Plus-que-parfait**: events already completed before that past scene.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il pleuvait. J'ai ouvert mon sac et j'ai compris que j'avais oublié mon parapluie.
>
> *English: It was raining. I opened my bag and realised that I had forgotten my umbrella.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Pendant que nous attendions, quelqu'un a éteint la lumière.
>
> *English: While we were waiting, someone switched off the light.*

Sequence markers help the reader: *d'abord, ensuite, puis, tout à coup, pendant ce temps, finalement*. Choose tense for viewpoint, not because a marker automatically demands it.

> **Check yourself:** Complete naturally: *La salle (être) vide parce que tout le monde (partir).*

> **Answer:** *La salle était vide parce que tout le monde était parti.*

## 3. The future anterior

> **Learning goal:** Present a future action as completed before another future point or as a probability about the past.

> **Pattern:** **avoir or être in the future simple + past participle**: *j'aurai fini, elle sera arrivée*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Quand tu auras terminé, nous sortirons.
>
> *English: When you have finished, we will go out.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** D'ici vendredi, ils auront pris une décision.
>
> *English: By Friday, they will have made a decision.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il n'est pas là; il aura oublié le rendez-vous.
>
> *English: He is not here; he has probably forgotten the appointment.*

> **Watch out:** In a future time clause, French uses future forms after *quand/dès que/lorsque*: *Dès qu'elle sera arrivée, je t'appellerai.*

## 4. Present and past conditional

> **Learning goal:** Express a hypothetical result, regret, unconfirmed information or a future seen from the past.

Present conditional = future stem + imperfect endings. Past conditional = conditional of *avoir/être* + past participle.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Avec plus de temps, je voyagerais davantage.
>
> *English: With more time, I would travel more.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** J'aurais accepté, mais j'étais absent.
>
> *English: I would have accepted, but I was away.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le ministre démissionnerait demain.
>
> *English: The minister is reportedly expected to resign tomorrow.*

The conditional also reports a future from a past viewpoint: *Elle a dit qu'elle viendrait* (she said she would come).

## 5. Si clauses

> **Learning goal:** Match the condition to a plausible, hypothetical or counterfactual result.

| Meaning | Si clause | Result clause |
| --- | --- | --- |
| General truth | present | present |
| Real/open future | present | present, future or imperative |
| Hypothetical present/future | imperfect | present conditional |
| Counterfactual past | plus-que-parfait | past conditional |

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Si on chauffe l'eau, elle bout.
>
> *English: If water is heated, it boils.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Si tu viens demain, nous irons au marché.
>
> *English: If you come tomorrow, we will go to the market.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Si j'avais plus d'argent, j'achèterais cette maison.
>
> *English: If I had more money, I would buy this house.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Si nous avions su, nous serions partis plus tôt.
>
> *English: If we had known, we would have left earlier.*

> **Watch out:** Do not place the future or conditional directly after ordinary conditional *si*: not *si tu viendras* or *si tu viendrais*. The conjunction *si* meaning **whether** follows different rules: *Je ne sais pas s'il viendra.*

## 6. The present subjunctive: formation

> **Learning goal:** Build the high-frequency subjunctive after expressions that require a non-asserted or evaluated clause.

For most verbs, take the present *ils* stem, remove *-ent*, and add *-e, -es, -e, -ions, -iez, -ent*. The *nous/vous* forms may use the present *nous* stem: *boive/boivions; prenne/prenions*.

Important irregular stems include *soi-* (*être*), *ai-* (*avoir*), *aill-* (*aller*), *fass-* (*faire*), *puiss-* (*pouvoir*), *sach-* (*savoir*), *veuill-* (*vouloir*), and *fall-* (*falloir*).

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il faut que vous soyez prudents.
>
> *English: You must be careful.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je veux qu'elle vienne.
>
> *English: I want her to come.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous sommes heureux que tu puisses rester.
>
> *English: We are happy that you can stay.*

## 7. Core subjunctive triggers

> **Learning goal:** Use the subjunctive after necessity, will, emotion, doubt and certain conjunctions.

- Necessity/judgment: *il faut que, il est important que, il vaut mieux que*.
- Will/order: *vouloir que, demander que, interdire que*.
- Emotion: *être content/triste/surpris que, avoir peur que*.
- Doubt/denial: *douter que, ne pas croire que, nier que*.
- Conjunctions: *pour que, afin que, avant que, bien que, sans que, à condition que*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Bien qu'il soit tard, nous continuons.
>
> *English: Although it is late, we are continuing.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je répète pour que tout le monde comprenne.
>
> *English: I am repeating it so that everyone understands.*

> **Usage note:** If both verbs have the same subject, French often prefers an infinitive: *Je veux partir* but *je veux que tu partes*; *avant de partir* but *avant qu'il parte*.

## 8. Relative pronouns dont, lequel and ce qui/ce que

> **Learning goal:** Link clauses when the missing element is introduced by *de*, another preposition, or has no stated antecedent.

- **dont** replaces *de + noun/person*: *le livre dont je parle; la femme dont le fils travaille ici*.
- **lequel/laquelle/lesquels/lesquelles** commonly follow prepositions: *la chaise sur laquelle je suis assis*.
- *à + lequel* contracts to *auquel/auxquels/auxquelles*; *de + lequel* to *duquel/desquels/desquelles*.
- **ce qui** = what/that which as subject; **ce que** = what/that which as direct object; **ce dont** = what + *de*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Voilà ce dont j'ai besoin.
>
> *English: That is what I need.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le dossier auquel je pense est urgent.
>
> *English: The file I am thinking about is urgent.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je ne comprends pas ce qui se passe.
>
> *English: I do not understand what is happening.*

> **Watch out:** *Dont* already contains *de*. Do not say *dont j'en parle*; choose *dont je parle* or *j'en parle*.

## 9. Double object pronouns and compound tenses

> **Learning goal:** Maintain pronoun order across negation, infinitives and compound tenses.

> **Pattern:** Before a normal finite verb: **me/te/se/nous/vous + le/la/les + lui/leur + y + en**. In compound tenses, pronouns precede the auxiliary.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je le lui ai envoyé hier.
>
> *English: I sent it to him/her yesterday.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle ne nous en a pas parlé.
>
> *English: She did not talk to us about it.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous allons vous y conduire.
>
> *English: We are going to take you there.*

Affirmative imperative order is **verb + le/la/les + moi/toi/lui/nous/vous/leur + y + en**: *Donnez-le-lui.* Before *en* and *y*, *moi/toi* become *m'/t'*: *Donne-m'en.*

## 10. Past participle agreement

> **Learning goal:** Apply the central agreement rules in compound tenses.

- With **être**, agree with the subject: *elles sont arrivées*.
- With **avoir**, normally no agreement, but agree with a preceding direct object: *les lettres que j'ai écrites; je les ai écrites*.
- No agreement with a preceding indirect object: *les personnes à qui j'ai parlé*.
- Pronominal verbs require analysis: agreement occurs with a preceding direct object, not with an indirect reflexive pronoun.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Marie s'est lavée.
>
> *English: Marie washed herself. (*se* is direct.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Marie s'est lavé les mains.
>
> *English: Marie washed her hands. (*les mains* follows; *se* is indirect.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les mains qu'elle s'est lavées étaient froides.
>
> *English: The hands that she washed were cold. (Direct object precedes.)*

> **Watch out:** Invariable constructions include *ils se sont parlé, elles se sont téléphoné, ils se sont souri* because the underlying verbs use *à*: *parler à, téléphoner à, sourire à*.

## 11. The passive voice

> **Learning goal:** Shift attention from the agent to the receiver of an action.

> **Pattern:** **subject + être in the required tense + past participle (+ par + agent)**. The participle agrees with the passive subject.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le pont a été construit en 1920.
>
> *English: The bridge was built in 1920.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Ces décisions seront annoncées demain.
>
> *English: These decisions will be announced tomorrow.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le roman est apprécié par les étudiants.
>
> *English: The novel is appreciated by the students.*

Use *de* rather than *par* with some state/feeling verbs: *aimé de tous, connu de tous, entouré d'arbres*. French often prefers active *on*: *On a construit le pont en 1920.*

## 12. Faire + infinitive: the causative

> **Learning goal:** Express causing or arranging for an action to happen.

> **Pattern:** **faire conjugated + infinitive**. The two verbs behave as one unit: *je fais réparer, nous avons fait installer*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je fais réparer ma voiture.
>
> *English: I am having my car repaired.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le professeur fait lire le texte aux étudiants.
>
> *English: The teacher makes the students read the text.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle s'est fait couper les cheveux.
>
> *English: She had her hair cut.*

> **Usage note:** The infinitive remains unchanged. In modern standard spelling, the participle *fait* is invariable before an infinitive: *les chansons qu'elle a fait enregistrer*.

## 13. Present participle and gerund

> **Learning goal:** Express simultaneous action, manner, means or cause compactly.

The present participle usually uses the *nous* stem + *-ant*: *parlant, finissant, prenant*. Irregular forms include *ayant, étant, sachant*. The **gerund** is *en + present participle*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il apprend en écoutant des podcasts.
>
> *English: He learns by listening to podcasts.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** En sortant, j'ai vu Léa.
>
> *English: While leaving, I saw Léa.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Ayant peu de temps, nous avons pris un taxi.
>
> *English: Having little time, we took a taxi.*

> **Watch out:** The gerund's understood subject is normally the same as the main clause subject. Avoid dangling structures equivalent to "While leaving, the rain began."

## 14. Reported speech

> **Learning goal:** Report statements, questions and commands without quoting exactly.

Use *que* for statements, *si* for yes/no questions, the original question word for information questions, and *de + infinitive* for commands.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle dit : « Je suis prête. » -> Elle dit qu'elle est prête.
>
> *English: She says that she is ready.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il demande : « Tu viens ? » -> Il demande si je viens.
>
> *English: He asks whether I am coming.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le médecin dit : « Reposez-vous. » -> Le médecin me dit de me reposer.
>
> *English: The doctor tells me to rest.*

After a past reporting verb, common backshifts are present -> imperfect, passé composé -> plus-que-parfait, and future -> conditional: *Il a dit qu'il était fatigué, qu'il avait fini et qu'il partirait.* Context can preserve the original tense when the information remains current.

## 15. Cause, consequence, purpose, concession and opposition

> **Learning goal:** Make logical relations explicit.

| Relation | Frequent connectors | Mood/form |
| --- | --- | --- |
| cause | parce que, puisque, comme, à cause de, grâce à | indicative or noun |
| consequence | donc, alors, c'est pourquoi, si ... que, tellement ... que | usually indicative |
| purpose | pour + infinitive; pour que, afin que | infinitive / subjunctive |
| concession | bien que, quoique; malgré | subjunctive / noun |
| opposition | mais, tandis que, alors que, en revanche | usually indicative |

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Comme il pleuvait, nous sommes restés chez nous.
>
> *English: As it was raining, we stayed home.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle parle lentement afin que chacun comprenne.
>
> *English: She speaks slowly so that everyone understands.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Malgré la pluie, ils sont sortis.
>
> *English: Despite the rain, they went out.*

## 16. Restriction with ne ... que

> **Learning goal:** Express **only** by restricting the element after *que*.

> **Pattern:** Place *ne/n'* before the conjugated verb and *que/qu'* directly before the restricted element.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je n'ai que dix euros.
>
> *English: I only have ten euros.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle ne travaille que le matin.
>
> *English: She works only in the morning.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous n'avons parlé qu'à Paul.
>
> *English: We spoke only to Paul.*

> **Watch out:** *Ne ... que* is restrictive, not negative in meaning. Articles do not automatically become *de*: *Je ne mange que du pain.*

## 17. Emphasis with c'est ... qui/que and dislocation

> **Learning goal:** Highlight a chosen element or maintain a conversational topic.

- *C'est X qui + verb* highlights the subject.
- *C'est X que + subject + verb* highlights a direct object or many adverbials.
- Left/right dislocation repeats a noun with a pronoun: *Ce film, je l'adore. Je l'adore, ce film.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** C'est Léa qui a gagné.
>
> *English: It was Léa who won.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** C'est ce livre que je préfère.
>
> *English: It is this book that I prefer.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Moi, je ne suis pas d'accord.
>
> *English: As for me, I disagree.*

> **Usage note:** Dislocation is extremely common in speech but should be used selectively in formal prose, where an unmarked clause is often more concise.

## B1 mastery checklist

- I can manage a three-tense past narrative and a future sequence.
- I can express real, hypothetical and counterfactual conditions.
- I can form the present subjunctive and use its common triggers.
- I can report speech, use the passive and causative, and link clauses logically.
- I can order two object pronouns and apply the main past-participle agreement rules.
- I can write a coherent 180-220 word narrative or opinion with paragraph-level organisation.

> **Check yourself:** Rewrite with pronouns: *J'ai envoyé les photos à mes parents, mais je n'ai pas parlé du voyage à mes parents.*

> **Answer:** *Je les leur ai envoyées, mais je ne leur en ai pas parlé.* *Envoyées* agrees with the preceding direct object *les* (= *les photos*).
