# A2: linking events and replacing repeated nouns

> *At A2, the sentence expands. You learn to move among present, past and future, to replace nouns with pronouns, and to combine clauses with relative words and time expressions.*

## 1. Regular -ir and -re patterns

> **Learning goal:** Extend present-tense control beyond regular *-er* verbs.

Regular *finir*-type **-ir** verbs use *-is, -is, -it, -issons, -issez, -issent*. Regular **-re** verbs such as *vendre* use *-s, -s, no ending, -ons, -ez, -ent*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous finissons à dix-huit heures.
>
> *English: We finish at six p.m.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elles choisissent le menu végétarien.
>
> *English: They choose the vegetarian menu.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je vends mon ancien ordinateur.
>
> *English: I am selling my old computer.*

> **Watch out:** Not every *-ir* verb follows *finir*. *Partir, sortir, dormir, venir* and *ouvrir* have other patterns. Learn a verb with its *je, nous,* and *ils* forms to reveal the stem changes.

## 2. Stem families among irregular verbs

> **Learning goal:** Notice recurring families instead of memorising every form in isolation.

- *partir, sortir, dormir*: *je pars/sors/dors; nous partons/sortons/dormons; ils partent/sortent/dorment*.
- *ouvrir, offrir, couvrir*: present endings resemble *-er*: *j'ouvre, nous ouvrons, ils ouvrent*.
- *lire, dire, écrire*: *je lis/dis/écris; nous lisons/disons/écrivons*. Note *vous dites*.
- *boire, croire, voir*: *je bois/crois/vois; nous buvons/croyons/voyons; ils boivent/croient/voient*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle ouvre la fenêtre et lit son message.
>
> *English: She opens the window and reads her message.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous partons tôt mais ils dorment encore.
>
> *English: We are leaving early but they are still sleeping.*

## 3. Pronominal verbs

> **Learning goal:** Conjugate verbs used with *me, te, se, nous, vous, se*.

> **Pattern:** **subject + reflexive pronoun + conjugated verb**: *je me lève, tu te lèves, il se lève, nous nous levons, vous vous levez, elles se lèvent*.

Pronominal verbs may be reflexive (*se laver*), reciprocal (*se téléphoner*), idiomatic (*se souvenir de*) or passive-like (*ça se dit*). In an infinitive construction, the reflexive pronoun matches the understood subject: *Je vais me coucher*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous nous rencontrons devant la gare.
>
> *English: We meet each other in front of the station.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle ne se couche pas tard.
>
> *English: She does not go to bed late.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Vous devez vous inscrire en ligne.
>
> *English: You must register online.*

> **Watch out:** The reflexive pronoun goes before the conjugated verb, and *ne ... pas* surrounds that verb: *Je ne me lève pas tôt.*

## 4. The passé composé with avoir

> **Learning goal:** Report a completed event with a clear endpoint.

> **Pattern:** **avoir in the present + past participle**. Regular participles are *-er -> -é*, *-ir -> -i*, *-re -> -u*: *parlé, fini, vendu*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** J'ai visité le Louvre hier.
>
> *English: I visited the Louvre yesterday.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous avons fini le projet.
>
> *English: We finished the project.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle n'a pas répondu.
>
> *English: She did not reply.*

Frequent irregular participles include *eu, été, fait, dit, écrit, lu, vu, bu, pris, appris, compris, mis, ouvert, offert, reçu, voulu, pu, dû, su, venu*.

> **Usage note:** In simple *avoir* constructions, the participle does not agree with a following direct object: *Elle a acheté des fleurs.* More complex agreement appears at B1.

## 5. The passé composé with être

> **Learning goal:** Form the compound past of pronominal verbs and a core group of intransitive movement/change verbs.

All pronominal verbs use *être*. Frequent non-pronominal *être* verbs include *aller, arriver, entrer, rentrer, monter, descendre, naître, mourir, partir, passer, rester, retourner, sortir, tomber, venir* and derivatives such as *revenir*.

The past participle normally agrees with the subject: *elle est arrivée; ils sont partis; elles se sont levées*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Léa est née en 1998.
>
> *English: Léa was born in 1998.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous sommes rentrés tard.
>
> *English: We came home late.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elles se sont promenées au bord de la mer.
>
> *English: They went for a walk by the sea.*

> **Watch out:** Some movement verbs take *avoir* when transitive: *Elle est sortie* (she went out) but *Elle a sorti son téléphone* (she took out her phone); *Nous sommes montés* but *nous avons monté les valises*.

## 6. The imperfect

> **Learning goal:** Describe past background, habits and ongoing states.

> **Pattern:** Take the present **nous** form, remove *-ons*, and add *-ais, -ais, -ait, -ions, -iez, -aient*. The only irregular stem is *être -> ét-*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Quand j'étais enfant, je jouais dehors.
>
> *English: When I was a child, I used to play outside.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il faisait froid et la rue était vide.
>
> *English: It was cold and the street was empty.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous attendions le bus.
>
> *English: We were waiting for the bus.*

Spelling changes remain visible in *nous* and *vous*: *nous mangions, vous commenciez*. The endings *-ais, -ait,* and *-aient* sound alike.

## 7. Passé composé or imperfect?

> **Learning goal:** Contrast completed foreground events with background, habit or an action in progress.

- Use the **passé composé** for a bounded event, a sequence or a change: *Le téléphone a sonné.*
- Use the **imperfect** for setting, repeated habit, state or ongoing action: *Je dormais.*
- A common narrative pattern is imperfect background + passé composé interruption.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je cuisinais quand Paul a appelé.
>
> *English: I was cooking when Paul called.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Tous les étés, nous allions en Bretagne.
>
> *English: Every summer, we used to go to Brittany.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Soudain, la lumière s'est éteinte.
>
> *English: Suddenly, the light went out.*

> **Watch out:** Time length alone does not decide. *J'ai travaillé pendant trois heures* presents a completed three-hour block; *je travaillais depuis trois heures* presents an activity already in progress.

> **Check yourself:** Choose: *Il (pleuvoir) quand nous (quitter) la maison.*

> **Answer:** *Il pleuvait quand nous avons quitté la maison.*

## 8. The future simple

> **Learning goal:** Express future facts, promises, predictions and more distant plans.

> **Pattern:** Add *-ai, -as, -a, -ons, -ez, -ont* to the infinitive; drop final *-e* from *-re* verbs: *parlerai, finirons, vendra*. Irregular stems include *ser-, aur-, ir-, fer-, viendr-, voudr-, pourr-, devr-, saur-, verr-, enverr-, recevr-*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Demain, je travaillerai de chez moi.
>
> *English: Tomorrow I will work from home.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous serons là à huit heures.
>
> *English: We will be there at eight.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Vous recevrez un courriel.
>
> *English: You will receive an email.*

> **Usage note:** After *quand, lorsque, dès que* and *aussitôt que*, French can use a future where English uses a present: *Quand tu arriveras, appelle-moi.*

## 9. The present conditional for politeness and imagined situations

> **Learning goal:** Make requests less direct and state a hypothetical preference.

> **Pattern:** Use the **future stem + imperfect endings**: *je parlerais, nous finirions, il viendrait*. The irregular stems are the same as the future.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je voudrais un café, s'il vous plaît.
>
> *English: I would like a coffee, please.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Pourriez-vous m'aider ?
>
> *English: Could you help me?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** J'aimerais vivre près de la mer.
>
> *English: I would like to live near the sea.*

> **Watch out:** In writing, distinguish future *-ai* from conditional *-ais*: *je partirai* (I will leave) versus *je partirais* (I would leave).

## 10. Direct object pronouns

> **Learning goal:** Replace a person or thing that receives the action directly.

Direct pronouns are *me/m', te/t', le/la/l', nous, vous, les*. They normally appear before the conjugated verb and before an infinitive when they belong to the infinitive.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Tu connais Marie ? Oui, je la connais.
>
> *English: Do you know Marie? Yes, I know her.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** J'achète les billets. Je les achète en ligne.
>
> *English: I am buying the tickets. I am buying them online.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous allons le visiter demain.
>
> *English: We are going to visit it/him tomorrow.*

> **Watch out:** A direct object has no preceding *à*. Compare *j'appelle Paul -> je l'appelle* with *je parle à Paul -> je lui parle*.

## 11. Indirect object pronouns

> **Learning goal:** Replace a person introduced by *à* after common communication and transfer verbs.

Indirect person pronouns are *me/m', te/t', lui, nous, vous, leur*. Use them with verbs such as *parler à, téléphoner à, écrire à, répondre à, donner à*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je téléphone à mes parents. Je leur téléphone.
>
> *English: I phone my parents. I phone them.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle lui donne l'adresse.
>
> *English: She gives him/her the address.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Vous pouvez me répondre demain.
>
> *English: You can answer me tomorrow.*

> **Watch out:** *Lui* can mean **to him** or **to her**. *Leur* has no written *-s* as an indirect object pronoun: *je leur parle*.

## 12. Y and en

> **Learning goal:** Replace repeated place or *à/de* complements.

- **y** usually replaces a place or *à + thing*: *Je vais à Lille -> J'y vais*; *Je pense à ce problème -> J'y pense*.
- **en** usually replaces *de + thing*, a partitive/indefinite noun, or a quantity: *Je parle de ce film -> J'en parle*; *Tu veux du pain ? J'en veux.*
- Keep the quantity after *en*: *J'en prends deux.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Vous allez à la bibliothèque ? Oui, j'y vais.
>
> *English: Are you going to the library? Yes, I am going there.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle a trois enfants. Elle en a trois.
>
> *English: She has three children. She has three of them.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous avons besoin de temps. Nous en avons besoin.
>
> *English: We need time. We need it.*

> **Watch out:** For people, prefer a stressed pronoun after many prepositions: *Je pense à elle*, not usually *j'y pense* when *elle* is a person.

## 13. Basic pronoun order

> **Learning goal:** Place one or two object pronouns before a finite verb in the standard sequence.

> **Pattern:** **me/te/se/nous/vous + le/la/les + lui/leur + y + en + verb**. With one pronoun, simply select its slot. With two, keep this order.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il me le donne.
>
> *English: He gives it to me.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous leur en parlons.
>
> *English: We talk to them about it.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je ne vais pas lui écrire.
>
> *English: I am not going to write to him/her.*

In an affirmative imperative, the order changes and pronouns follow the verb: *Donne-le-moi.* In a negative imperative, normal pre-verbal order returns: *Ne me le donne pas.*

## 14. Relative pronouns qui, que and où

> **Learning goal:** Join two sentences without repeating the shared noun.

- **qui** is the subject of the following verb: *la femme qui parle*.
- **que/qu'** is the direct object of the following subject + verb: *le film que j'aime*.
- **où** expresses place or time: *la ville où je vis; le jour où il est arrivé*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** J'ai un voisin qui joue du piano.
>
> *English: I have a neighbour who plays the piano.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Voici le livre que tu cherches.
>
> *English: Here is the book you are looking for.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** C'est l'année où nous nous sommes rencontrés.
>
> *English: That is the year when we met.*

> **Watch out:** Look immediately after the relative pronoun: if a conjugated verb comes next and has no other subject, use *qui*; if a subject comes next, use *que*.

## 15. Comparison and superlatives

> **Learning goal:** Compare qualities, actions and quantities.

- Adjective/adverb: *plus/aussi/moins + adjective/adverb + que*.
- Noun quantity: *plus de/autant de/moins de + noun + que*.
- Verb: *verb + plus/autant/moins que*.
- Superlative: *le/la/les plus* or *le/la/les moins*; with adverbs, usually *le plus*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Cette chambre est plus calme que l'autre.
>
> *English: This room is quieter than the other one.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Léa travaille autant que Marc.
>
> *English: Léa works as much as Marc.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** C'est le meilleur restaurant du quartier.
>
> *English: It is the best restaurant in the neighbourhood.*

Irregular forms: *bon -> meilleur*; *bien -> mieux*. Use *meilleur* as an adjective and *mieux* as an adverb.

## 16. Forming adverbs

> **Learning goal:** Modify a verb, adjective, adverb or whole sentence.

Many adverbs come from the feminine adjective + **-ment**: *lent -> lente -> lentement; heureux -> heureusement*. Adjectives ending in *-ant* often form *-amment*; those in *-ent* often form *-emment*: *constant -> constamment; prudent -> prudemment*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle parle doucement.
>
> *English: She speaks softly.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** C'est vraiment intéressant.
>
> *English: It is really interesting.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Heureusement, personne n'a été blessé.
>
> *English: Fortunately, nobody was injured.*

Common non-derived adverbs include *bien, mal, vite, tôt, tard, souvent, parfois, déjà, encore,* and *toujours*.

## 17. Extended negation

> **Learning goal:** Replace *pas* with a more precise negative expression.

| Structure | Meaning | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ne ... jamais | never | *Je ne fume jamais.* |
| ne ... plus | no longer / no more | *Il ne travaille plus ici.* |
| ne ... rien | nothing / not anything | *Je ne vois rien.* |
| ne ... personne | nobody / not anybody | *Elle ne connaît personne.* |
| ne ... ni ... ni | neither ... nor | *Il ne boit ni thé ni café.* |

When *personne* or *rien* is the subject, it precedes *ne*: *Personne ne répond. Rien ne change.* With a compound tense, *jamais, plus,* and *rien* usually follow the auxiliary; *personne* commonly follows the participle.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je n'ai rien compris.
>
> *English: I understood nothing.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous n'avons vu personne.
>
> *English: We did not see anybody.*

## 18. Indefinite determiners and pronouns

> **Learning goal:** Refer to non-specific people, things or amounts.

Frequent forms include *chaque* (each, singular noun), *quelques* (a few), *plusieurs* (several), *tout/toute/tous/toutes* (all/every/whole), *quelqu'un* (someone), *quelque chose* (something), *personne* and *rien*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Chaque étudiant reçoit un dossier.
>
> *English: Each student receives a file.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Plusieurs sont déjà arrivés.
>
> *English: Several have already arrived.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Tout le monde est prêt.
>
> *English: Everybody is ready.*

> **Watch out:** *Tout le monde* is grammatically singular: *Tout le monde est là*, not *sont là*.

## 19. Depuis, pendant, pour, il y a and dans

> **Learning goal:** Express duration and locate an event relative to now.

- *depuis + starting point/duration* with a present verb = began in the past and continues: *J'habite ici depuis 2020.*
- *pendant + duration* = completed or bounded duration: *J'ai attendu pendant une heure.*
- *pour + intended duration* is common with plans: *Je pars pour deux semaines.*
- *il y a + duration* = ago: *Il est parti il y a dix minutes.*
- *dans + duration* = in/from now: *Il reviendra dans une heure.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle apprend le français depuis six mois.
>
> *English: She has been learning French for six months.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous resterons à Nice pendant trois jours.
>
> *English: We will stay in Nice for three days.*

## 20. Infinitive links and basic connectors

> **Learning goal:** Link actions efficiently and avoid conjugating every verb.

After a conjugated verb, the next verb often stays infinitive: *aimer faire, vouloir faire, pouvoir faire, devoir faire*. Some verbs require *à* (*commencer à, réussir à, apprendre à*); others require *de* (*essayer de, décider de, arrêter de, oublier de*).

> **Example**
>
> **French:** J'essaie de comprendre.
>
> *English: I am trying to understand.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle commence à travailler.
>
> *English: She starts working.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous sortons pour acheter du pain.
>
> *English: We are going out to buy bread.*

Use *et, mais, ou, parce que, donc, puis, ensuite, avant de,* and *après + infinitive past* to connect ideas. At this level, *après avoir/être + past participle* may be learned as a useful chunk: *Après avoir mangé, nous sommes partis.*

## A2 mastery checklist

- I can narrate a short past event with the passé composé and add background in the imperfect.
- I can express a future plan with *aller* and a future fact with the future simple.
- I can use direct, indirect, *y* and *en* pronouns without repeating every noun.
- I can join ideas with *qui, que, où* and basic logical/time connectors.
- I can use extended negatives, comparison, pronominal verbs and duration expressions.
- I can write a clear 120-word account of a trip or memorable day.

> **Check yourself:** Translate: "I had two tickets, but I gave one to my sister because she had never visited the museum."

> **Answer:** *J'avais deux billets, mais j'en ai donné un à ma soeur parce qu'elle n'avait jamais visité le musée.* The plus-que-parfait *n'avait jamais visité* is introduced fully at B1; at early A2, *parce qu'elle ne connaissait pas le musée* is a simpler alternative.
