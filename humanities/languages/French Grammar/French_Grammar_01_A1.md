# A1: building accurate simple sentences

> *At A1, concentrate on a reliable sentence frame. You should be able to identify people and things, describe them, state habits and immediate plans, ask basic questions, negate, count and locate.*

## 1. Definite, indefinite and plural articles

> **Learning goal:** Choose an article that matches the noun's gender, number and meaning.

- **Definite**: *le* (masculine), *la* (feminine), *l'* (before a vowel/mute *h*), *les* (plural). Use them for something identified, a class in general, or a recurring preference.
- **Indefinite**: *un* (masculine), *une* (feminine), *des* (plural). Use them to introduce one or more non-identified countable things.
- A French countable singular noun normally needs a determiner: article, possessive, demonstrative or similar word.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** J'achète un livre. Le livre est intéressant.
>
> *English: I am buying a book. The book is interesting.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle aime le café.
>
> *English: She likes coffee.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous avons des voisins sympathiques.
>
> *English: We have some friendly neighbours.*

> **Watch out:** After verbs of preference such as *aimer, adorer, préférer* and *détester*, use the definite article for a general category: *J'aime le chocolat*, not *j'aime du chocolat* when you mean chocolate in general.

> **Check yourself:** Complete: "I am looking for a hotel. The hotel is near the station."

> **Answer:** *Je cherche un hôtel. L'hôtel est près de la gare.*

## 2. Noun gender and plural

> **Learning goal:** Treat gender as part of the noun and form the common written plural.

Most nouns add **-s** in the plural, although the *s* is usually silent. Nouns ending in *-s, -x,* or *-z* do not change. Many nouns ending in *-eau* take *-x*; many ending in *-al* change to *-aux*. Important exceptions must be learned individually.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** une table -> des tables
>
> *English: a table -> tables*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** un journal -> des journaux
>
> *English: a newspaper -> newspapers*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** un prix -> des prix
>
> *English: a price -> prices*

Common tendencies help but do not replace learning the article: *-tion, -sion, -té,* and *-ette* are often feminine; *-age, -ment, -eau,* and *-isme* are often masculine. Exceptions include *la plage, la page, l'image* and *l'eau* (feminine).

> **Watch out:** Biological sex does not reliably predict grammatical gender for objects, and the noun's ending is only a clue. Record new vocabulary as *la décision*, not merely *décision*.

## 3. Subject pronouns and the conversational pronoun on

> **Learning goal:** Match the verb to *je, tu, il/elle/on, nous, vous,* or *ils/elles*.

| Person | Pronoun | Core meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1st singular | je / j' | I |
| 2nd singular | tu | you, familiar singular |
| 3rd singular | il / elle / on | he, she, it, one/we |
| 1st plural | nous | we |
| 2nd plural | vous | you, plural or respectful singular |
| 3rd plural | ils / elles | they; *elles* only for an all-female group |

*On* always takes a third-person singular verb. In conversation it very often means *we*. Adjectives may reflect the real people: *On est contents* (a mixed group), *On est contentes* (an all-female group).

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Tu habites ici ?
>
> *English: Do you live here?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** On va au cinéma.
>
> *English: We are going to the cinema.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elles parlent français.
>
> *English: They speak French.*

> **Watch out:** *Vous* may refer to one person politely or to several people. The verb form stays plural in both cases: *Vous êtes prêt, Monsieur ?*; *Vous êtes prêts, tout le monde ?*

## 4. Être and avoir in the present

> **Learning goal:** Use the two most important irregular verbs for identity, description, possession and age.

| Subject | être | avoir |
| --- | --- | --- |
| je / j' | suis | ai |
| tu | es | as |
| il / elle / on | est | a |
| nous | sommes | avons |
| vous | êtes | avez |
| ils / elles | sont | ont |

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je suis étudiant et j'ai vingt ans.
>
> *English: I am a student and I am twenty years old.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous avons une question.
>
> *English: We have a question.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elles sont fatiguées.
>
> *English: They are tired.*

> **Watch out:** French uses *avoir* for age and several physical states: *avoir faim, avoir soif, avoir chaud, avoir froid, avoir peur, avoir besoin de*. Say *J'ai 30 ans*, not *je suis 30 ans*.

## 5. Regular -er verbs in the present

> **Learning goal:** Describe habits and current actions with the largest regular verb group.

> **Pattern:** Remove **-er** and add **-e, -es, -e, -ons, -ez, -ent**: *je parle, tu parles, elle parle, nous parlons, vous parlez, ils parlent*.

The endings *-e, -es,* and *-ent* are normally pronounced alike or are silent. The *nous* and *vous* forms are clearly audible. Present French can correspond to English *I speak*, *I am speaking* or, with context, *I do speak*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous travaillons à Paris.
>
> *English: We work / are working in Paris.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Vous regardez la télévision ?
>
> *English: Are you watching television?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Ils arrivent demain.
>
> *English: They arrive / are arriving tomorrow.*

Spelling keeps pronunciation stable: *manger -> nous mangeons*; *commencer -> nous commençons*; *acheter -> j'achète*; *préférer -> je préfère*; *appeler -> j'appelle*.

> **Check yourself:** Conjugate *habiter* for "we" and *manger* for "we".

> **Answer:** *Nous habitons*; *nous mangeons*.

## 6. High-frequency irregular present verbs

> **Learning goal:** Memorise complete forms of verbs that organise everyday communication.

| Verb | Singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- |
| aller | vais, vas, va | allons, allez, vont |
| faire | fais, fais, fait | faisons, faites, font |
| venir | viens, viens, vient | venons, venez, viennent |
| prendre | prends, prends, prend | prenons, prenez, prennent |
| pouvoir | peux, peux, peut | pouvons, pouvez, peuvent |
| vouloir | veux, veux, veut | voulons, voulez, veulent |
| devoir | dois, dois, doit | devons, devez, doivent |
| savoir | sais, sais, sait | savons, savez, savent |

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je veux réserver une table.
>
> *English: I want to book a table.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Pouvez-vous répéter ?
>
> *English: Can you repeat?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Ils viennent avec nous.
>
> *English: They are coming with us.*

> **Usage note:** A conjugated modal verb is normally followed directly by an infinitive: *je peux venir, nous devons partir, elle veut apprendre*.

## 7. Basic negation

> **Learning goal:** Put a simple finite verb or an auxiliary inside *ne ... pas*.

> **Pattern:** **subject + ne/n' + conjugated verb + pas + complement**. Before a vowel or mute *h*, *ne* becomes *n'*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je ne comprends pas.
>
> *English: I do not understand.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle n'habite pas ici.
>
> *English: She does not live here.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous ne sommes pas prêts.
>
> *English: We are not ready.*

After negation, indefinite and partitive articles usually become **de/d'**: *J'ai une voiture -> Je n'ai pas de voiture*; *Il boit du lait -> Il ne boit pas de lait*. The definite article stays: *Je n'aime pas le lait*.

> **Watch out:** Everyday speech often drops *ne*, but formal and standard written French retains it. Learn *Je ne sais pas* as the complete structure.

## 8. Three ways to ask a yes/no question

> **Learning goal:** Recognise and produce intonation, *est-ce que* and inversion questions.

- **Intonation**: keep statement order and raise the voice: *Tu viens ?* Common in speech.
- **Est-ce que**: place it before a statement: *Est-ce que tu viens ?* Neutral and widely useful.
- **Inversion**: invert verb and subject pronoun: *Viens-tu ?* More formal or written. Insert *-t-* between two vowel sounds: *A-t-elle faim ?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Vous travaillez demain ?
>
> *English: Are you working tomorrow?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Est-ce qu'il parle anglais ?
>
> *English: Does he speak English?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Avez-vous une réservation ?
>
> *English: Do you have a booking?*

> **Watch out:** Do not combine *est-ce que* and inversion: use *Est-ce que vous venez ?* or *Venez-vous ?*, not both.

## 9. Information questions

> **Learning goal:** Ask for a person, thing, place, time, reason, manner or quantity.

Useful question words include *qui* (who), *que/qu'est-ce que* (what), *où* (where), *quand* (when), *pourquoi* (why), *comment* (how), and *combien (de)* (how many/much).

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Où est la banque ?
>
> *English: Where is the bank?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Qu'est-ce que vous cherchez ?
>
> *English: What are you looking for?*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Combien de billets voulez-vous ?
>
> *English: How many tickets do you want?*

When *qui* is the subject, it is followed directly by the verb: *Qui arrive ?* When asking about a selected noun, use *quel/quelle/quels/quelles*: *Quelle heure est-il ?*

> **Check yourself:** Ask informally: "Why are you leaving?"

> **Answer:** *Pourquoi tu pars ?* A neutral version is *Pourquoi est-ce que tu pars ?*

## 10. Adjective agreement and position

> **Learning goal:** Make regular adjectives agree and place common adjectives naturally.

Usually add **-e** for feminine and **-s** for plural: *petit, petite, petits, petites*. If the masculine form already ends in mute *-e*, the feminine is often unchanged: *jeune -> jeune*. Frequent changes include *-f -> -ve* (*sportif/sportive*), *-x -> -se* (*heureux/heureuse*), and doubled consonants (*bon/bonne*).

Most descriptive adjectives follow the noun. Common short adjectives of beauty, age, goodness and size often precede it: *un beau village, une jeune femme, un bon repas, une petite voiture*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** C'est une ville moderne.
>
> *English: It is a modern city.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle porte une jolie robe bleue.
>
> *English: She is wearing a pretty blue dress.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Mes voisins sont gentils.
>
> *English: My neighbours are kind.*

> **Watch out:** Some meanings change with position: *un ancien professeur* means a former teacher; *un meuble ancien* means an antique piece of furniture.

## 11. Possessive determiners

> **Learning goal:** Match the possessive form to the possessed noun, not to the possessor's gender.

| Owner | Masculine singular | Feminine singular | Plural |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| my | mon | ma | mes |
| your (tu) | ton | ta | tes |
| his/her/its | son | sa | ses |
| our | notre | notre | nos |
| your (vous) | votre | votre | vos |
| their | leur | leur | leurs |

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Marie cherche son frère et sa soeur.
>
> *English: Marie is looking for her brother and her sister.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous aimons notre quartier.
>
> *English: We like our neighbourhood.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Ils parlent à leurs parents.
>
> *English: They speak to their parents.*

Before a feminine noun beginning with a vowel or mute *h*, use *mon/ton/son* for sound: *mon amie, ton école, son histoire*. The noun remains feminine.

## 12. Demonstrative and interrogative determiners

> **Learning goal:** Point out a noun with *ce* and select a noun with *quel*.

Demonstratives are *ce* (masculine), *cet* (masculine before vowel/mute *h*), *cette* (feminine), and *ces* (plural). Add *-ci* or *-là* to contrast: *ce livre-ci, ce livre-là*.

Interrogatives are *quel, quelle, quels, quelles* and agree with the noun.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Cet appartement est lumineux.
>
> *English: This apartment is bright.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je préfère ces chaussures-là.
>
> *English: I prefer those shoes.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Quelle langue parlez-vous ?
>
> *English: Which/what language do you speak?*

## 13. C'est, ce sont, il/elle est and il y a

> **Learning goal:** Introduce, identify, describe and state existence.

- Use *c'est/ce sont* before a noun with a determiner, a proper name or a stressed pronoun: *C'est ma soeur. Ce sont eux.*
- Use *il/elle est* before an adjective or an unmodified profession/nationality/religion noun: *Elle est gentille. Il est médecin.*
- Use *il y a* for **there is/there are**; it does not change for number.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** C'est un excellent médecin. Il est très patient.
>
> *English: He is an excellent doctor. He is very patient.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il y a deux cafés dans la rue.
>
> *English: There are two cafés in the street.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Ce sont mes collègues.
>
> *English: These/They are my colleagues.*

> **Watch out:** With an article and modified profession, use *c'est*: *C'est une architecte talentueuse.* Without an article in a simple classification, use *elle est architecte*.

## 14. À, de and place expressions

> **Learning goal:** Express destination, location and origin with the common country and city patterns.

- Cities: *à Paris*, *de Paris*.
- Most feminine countries and vowel-initial masculine countries: *en France, en Iran; de France, d'Iran*.
- Most masculine countries: *au Canada; du Canada*.
- Plural countries: *aux États-Unis; des États-Unis*.
- People/ordinary places often use *chez*: *chez Paul, chez le médecin, chez moi*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle habite en Espagne mais elle vient du Portugal.
>
> *English: She lives in Spain but comes from Portugal.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous allons au musée.
>
> *English: We are going to the museum.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je dîne chez mes parents.
>
> *English: I am having dinner at my parents' home.*

> **Usage note:** The contractions are *à + le = au*, *à + les = aux*, *de + le = du*, and *de + les = des*. There is no contraction with *la* or *l'*.

## 15. Partitive articles and quantities

> **Learning goal:** Refer to an unspecified amount of food, drink or material.

Use *du* (masculine), *de la* (feminine), *de l'* (before vowel/mute *h*) and *des* (plural). After a quantity expression, use **de/d'**: *beaucoup de, un kilo de, une bouteille d', assez de*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je voudrais du pain et de la soupe.
>
> *English: I would like some bread and some soup.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle boit de l'eau.
>
> *English: She drinks some water.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous achetons trois kilos de pommes.
>
> *English: We are buying three kilos of apples.*

> **Watch out:** Compare category and portion: *J'aime le fromage* (cheese in general) versus *Je mange du fromage* (an unspecified amount now).

## 16. The imperative

> **Learning goal:** Give a direct instruction or suggestion to *tu, nous* or *vous*.

The imperative normally uses the present forms without subject pronouns: *parle, parlons, parlez*. Regular *-er* verbs and *aller* drop the written *-s* in the *tu* form: *Parle ! Va !* The main irregular forms are *sois/soyons/soyez*, *aie/ayons/ayez*, and *sache/sachons/sachez*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Écoutez attentivement.
>
> *English: Listen carefully.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Allons au marché.
>
> *English: Let's go to the market.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Ne ferme pas la porte.
>
> *English: Do not close the door.*

With an affirmative imperative, object pronouns follow the verb with hyphens: *Regardez-le.* In a negative command, they return before the verb: *Ne le regardez pas.*

## 17. Immediate time: aller + infinitive and venir de + infinitive

> **Learning goal:** Express a near future and a very recent past without building a new tense.

> **Pattern:** **aller (present) + infinitive** = be going to do; **venir (present) + de/d' + infinitive** = have just done.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je vais appeler Léa.
>
> *English: I am going to call Léa.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous allons partir dans cinq minutes.
>
> *English: We are going to leave in five minutes.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il vient d'arriver.
>
> *English: He has just arrived.*

> **Watch out:** Only the first verb is conjugated: *nous allons manger*, not *nous allons mangeons*.

## A1 mastery checklist

- I can use articles and basic agreement without translating word for word from English.
- I can conjugate *être, avoir, aller, faire* and regular *-er* verbs in the present.
- I can make a sentence negative and ask a question in at least two ways.
- I can describe a person or place with agreeing adjectives.
- I can express possession, location, quantities, commands and immediate plans.
- I can write a connected paragraph of 6-8 simple sentences about myself or my day.

> **Check yourself:** Write: "My friends are going to eat at my house. There is a good restaurant nearby, but we do not have much money."

> **Answer:** One natural answer is: *Mes amis vont manger chez moi. Il y a un bon restaurant près d'ici, mais nous n'avons pas beaucoup d'argent.*
