# C1: precision, cohesion and register

> *C1 is less about collecting new endings and more about selecting a viewpoint. An advanced user can foreground or background responsibility, distinguish event from result, compress clauses without obscuring reference, and sustain an appropriate register across long discourse.*

## 1. Tense as viewpoint and aspect

> **Learning goal:** Choose tense according to how the speaker frames an event, not merely when it occurred.

The imperfect opens an internal view of a past situation; the passé composé packages it as bounded; the present can make a past scene vivid; the future can signal prediction, command, concession or probability.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il a vécu dix ans à Marseille.
>
> *English: He lived in Marseille for ten years. (Completed block.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il vivait à Marseille depuis dix ans lorsqu'il a déménagé.
>
> *English: He had been living in Marseille for ten years when he moved. (Situation in progress at a past point.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** En 1789, la Révolution éclate.
>
> *English: In 1789, the Revolution breaks out. (Historical present.)*

The imperfect can also soften, suggest, or mark an unrealised plan: *Je voulais vous demander un service*; *Un pas de plus et il tombait*; *Je partais demain, mais le vol a été annulé*.

## 2. Modal nuance: indicative, subjunctive and conditional

> **Learning goal:** Interpret mood as a stance toward the proposition.

The indicative presents information inside the speaker's asserted world. The subjunctive embeds it under evaluation, will, purpose, non-identification or another non-assertive frame. The conditional creates distance: hypothesis, future-in-the-past, politeness, conjecture or evidential reservation.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il me semble qu'il a compris.
>
> *English: It seems to me that he understood. (The speaker presents a personal impression as likely information.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il semble qu'il ait compris.
>
> *English: It seems that he understood. (The conclusion is kept under appearance/evaluation.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il aurait compris.
>
> *English: He is said to have understood / He would have understood. (Meaning depends on context.)*

> **Usage note:** With expressions such as *il semble que, il paraît que,* and *il est probable que*, actual mood choice varies with register and intended commitment. Analyse the stance rather than memorising an inflexible label.

## 3. Mixed and implicit conditions

> **Learning goal:** Handle conditions whose time frames do not match or whose *si* clause is omitted.

Mixed conditions connect a past cause to a present result or a present state to a past result.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Si j'avais accepté ce poste, je vivrais à Montréal aujourd'hui.
>
> *English: If I had accepted that job, I would be living in Montreal today.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Si elle était plus prudente, elle n'aurait pas pris ce risque hier.
>
> *English: If she were more careful, she would not have taken that risk yesterday.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Sans votre aide, nous aurions échoué.
>
> *English: Without your help, we would have failed.*

Conditions can be encoded by *à condition de/que, à moins de/que, en cas de, faute de, moyennant, pour peu que*, an imperative, or simple juxtaposition: *Encore un effort et vous réussirez.*

## 4. Advanced reported discourse and free indirect style

> **Learning goal:** Move among direct, indirect and free indirect representation while keeping reference coherent.

Direct speech preserves the speaker's original words. Indirect speech subordinates them to a reporting verb. **Free indirect discourse** removes the reporting frame while retaining shifted person/tense and often the character's exclamations or questions.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle pensa : « Je partirai demain. »
>
> *English: She thought: "I will leave tomorrow."*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle pensa qu'elle partirait le lendemain.
>
> *English: She thought that she would leave the next day.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle partirait le lendemain. À quoi bon attendre davantage ?
>
> *English: She would leave the next day. What was the point of waiting any longer? (Free indirect style.)*

> **Watch out:** Long reports fail when *il/elle*, tense anchors or time expressions can refer to more than one source. Repeat a noun or reporting verb when needed; elegant cohesion is clearer, not merely shorter.

## 5. Infinitive clauses and subject control

> **Learning goal:** Identify who performs an infinitive and choose a structure that makes that subject recoverable.

With control verbs, the infinitive's understood subject comes from the main clause: *Paul promet de venir* (Paul will come); *Paul demande à Léa de venir* (Léa will come). With perception verbs, the agent may be a noun or object pronoun: *J'entends les enfants chanter; je les entends chanter*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le directeur autorise les employés à télétravailler.
>
> *English: The manager authorises employees to work remotely.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** On lui a conseillé de refuser.
>
> *English: He/She was advised to refuse.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je croyais avoir fermé la porte.
>
> *English: I thought I had closed the door.*

> **Usage note:** The past infinitive (*avoir/être + past participle*) marks anteriority with the same understood subject: *Après être partie, elle a appelé; il regrette d'avoir menti*.

## 6. Causative and perception constructions

> **Learning goal:** Control object relations when *faire, laisser, voir, entendre* or *sentir* governs an infinitive.

With *faire*, the causee is direct if the infinitive has no direct object, but commonly introduced by *à* if the infinitive already has one: *Je fais travailler Paul; je fais lire le rapport à Paul*. Pronoun patterns can be dense: *Je le lui fais lire* = I make him/her read it.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Elle a fait venir un technicien.
>
> *English: She had a technician come.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Je l'ai entendu chanter.
>
> *English: I heard him/her sing.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les chansons que j'ai entendu chanter étaient anciennes.
>
> *English: The songs that I heard being sung were old.*

> **Watch out:** Past-participle agreement before an infinitive can depend on whether the preceding direct object performs the infinitive. Traditional rule: *les chanteuses que j'ai entendues chanter* (the singers sing) but *les chansons que j'ai entendu chanter* (the songs are sung). In difficult prose, recast if the distinction would distract.

## 7. Participial, absolute and verbless clauses

> **Learning goal:** Compress information in formal writing while maintaining logical attachment.

- Present/compound participle: *les mesures visant à réduire; les mesures ayant échoué*.
- Absolute clause with its own subject: *La réunion terminée, chacun repartit*.
- Verbless supplement: *Une fois seul, il relut la lettre*; *Bien que très coûteux, le projet fut accepté*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les données étant incomplètes, aucune conclusion définitive n'a été tirée.
>
> *English: Since the data were incomplete, no definitive conclusion was drawn.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le contrat signé, les travaux pourront commencer.
>
> *English: Once the contract is signed, work can begin.*

> **Watch out:** Participial compression can hide cause, time, concession or condition. Add an explicit connector when the intended relationship is not immediately recoverable.

## 8. Information structure: theme, focus and rhythm

> **Learning goal:** Organise old and new information deliberately.

Unmarked French often moves from a known topic toward new information. Dislocation establishes a topic; clefts create contrastive focus; passive or pronominal structures suppress an agent; presentative structures introduce a new referent.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Quant à la seconde proposition, elle mérite un examen plus approfondi.
>
> *English: As for the second proposal, it deserves closer examination.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Ce qui pose problème, c'est l'absence de contrôle.
>
> *English: What causes a problem is the lack of oversight.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il existe néanmoins une autre solution.
>
> *English: There is nevertheless another solution.*

Avoid stacking several focus devices unless a marked rhetorical effect is intended: *Ce que c'est que je veux...* is conversational and unsuitable in most formal prose.

## 9. Advanced inversion and fronting

> **Learning goal:** Recognise and use inversion after restrictive or connective openings in formal prose.

Stylistic inversion is common after *à peine, aussi, sans doute, peut-être, du moins, encore, ainsi* and in literary narration. Some openings trigger a linking *que*: *Peut-être viendra-t-il* or *Peut-être qu'il viendra*; *Sans doute a-t-elle oublié*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** À peine était-il arrivé que le téléphone sonna.
>
> *English: Hardly had he arrived when the telephone rang.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Aussi convient-il de distinguer les deux cas.
>
> *English: It is therefore appropriate to distinguish the two cases.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Rares sont les études qui abordent ce point.
>
> *English: Few are the studies that address this point.*

> **Usage note:** Inversion can sound elegant in an essay and affected in conversation. At C1, control includes knowing when not to use it.

## 10. Cohesion: pronoun and demonstrative reference

> **Learning goal:** Maintain a clear chain of reference across sentences and paragraphs.

Demonstrative pronouns *celui/celle/ceux/celles* require a complement or contrast: *celui de Paul; ceux qui restent; celle-ci*. Neutral *ceci/cela/ça* can summarise a proposition; *ce dernier/cette dernière* identifies the last-mentioned noun but becomes awkward in long chains.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Deux solutions ont été proposées. La première est rapide; la seconde, moins coûteuse.
>
> *English: Two solutions were proposed. The first is quick; the second less expensive.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Le comité a reporté le vote. Cette décision a surpris les participants.
>
> *English: The committee postponed the vote. This decision surprised participants.*

> **Watch out:** Avoid a vague *ce qui/cela* after several possible antecedents. Name the proposition: *Ce report*, *Cette absence de données*, *Le fait qu'il ait refusé*.

## 11. Nominal style and agent management

> **Learning goal:** Use nominalisation to create abstraction while keeping actions and responsibility visible.

Formal prose often creates a thematic chain through related nouns: *décider -> décision; mettre en oeuvre -> mise en oeuvre; évaluer -> évaluation*. Agents can appear with *par*, *de la part de*, a possessive or a noun complement.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Après que le conseil a approuvé le plan, l'équipe l'a mis en oeuvre.
>
> *English: After the board approved the plan, the team implemented it.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** L'approbation du plan par le conseil a permis sa mise en oeuvre par l'équipe.
>
> *English: The board's approval of the plan enabled its implementation by the team.*

> **Watch out:** The nominal version is denser but can obscure who did what. In legal, technical and institutional prose, restoring a finite verb often improves accountability.

## 12. Logical connectors and scope

> **Learning goal:** Choose a connector whose logic, syntax and register match the intended relation.

| Function | Neutral | Formal/precise alternatives |
| --- | --- | --- |
| addition | aussi, de plus | en outre, qui plus est, de surcroît |
| contrast | mais, pourtant | toutefois, néanmoins, en revanche |
| cause | parce que, puisque | du fait que, étant donné que, dans la mesure où |
| consequence | donc, alors | par conséquent, dès lors, si bien que |
| concession | même si, bien que | certes ... mais, quand bien même, en dépit de |
| reformulation | c'est-à-dire | autrement dit, en d'autres termes |

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Certes, la méthode est rapide; elle demeure néanmoins imprécise.
>
> *English: Admittedly, the method is quick; it nevertheless remains imprecise.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Dans la mesure où les données sont provisoires, ces résultats doivent être interprétés avec prudence.
>
> *English: Insofar as the data are provisional, these results must be interpreted cautiously.*

> **Usage note:** Connectors have scope. A sentence-initial *cependant* typically relates whole propositions, while *mais* can contrast words, phrases or clauses. Punctuation should reveal that scope.

## 13. Punctuation as grammar

> **Learning goal:** Use punctuation to show clause boundaries, attachment and information hierarchy.

French normally places a space before two-part punctuation marks in professional typography (*; : ? !*) and uses guillemets (« ... »), though exact spacing depends on publishing environment. Grammar-wise, commas separate supplements and framing elements but should not split a subject from its verb.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Mon frère, qui vit à Lille, viendra demain.
>
> *English: My brother, who lives in Lille, will come tomorrow. (The relative clause is supplementary.)*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Les étudiants qui ont terminé peuvent partir.
>
> *English: The students who have finished may leave. (The clause identifies which students.)*

A semicolon can coordinate closely related independent clauses; a colon announces explanation, consequence, quotation or elaboration. Parentheses weaken attachment; dashes heighten interruption or emphasis.

## 14. Register and grammatical alternatives

> **Learning goal:** Express the same proposition naturally in conversational, neutral and formal styles.

| Function | Conversational | Neutral | Formal/literary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| question | *Tu viens quand ?* | *Quand est-ce que tu viens ?* | *Quand viendras-tu ?* |
| negative | *J'ai pas compris.* | *Je n'ai pas compris.* | *Je n'en ai nullement saisi la portée.* |
| we | *On part.* | *Nous partons.* | *Nous nous retirerons.* |
| cause | *vu que* | *parce que* | *étant donné que / puisque* |

Register is not a ladder from bad to good. Conversational grammar can be the most natural choice in dialogue, while formal grammar may be required in a report. The error is an uncontrolled mismatch: dense inversion beside casual fragments, or dropped *ne* in a formal application.

## 15. Lexical grammar and valency

> **Learning goal:** Treat vocabulary as constructions with complements, not as isolated translations.

Advanced accuracy depends on frames: *aboutir à un résultat; bénéficier de quelque chose; pallier quelque chose* (traditionally direct); *remédier à quelque chose; se conformer à; mettre quelqu'un en garde contre; tenir quelqu'un au courant de*.

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Cette mesure vise à réduire les inégalités.
>
> *English: This measure aims to reduce inequalities.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Il s'est abstenu de commenter la décision.
>
> *English: He refrained from commenting on the decision.*

> **Example**
>
> **French:** Nous veillerons à ce que la procédure soit respectée.
>
> *English: We will ensure that the procedure is followed.*

> **Usage note:** A dictionary entry should be learned with its construction, typical subject/object and register. This is where grammar and collocation meet.

## C1 mastery checklist

- I can shift viewpoint using tense and mood without changing the factual timeline accidentally.
- I can express mixed and implicit conditions and sustain complex reported discourse.
- I can compress clauses while keeping subjects and logical relations unambiguous.
- I can control topic, focus, reference chains, inversion and nominalisation.
- I can maintain a stable conversational, neutral, academic or professional register.
- I can edit a 400-word text for cohesion, information flow and grammatical precision.

> **Check yourself:** Improve the vague chain: *Le comité a rejeté le rapport après avoir interrogé les auteurs. Cela a surpris les observateurs, qui l'ont critiqué.*

> **Answer:** A clear revision is: *Après avoir interrogé les auteurs, le comité a rejeté leur rapport. Ce rejet a surpris les observateurs, qui ont critiqué la décision du comité.* The revised nouns remove uncertainty about what *cela* and *l'* refer to.
