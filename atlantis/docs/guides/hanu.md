## Introduction

[Image 6946495 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6946495)

Hanu the Trickster is the pinnacle support hero. His repertoire of cards allow
for copious amounts of shenanigans, bamboozling, knavery, and outright mischief.
If you like to control the board state whilst simultaneously giggling as you
muck about with unit positions and initiative order, then this hero just might
be the one for you. As a support hero, Hanu's job is to deny his opponents the
opportunity to take minions, initiate fights using his insane initiative stat,
and finally, to set his teammates up for powerhouse turns so that they can carry
you to victory. And all of this is not to mention that you're always welcome to
toss the occasional rock, too.

### The Basics

**Gold: Fight and Flight**

*If your discard is empty, choose one —*

*• An enemy hero adjacent to you discards a card, if able. You then move 2
spaces in a straight line, if able.*

*• Defeat a non-heavy minion adjacent to you.*

Fight and Flight, also known as the "Boop and Scoot" is one of the fastest cards
in the game. It is fantastic for taking minions at lightning speed, and for
initiating fights while getting out of dodge. It comes with two huge caveats,
however. 1) It cannot take heavy minions, so keep that in mind when racing for
the push, and 2) It gets turned off by having a card in the discard pile. Unlike
many of the other heroes who benefit from holding their Red and Gold attack
cards until the end of the round, Hanu breaks this trope slightly by often
favoring playing Gold earlier, so as to avoid getting stuck with a 13-ini
movement card later in the round in case you get hit with a discard. With that
being said, Fight and Flight has a whopping 3 movement on it, which is by far
one of the greatest movements on a fast escape card in the game (Shut up Misa,
your stinking Gold is only 9 initiative!).

The fact that Hanu's Gold card can neither kill heavy minions, nor kill other
heroes further solidifies his role as a support hero. Some key uses for the Gold
card are:

- Setting a teammate up for taking the heavy minion by defeating the penultimate
  minion at lightning speed. In far too many games I have seen two heroes lining
  up for a one-two combo to secure the push, only to realize their initiative
  values don't work out and the person who planned to take the heavy goes before
  the person who planned to remove the final melee minion. Hanu never needs to
  worry about that.

- Setting a teammate up for a kill, either by double-tapping on turn 4 (again,
  due to his insane initiative Hanu will likely force a discard earlier in turn
  order so that your teammate can card-kill an opponent), or by forcing a
  discard on any adjacent hero and scooting away at any point in the round.
  Unlike many other adjacent "pseudo-attacks", Hanu doesn't have to worry about
  immediate counterattack from melee or short-range attacks because he can scoot
  away immediately after forcing a discard.

**Silver: Hurry Up!**

*If your discard is empty, set the printed Initiative value of an unresolved
card of a hero in range to 11, until it is resolved.*

*This turn: If that hero performs a movement action, that hero must move in a
straight line and the full distance, if able.*

Hurry Up! is the lynchpin of Hanu's kit. It has enormous value both when used on
a friendly hero, or an enemy. It has fantastic base range as well (4) so you can
often be flexible with targeting after cards are revealed and you have a better
understanding of the board state. As with his Gold card, Hanu needs to be
mindful of discards because they turn his Silver into a complete dud. Some of
the best uses for Hurry Up! are:

•Boosting a friendly hero's Red attack card to initiative 11 (+ their own
initiative items!). By the time you get Hanu to the table, you've likely seen
all of the core heroes in action at least once. Everyone knows that Brogan's
charge is scary, but at least it's slow and you can step out of the way, right?
Oh, wait -- unless Hanu decides to speed it up, and now you're in two pieces
because you were sure your Blue card was fast enough to get out of the way.

•Boosting a friendly hero's initiative so they can escape an attack with a
slower card (with the caveat being they need to move the full distance, and in a
straight line).

•Boosting an enemy hero's initiative so that they need to move first, allowing a
now slower teammate to follow up and threaten an attack.

•Boosting an enemy hero's movement such that they cannot move into the exact
spot they wanted. Remember that boosting movement requires the hero to move the
full distance, and in a straight line. Doing this to an enemy hero who wanted to
use Red for movement can often throw a monkey wrench into their plans.

### The Branches

#### Green

If you take a quick look at Hanu's hero board, you'd quickly notice that his
Movement and Initiative values are cranked to the max, but his Attack and
Defense values are bad. Like, really bad. As low as they get, bad. But that's
okay, because who needs defense numbers when you can just go home for a snack
before getting back into the action? Both Hanu's Green branches offer huge
movement (3+) at an incredibly low initiative (remember, this is good for Green
cards. You want to use them to position last in a turn to either secure a kill
or safely escape). Furthermore, as long as you can avoid pesky discards your
Primary Green can help you cheat death (and fluster your opponents, yay!) for
basically the entire game. Speaking of Primary Green, let's take a look at that
now.

**Primary Green: Killing Joke**

*If you have no cards in the discard, block the attack and place yourself on a
spawn point in your throne zone, if able. If you do, retrieve this card. Each
enemy hero gains 1 coin.*

Hanu's Primary Green card is a Defense primary action that blocks an attack and
sends you back to your throne zone. Not only do you not lose any life counters
(ugh!), nor do you resolve your unresolved card (double ugh!), but your
opponents only collect 1 coin each instead of a Scrooge McDuck swimming pool's
worth, AND Hanu immediately heals himself. Do not pass GO, do not collect $200,
silly enemies! As long as this card is in hand and your discard is empty, Hanu
is unkillable by regular attacks. Take that, defense stat! We don't need you
anyways. Just be weary of early discards because playing with Primary Green
means that 3/5 of your cards get shut down when you're wounded.

**Alternate Green: See Nothing**

*Move up to 1 space. Swap with an enemy hero in radius. Move up to 1 space.*

The Alt Green branch is a fantastic low initiative control card, but comes with
the expense of ditching your Primary defense. I'm likely to pick this branch
either defensively in response to long range/low damage heroes (Silverarrow,
Dodger, etc.), or against heroes who can force a myriad of discards (Whisper,
Cutter, etc.) because it cannot be turned off the same way Primary Green can. I
will also sometimes pick this branch offensively to more easily support my
beefcake teammate (I'm looking at you, Brogan) by using the low initiative swap
to position my enemies into danger immediately before I use Hurry Up! on my pal
so that he can swiftly smash them. This card is also useful to pull enemy heroes
away from minions they planned to take, and is especially nasty in those
critical rounds when the enemy team is desperately trying to defend their own
beach minions.

#### Blue

Hanu's Blue branches focus primarily on two of his key gameplay roles - denial
and support. Which branch you choose will largely be matchup dependent, as both
options are incredibly useful. Unlike Brogan, whose Blue card text I've seen
activate less times than I've got fingers on a hand, Hanu will benefit from the
text on his Blue cards many times throughout the average game.

**Primary Blue: Safe Travels**

*Swap with an enemy unit in radius. This turn: You and that unit are immune to
all other actions. End of turn: Swap with that unit, regardless of radius, if
able.*

The Primary Blue has a handful typical uses:

•Swap an enemy hero on a turn they played an attack card to force them to miss
their intended target. At base initiative 11, you're likely only catching Red
cards unless you've stacked initiative, so you have to keep in mind that they
will likely use their boots to put you into an awful position for the swap-back
at the end of the round. Depending on how minion combat is looking, this is
often a worthwhile trade.

•Swap an enemy hero on a turn they planned to reposition to take a minion/hero
in the future, rooting them in place and likely denying them at least 1 shot.
This will still get you into a bad position, but not nearly as bad as if they
moved with Red. Be mindful of swapping the enemy into a spot where they can fast
travel you waaaaay out of position. If their team works together to deny your
fast travel back, it can sometimes be worse for you than them.

•Swap an enemy hero on any particular turn, JUST to root them in place so that
your friendly teammate can approach and swing next turn, after the swap-back.

•Swap with an enemy hero to grant both heroes immunity. This not only saves you
from any other attacks or abilities for the rest of the turn, but granting an
opponent immunity can sometimes have very niche benefits to your team. An
example would be swapping with Misa on a turn she plays Swoop In. Normally Misa
would taunt an attack for the round, forcing you to shoot her instead of a
minion. Making her immune makes her un-targetable, thus allowing your teammate
to shoot whoever/whatever they want.

Tier 3 Primary Blue also happens to come with a +Range upgrade, which is really
beneficial for Hanu's Red and Silver cards. This allows him to support his team
from further away, and also allows him to safely farm from a distance while
avoiding discards and other forms of potential death.

**Alternate Blue: That Way!**

*A friendly hero in radius chooses a distance of 1, 2, or 3. You choose a
direction. You both move that number of spaces in the chosen direction, stopping
if an obstacle is reached.*

Alt Blue is most often used as offensive support. It is particularly beneficial
to heroes who require specific positioning to attack (Ignatia, Brogan, Swift,
etc.) because you can  move your teammate into position to shoot on a turn your
opponents thought they were safe and played something slow. It can also be used
defensively as an escape tool to get you and your friend out of dodge relatively
quickly, if needed. I find I rarely take the Alt Blue in most games, because
both the Tier 2 and Tier 3 cards come with +Attack, and that is not particularly
useful on a low-damage hero with a single attack card. The Primary Blue cards
come with +Defense and +Range at Tier 2 and 3 respectively, which are usually a
safer, more reliable choice.

#### Red

As mentioned above, your attack stat is fairly dismal. Don't expect to be
one-shotting heroes while playing as Hanu. But, don't be too dismayed, you can
still use your one and only attack to make meaningful contributions to the game
each round. Hanu's Red cards can loosely be broken down into one of two
categories. The Primary Red is for brawling/support, whereas the Alt Red is for
initiating and safe farming. One of the cool things about both of Hanu's Red
cards is that at Tier 3 they are both repeat attacks, so you will often get to
take a minion AND harass a hero, all at once!

**Primary Red: Trusted Sidekick**

*Choose one, or both, in any order —*

*• Target a unit adjacent to you.*

*• Target a hero in range, adjacent to your friendly hero.*

Primary Red comes in at 4-damage, 4-range, which is nothing to scoff at. There
is one teeny, tiny little asterisk next to that 4-range, however, and that's the
fact that you can only target heroes at range, AND they have to be adjacent to a
friendly hero. This branch pairs well with melee brawlers who typically aim to
be adjacent to enemies anyways, and is also a decent pick when you really need
an extra +Defense item. One cool thing about Hanu Primary Red is that at Tier 3
it is one of the few (if not the only?) Red attack card that can repeat twice on
the same hero, in the same turn, provided they are adjacent to you and a
friendly hero. This means that it is a guaranteed solo turn 4 kill if you act
before the enemy can step away.

**Alternate Red: Pile On**

*Target a unit in range. +1 Attack for each friendly unit adjacent to you. If
you target a hero, may repeat once on a different target.*

The Alt Red boasts a lowly 2-damage, 2-range. It is much more flexible in it's
targeting restrictions, which makes it safer and easier to farm with. It has a
neat +Attack text that sometimes scores you a kill because math is hard, but
with such low base attack I still don't really ever expect it to do anything
other than card kill a hero who otherwise cannot defend. I like that taking Tier
3 Alt Red gives you a +Boot item, which turns you into the most mobile hero in
the game (Yeah, that's right. Suck it Misa!), and it's also a repeat attack that
is relatively easy to pull off.

#### Ultimate: The Ultimate Trick

*You choose the action, and how it is performed for a hero you target with the
“Hurry Up!”.*

The Ultimate Trick is largely considered to be amongst the most powerful
Ultimates in the game. Nothing hurts worse than having your opponent take your
entire turn for you. "Oh, did you plan to take that minion? How about you
fast-travel back to your own throne instead? Okay byeeeee!"

Ultimates typically enter the game right before the final wave, and messing with
your opponent's tempo this badly usually seals the deal and clinches you a win.

### The Paths

**Monkey Business**

[Image 6946793 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6946793)

The goal with this build is to farm safely, win the mid-lane pushes, and act as
initiator for team kills as often as possible. In the early game, a nice goal to
have as Hanu is to take your two minions as quickly as possible, then look for
opportunities to deny your opponents with either Blue or Silver (Or both,
preferably). You often aim to keep Primary Green in hand to deter heroes from
wasting an attack on you, and if possible and safe to do so you can use it for 3
boots movement at the end of the round.

**At Tier 2**

For this build, taking Alt Red and Primary Green at Tier 2 are almost a given.
They each net you +1 initiative which is a huge boon for heroes like Hanu.
Depending on the matchup, I will mix up my Tier 2 Blue pick. If low-damage
heroes are in the game, such as Dodger or Silverarrow, I'll take Alt Blue to
give myself +1 Defense, allowing me to block their piddly attacks with Silver if
needed. That way I can choose whether or not to lose a bit of tempo by defending
(or not) with Green and going back to my throne. Otherwise I'll nab the +Attack
and hope that it makes a difference at some point later in the game.

**At Tier 3**

Tier 3 looks much the same as Tier 2. This time there really isn't much debate
as to which cards to pick. In most cases I'll grab Primary Blue as my first Tier
3 upgrade because that gets you a super useful +Range item, but if at the top of
the round I foresee opportunities to make Tier 3 Alt Red repeat worthwhile, I
might try to sneak that in first, before I upgrade range. Tier 3 Green is most
often my last Tier 3 upgrade, but if you're feeling heavy aggression from the
opposition it might really help deter them away from you.

By the time you get to level 7, you've got an initiative 16!! Gold card, an
initiative 13 range-3 repeat attack, and initiatives 15 and 14 disruption cards
with Silver and Blue respectively. You can play all of these cards while
simultaneously holding your Primary Green for defense. Timing your attacks and
denials is key to playing this Hanu build well.

**Short Round**

[Image 6946800 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6946800)

This is a brawling, hero-hunting, support build. The goal is to initiate as
often as you can with Gold early in the round, then use Blue and Green to
maneuver and jockey friendly and enemy heroes into position for an untimely
death. This build pairs best with teammates who don't mind being in the thick of
things, but also does okay with position-dependent heroes like Swift because you
can very easily line them up for a Sniper shot (Hang on lady, we going for a
ride!), while also semi-protecting them with your Primary Red (because if
someone tries to sit next to your buddy, you bonk them with 4 range and 7
attack). Furthermore, this build comes with +2 defense items, so you can take a
hit if needed and not fold like a wet napkin.

**At Tier 2**

I almost always take Tier 2 Primary Red as my first upgrade here. Not only does
it get you a defense item, but it also gives you an extra range on your Red
card, which is useful because team members are often a bit spread out after the
first round. This helps you stay relevant and supportive, while also allowing
you to keep a bit of distance and take minions if necessary. Both of the Tier 2
Alt Green and Alt Blue cards come with +Attack, so you can take either of them
at your leisure, depending on what seems relevant at the time. By the time
you're ready to start getting your Tier 3 cards you've got +1 Defense, and +2
Attack. At this point your foes have to start respecting your long-distance
relationship with your partner, lest they be lightly smote (smited? smitten?) by
an angry monkey.

**At Tier 3**

At Tier 3, your most valuable upgrade here is likely Primary Red. Not only does
it provide you with +Radius, which greatly benefits both your Blue and Green
cards, but it also bumps up your damage by 1, as well as enables the "choose
both" portion of your attack. From there you can select Blue or Green as you see
fit. I'd personally probably grab Blue first, as it's defense stat increases by
1, and also makes it easier to line up kills.
