# Wuk Hero Spotlight

- Hero: Wuk the Grovekeeper
- Author: Panetierre, on BoardGameGeek

## Introduction

With Wuk at the table, nobody needs to be reminded about the thousand pound
gorilla in the room. Everyone's watching to see where Wuk puts down his trees
and defines his turf. The big ape is slow and his attacks are tied to where
those trees are, but in exchange he has enormous attack values, titanic defense
values, and powerful minion pushing potential. His enemies don’t dare stand near
the trees, and his allies can take refuge among them.

A brawler’s job is to take up space, zoning enemy heroes out of important
territory on the board to gain tempo for the team. And nobody does it better
than Wuk.

### The Basics

**Silver: Mystic Saplings**

*Place up to 3 tree tokens in radius, not adjacent to a tree token. Heroes may
move through a space with a tree token. Do not remove the tree tokens at the end
of round.*

Your Silver is central to your kit. It’s possible for you to play without your
trees, but your biggest threats and support abilities will ask you to have a
tree in position. Getting the most of your tree placements is critical. Here are
a few types of good positions for Wuk’s trees:

- adjacent to enemy minions,

- on or adjacent to spaces enemy heroes want to go,

- adjacent to spaces friendly heroes want to go,

- and adjacent to Wuk or to spaces you want to go.

It takes a little practice to identify spaces for some of these categories, but
after a couple of rounds you’ll get a sense for it. And one last tip: Creating a
choke point can make it hard for enemies to avoid a tree, so try leaving a one
space gap between your tree and terrain in a high traffic area.

Unlike other tokens, your trees stay between rounds. That lets you get ahead
with some planning, giving you rounds where you won’t need to spend time playing
Silver. Using Silver every other round is a nice goal to aim for. There’s a
somewhat obscure rule that’s very relevant to you: when you go to place tokens
and there are no more of them in the supply, you decide how many you're going to
place, remove tokens of that type from the board until you have enough, and then
place all of them, one at a time. This means you can adjust the placement of a
tree by 1 space if you remove that tree first and then place it. You can also
leave a tree that’s outside your radius where it is, and place others in radius.

**Gold: Tree Slam**

*If you are adjacent to a tree token, target a unit in range; otherwise, target
a minion adjacent to you. After the attack: If the target was adjacent to you,
move it 1 space to a space adjacent to you, if able.*

Your Gold is the backbone of your tree threat. While you’re away from the trees
other heroes don’t have to mind you so much; while you stand next to a tree
everyone has to respect that Gold attacking for 4. Your attacks are more about
the threat than actually hitting heroes. Without initiative items or that range
item boosting its range to 2 you’re mostly forcing people to move and taking a
minion for your trouble. But there will be moments where you catch someone
without an escape plan, and this attack will cream them.

### The Branches

#### Green

**Primary Green: Monstrous Throw**

*Move up to 1 space. Push a unit or a token adjacent to you up to 5 spaces. If
it is pushed 2 or more spaces, an enemy hero adjacent to that unit or token
discards a card, if able.*

I won’t even pretend not to be biased; this Primary Green is my favorite card in
the game. As a discard skill it’s decently powerful, but the opportunity to
shape the board at the same time makes it terrific. When you throw a tree at
someone, you’re setting up your Gold and Red at the same time they’re getting a
discard. When you throw a minion, you can block escape routes, set up your ally
for a minion, or you can forget the discard part and just use it to toss your
friendly minion away to safety. And when you throw a hero, you can send your
friend to a target and wound that target at the same time, earning all of the
style points. 🤩

That “move up to 1 space” lets you pick from 3 lines to push an adjacent object,
and it also lets you slam something you aren’t adjacent to if it’s a space away.

**Alternate Green: Treetop Ride**

*Swap with a tree token in radius. You may then place a unit adjacent to that
tree token into a space adjacent to you.*

Wuk’s Alternate Green pairs excellent mobility with an interesting support
ability. Carrying off minions can have a similar impact to what we discussed
with the push from Primary Green, protecting your minions or setting up your
team to farm. That set up can come in handy with your Alternate Red and
Alternate Blue. Carrying off heroes has amazing combo potential, as you get to
place them in any of the 6 spaces around where you land. All that and your tree
teleports to where you were.

#### Blue

**Primary Blue: Abundance**

*Both you and a friendly hero in radius may retrieve a discarded card if either
of you are adjacent to a tree.*

😋🍌 One of the most flexible heal skills in the game. You already like being
adjacent to trees for your Gold, and your friends are already going to be safer
near your trees because of the threat from your Gold and your Primary Red. If
your team can meet either of those conditions, you get a lot of healing all at
once. Simple, straightforward, good skill. When you’re upgrading Blue, check in
with your team. Sometimes your team really needs this ability, other times
you’re free to go with the other option.

**Alternate Blue: Assert Dominance**

*This Round: Up to 2 enemy minions adjacent to you do not count towards the
minion total during minion battle, regardless of immunity.*

Yeah, flex on those minions! 💪💪

On paper it sounds like you can get the value of four minions in a round. In
practice the turn you have to spend activating this ability means you aren’t
likely to kill minions with two other cards and also get the full benefit of
this one. But your Alternate Blue still offers you a lot of flexibility in what
you’d like to do this round. Aggression against heroes often comes at a cost to
the minion battle, and this can make your life easier. Most importantly, at Tier
3 it affects the heavy minion, a major change to which minions you can knock out
of the end of round battle.

Unlike other wave push abilities, this skill leaves the enemy minions standing
(if they survive the minion battle) so you’ll still have to spend actions on
them in the future if you want them off the board. But in some situations
there’s a benefit to that: defeating a minion now is 2 coins and may level you
up this round, where waiting until you collect a pity coin and defeating that
minion next round gets you 3 coins total. Less power now in exchange for some
income can be worthwhile, particularly when it means you get to use your attacks
to menace heroes.

#### Red

**Primary Red: Nature’s Champion**

*Target a unit in range, adjacent to a tree token. May repeat once, on a diff
erent hero.*

Here it is, the main feature of your tree threat. Nobody wants to get hit by
your Primary Red’s base 6 attack, and that means nobody can linger in any space
in range 3 of you that’s next to a tree. So place your trees near your minions,
or key locations your allies like to stand in, and your enemies won’t be able to
afford to stay still to attack them.

If you catch someone with the attack, that’s awesome! But similar to your Gold,
with your poor initiative what you can expect to do is control space and force
heroes to abandon whatever plan they’d made and keep moving. As long as there’s
a minion next to a tree to take instead, that’s just fine for you..

**Alternate Red: Angry Stampede**

*If you move in a straight line: You may ignore obstacles; if you moved through
an enemy hero, that hero discards a card, or is defeated; defeat up to 2 minions
you moved through.*

This is a movement type Red card, unique among heroes. When you take Wuk’s
Alternate Red, you give up half of the package that makes your trees so
dangerous, and gain freedom and a minion wave push threat. Between the time
saved not playing Silver as often, moving when you play Red, and the option to
move through tokens and terrain, trample Wuk feels far more mobile than tree
guardian Wuk. Your hero aggression is toned down to a discard, but one that’s
more likely to actually land on a hero. However, someone can walk into the last
space you’d need to enter, or put a token there, and block your charge. You can
expect to see that move when you set up to defeat 2 minions, so keep that
movement item in mind; it improves your effective range and makes it much harder
to block you.

#### Ultimate

**March of Nature**

*Tree tokens in a battle zone count as melee minions immune to ranged attacks as
long as they are adjacent to you.*

Wow! Three extra minions ready to help take the minion battle every round, as
long as you can stick around and play Silver. Remember that you can’t place them
adjacent to each other, so you do need a little room to work with.

### The Paths

**Gorilla Warfare**

On this path you focus on the attack and defense items and lean into the
brawling role. Brawler Wuk is set up to control space like no other. Your epic
defense and powerful heal will keep you and your team up and fighting. This path
does ask you to anticipate the flow of the game, so you and your trees are
always in control of the most important areas of the board.

**At Tier 2**

Getting extra range on your Red is a big priority, as it helps you stay mobile
and reposition your trees less often. Blue and Green can come in either order,
depending on whether your team needs a heal available. Or you can take Green
first to bide time before committing down a build path.

The Green discard is nice aggression for your team that can keep your Red
threatening heroes, and the one initiative item helps your Gold find targets.
But the mobility gained from the swap Green and attack item make it a solid
upgrade, too. And if your enemies aren’t pushing you into those defense items,
you can take Alternate Red while you’re on Tier 2 to get another initiative
item.

**At Tier 3**

Upgrades on this tier are mostly a matter of fixing your problems. Red gets a
little stronger and comes with a radius item to help your Silver out and heal
from farther away. Blue's own defense gets a boost on its upgrade, and the
double heal is sometimes very welcome, all with an attack item. Green has a
defense item to secure your survivability and adds a cute hero throw option.

Like before, which Green is up to you. The overall mobility and the hero
carrying option can make all the difference in some games. And if your team
doesn’t need a heal, and your attack values are already high enough, Alternate
Blue and the range item can make a big difference to your Gold.

**Certified Organic Wrecking Ball**

This path for Wuk sets you up to leave the trees behind and take control of the
minion wave. You’re favoring initiative because you want your Alternate Red to
resolve as early as possible to avoid being blocked by someone’s movement
action. Being on only one attack makes the attack items less appealing, but if
you end up taking some your Gold will still appreciate it.

Even on this path your Silver can be a good play because your trees take up
space, enable your Gold attack to do more than take adjacent minions, and enable
your swap Green to do work as a mobility and team support skill. That said,
there’s a good chance you’ll be defending with your Silver; taking a defense
item or two can tune it to defend against relevant attacks.

**At Tier 2**

There’s no specific order to your upgrades on this tier. It’s usually best to
keep the Tier 1 Red while your trees are still in relevant positions and make
the switch to Alternate Red when it’ll let you skip planting trees in a round.
Green first makes a lot of sense; it’s the least commitment if you decide to go
down a different path, and throwing minions is a valuable tool. Blue is usually
a low priority, and which Blue you take is more about the matchup and whether
you want some better defenses or you want Gold to hit harder. Having a team heal
versus dropping the tree-based play entirely is another consideration.

We’re looking to pick up all of the initiative to make your Alternate Red as
reliable as it can be, but you could skip one of these two. Keep the ranged red
to be a tankier ape and more threatening through Tier 2, or take the swap green
to power up that Gold.

**At Tier 3**

Alternate Red is probably your most important upgrade here and generally comes
first. That income from a double farm will keep you leveling up, and the last
initiative item makes it more consistent. The movement item from Alternate Green
is a very big deal for both setting up and resolving your Red, so it typically
comes second. That puts Alternate Blue last, but keep an eye out for a chance to
take Alternate Blue early to close out a game.

If you did opt for Primary Red at Tier 2, then same as before you’ll likely want
to keep it until the upgrade to Alternate Red saves you a tree planting, or you
see an opportunity to set up for the double farm. And in some games, having the
heal Primary Blue is more important. Your team will let you know if you need to
switch to it.

## Follow-up by the author (2022-07-04)

People often ask about teamups, but I find it a difficult thing to try and write
about. Partly because it really is true that any two heroes can make a plan in
2v2, and in larger teams there's no doubt you'll find awesome synergies with
teammates. And partly because there's so much to explore, and so much fun in the
exploring. Still, I can highlight a few things.

Most obvious is a team of two low initiative, low movement brawling heroes would
be missing some opportunities. One of you can take all of the initiative items
to help, but varying your team's natural abilities is a good idea.

Wuk is incredibly threatening, but has a difficult time targeting heroes. Any
time someone wishes their target would sit still and take a hit, Bain's Silver
card, Get Over Here!, immediately comes to mind. As long as you're mindful about
how you place trees, or Bain upgrades to mitigate his issues working around
obstacles, the Wuk-Bain team can get a lot done. If you both take discard skills
and Bain goes for the Alternate Red, you can threaten both win conditions well.

Wuk has the worst initiative stat a hero can have, and his abilities are
balanced around the opportunity enemy heroes get to react to them. That's the
sort of thing that makes Hanu's ears perk up. His Silver card, Hurry Up!, is the
perfect match for slow heroes, setting any card to a Gold-like initiative 11.
And the little monkey can make use of his potent swap Alternate Green to set you
up, since he can stay safer among your trees.

Speaking of, the last thing I'll point out is Wuk's trees are the perfect place
for a long-range ally to set up. Heroes like Silverarrow and Swift can get a lot
done when they aren't being harassed by enemies, and if they lure enemies into
your turf, all the better.

I've always wanted to try the Wuk-Swift team, aiming to combo your Treetop Ride
Alternate Green with Swift's equal initiative Reload! Gold skill. It's hard to
imagine there's a space on the board Swift couldn't attack after you carry her
in a swap, if the trees are placed out of straight lines of each other. Of
course, that's a move that has to wait until you get to Tier 3.

As for bad matchups, Wuk's a very resilient hero that's difficult to bring down
by a large attack or, with his quick and easy heal, to run out of cards for a
kill. Instead you need to watch for heroes that can hold you still, or move
other units around to avoid your trees. Heroes like Xargatha, with her Silver
drawing your friends out and Primary Blue preventing movement actions, and
Arien, with his Gold moving another unit or his Alternate Blue reducing movement
to 1 space, those can be a real pain.

Long-range heroes like Silverarrow can work from the sides of the board, away
from your trees, and you don't have many options for chasing them down. A tree
off to the side and your ranged Primary Red can help narrow your enemy's
options, but working out roles with your teammate and developing a plan will
help you both focus.

Some heroes will rely on a block card to shrug off your attack. Your team can
plan to follow up on a card kill if that happens, or you can engage with discard
skills or your Alternate Red to disable the block option later in the round.
Just remember that other than a card kill with Alternate Red, securing the kill
isn't usually your role. Take on the minion fight and leave your friends to make
the final blows.
