## Introduction

This is a guide for players that have played Guards of Atlantis II a few times
and want an introduction to Garrus before playing him. If you’re a first-time
player, this might be a little too much, I’ll try to put something more
beginner-friendly out if people like this.

Garrus is an Aggressive Melee Brawler with access to Disruption, Threat, and
Slow options. If you love playing slow, doing heavy damage, or making the
enemy’s life difficult then Garrus is on the short list of characters you need
to try.

## The Cards

As normal we’ll start with the basics.

### Gold

This card has the highest attack value on a gold card (4), but the lowest
initiative for a Gold Attack. You can use your silver card to discard a card and
activate this effect, or wait for the enemy heroes to hit you. Playing this card
early in a round is an excellent way to initiate fights. It will often cause an
enemy to discard a blue or green card, which can give your teammates
opportunities to finish the target later on.

### Silver

Silver has the best INI in the game. You can use this card to activate effects
on your gold and primary red cards, slow down high movement heroes like Hanu or
Trinkets, or even play it to keep your blue card in hand for the round, making
you extremely tanky. This card alone makes Garrus a strong consideration for
kill victories against high movement heroes.

### Green

Before we jump in, note the initiative on this branch? It's pretty bad. This
means you can’t chase heroes that also play green. Finding a way to play green
on turns the enemies don’t is a key component of good Garrus play.

### Primary Green

Self-heals are the cornerstone of a good brawler, and this card is an EXCELLENT
self-heal. You can even retrieve cards you discarded yourself with silver if you
need to be extra tanky. Use this the turn you think the enemy will attack you
for best effect.

### Alternate Green

This is Garrus’ area denial option, and its effect is premium. In most cases
you’re happy if they run, happy if they discard, and happy if they move next to
you. Here Garrus’ fast green initiative is turned into an advantage, allowing
you to target heroes who play slow before they can act. For best effect, chuck a
spear at someone in the first two turns of the round, ideally the same turn they
play silver, take a minion, or play green. Marking enemy heroes early can allow
your teammates to grab the kill later in a round.

### Blue

A general note first that for tanky characters like Garrus, holding your blue
card is the best way to stay tanky all round. Blue cards should be played
rarely, and only after careful consideration.

### Primary Blue

Both of Garrus’ blues are minion denial options, but this is the more flexible
of the two. It can move an enemy hero away from a minion or friendly hero they
wanted to target. Many red cards require precise positioning, and often moving
just 1 space can be enough to disrupt the enemies plans. For best effect, move
low-movement heroes like Brogan or Ursafar far away and watch them struggle to
run back the rest of the round.

### Alternate Blue

This effect isn’t as flexible as primary blue, but it is a very potent
anti-range effect. It affects a wide variety of enemy attacks AND skills,
meaning ranged heroes will need to get up closer to you (The Scarry Garry) if
they want to be effective. For best effect, get next to an enemy minion and then
activate this card to protect all nearby friendly minions \*Show Garrus next to
Brogan minion\* This way you can still farm a minion while protecting your own.

### Red

Garrus is a double melee character which means killing two minions is often very
hard or impossible. This means that often his red card is better used for
movement then attacks. Always be looking for opportunities to move next to
heroes with red, then hit them with gold.

### Primary Red

This is Garrus’ offensive attack card. Your silver card is very important when
using this card, it gives the discard you need to use this card's effect, AND
the silver slows nearby enemies, making it harder for them to get away from this
attack. Use this on turns you think the enemy will attack you, or on turn 4 to
chase down heroes with low defense.

### Alternate Red

Now here’s a true defensive option, and it is FUN. This card comes with loads of
numbers, 6 ATK and 7 DEF makes you pack a punch. This card can deny the enemy
space, it can push enemies towards your teammates for combos, it can attack one
target near you and push an entirely different target away. This card does it
all!

### Ultimate

Walk up to a hero. Hit them with attacks until you win the game. \*Shrug\*

## The Builds

“Builds” in Guards of Atlantis II are sort of nonsense. The best players will
always pick the option that best suits any given scenario. That said, here are
some paths I think you can use as a baseline.

### The Brawler Path

[Image 6919761 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6919761)

Sometimes known as the Scarry Garry build, the idea is to maximize Garrus’
threat, to duke it out with other brawlers, and to deny space against low
defense heroes. This build is less about hero kills and more about controlling
the battlefield. The most important items here are the Attack, because they
often turn your gold card into an unblockable attack. The level order isn’t VERY
important, so try to choose them in and order that serves the upcoming round.

### At Tier 2

You can reverse the blue cards here depending on the match-up. 2 defense if you
need to respect the enemy brawler or some other threat, 2 offense if it offers
one-shot potential or if you don’t feel threatened. The attack makes opponents
think twice before getting close enough to attack, and your heal will come in
handy when they do. Finally, the alt red makes you more survivable and increases
your chances of denying the enemy farm opportunities.

### At Tier 3

Feel free to go for Triple ATK or DEF if you think it’s important. The
initiative is important to avoid catastrophic turn orders (Enemy red cards going
faster than your blue or gold for example) and alt green can create great kill
opportunities if the enemy team doesn’t that it now defeats heroes. The movement
item comes with a heal and can be very potent, but be sure you don’t need that
initiative from the alt green before choosing it. You’re alt reds item comes
with a nice boost to green cards, and the card itself both increases attack and
defense stats as well as the push that happens after an attack, allowing you to
really punish heroes that don’t have a lot of movement, or are under the effects
of your Silver.

### The Anti-Range Path

[Image 6919762 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6919762)

Fun-Police anyone? The idea here is to threaten card kills. Use alt green to
make characters move away, then throw up shields with alt blue. If they haven’t
gotten the hint push them out of range again with alt red or simply kill them
with gold.

### At Tier 2

The Initiative makes your gold faster, which can be scary for low defense heroes
(Most ranged heroes). The two defense items just come along with getting into
the correct branch, it might be helpful later on in the game when ranged
characters start to build lots of attack, but it’s likely overkill at the
beginning of the game.

### At Tier 3

The defense and Initiative items make you very hard to kill, defense protects
from Value kills and Initiative protects from Card kills. Alt green basically
gives you a range 4 attack. Discards, immunity, pushes all allow you to disrupt
the enemy and create space for your allies.
