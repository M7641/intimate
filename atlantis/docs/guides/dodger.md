# Dodger Hero Spotlight

- Hero: Dodger the Warlock
- Author: Panetierre, on BoardGameGeek
- Source: <https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2901324/dodger-hero-spotlight>
- Posted: 2022-07-17
- Printing: not stated

## Introduction

Dodger is the goth queen of Guards of Atlantis. She delights in the grim and
macabre, using dead minions and bleeding heroes to make her magic. On her own
she’s a fast, weak ranged attacker, but when her black magic is flowing strong
she can do it all: brawl with best, take down heroes, and push the wave with the
risen dead. Dodger invites us to make the most greedy plays in some of the most
tense and exciting moments out there. How far can you take it?

### The Basics

**Gold: Dread Razor**

*+1 Range if you are adjacent to an empty minion spawn point in the battle zone.
Target a unit in range.*

While this attack starts at range 1, all you need is for someone to kill a
minion and suddenly your Gold can attack at range 2. Sometimes you’ll be taking
that minion with your Red attack to set up your own Gold; other times you can
get a friend to help you out. Even your friendly minion spawns will do the
trick.

Dodger’s Gold only hits for 1, but underestimate it at your peril. That’s plenty
enough to defeat a minion or a wounded hero with no cards in hand. And it can
wound a hero itself, of course, setting up several of Dodger’s effects.

**Silver: Death Trap**

*You may remove a friendly minion in radius. One enemy hero in radius who is
adjacent to an empty minion spawn point in the battle zone, discards a card, if
able.*

Everyone loves having a skill that makes an enemy discard a card. Only Dodger
can say she does it with her Silver card. Early in a minion wave you threaten a
counterattack on someone setting up to take your minion. Late in a wave there
are plenty of empty spawn points around to threaten your enemies through. Just
watch out for someone taking the heavy minion and moving the battle zone out
from under you.

Most of the time that first sentence is a nice bit of flavor, that you’d kill
your own minion to have a corpse to work with. But there are times it’s worth
it. A hero kill is of course more valuable than one little minion, so if it
looks like the discard will get you there then take it. Sometimes you know that
an enemy hero would kill this minion anyway and that they can’t make it to
another minion, so a sacrifice at least denies them the coins for killing it.
And it’s definitely risky, but you can use it to delay a push you’re about to
win. Dodger just loves it when the minion wave stalls and all those empty spawn
points keep powering her abilities.

### The Branches

#### Green

**Primary Green: Darkest Ritual**

*You may remove a friendly minion in radius. If there are 3 or more empty minion
spawn points in radius in the battle zone, gain 2 coins, and you may retrieve a
discarded card.*

Live the dream and get paid to stand around. Gather energy from the dead minions
in a battle zone to keep yourself leveling up. Between this income and the
farming you can do with your ranged attacks, Dodger often enjoys a level
advantage over her enemies. At Tier 3 you get a welcome heal out of it, too.
Much like with your Silver, you can generally ignore that part about sacrificing
your own minion. But it can occasionally be worthwhile, particularly when that
heal saves you from dying.

**Alternate Green: Necromastery**

*If an enemy hero in radius has a card in the discard, respawn a friendly minion
in a spawn point in radius in the battle zone.*

Raising the dead is a neat trick! All you need is an enemy hero to have taken
damage earlier this round and you can bring your minion back to the fight.
Getting an extra minion in a wave is a huge boon to your team. When it comes to
the minion battle that's as good as a third attack.

#### Blue

**Primary Blue: Aegis of Doom**

*You may remove a friendly minion in radius. If there are 2 or more empty minion
spawn points in radius in a battle zone +4 Defense and This Turn: You are
immune.*

Dodger may love death and decay, but she’s always a little too close to dying
herself. With Primary Blue you can have a card with potent defenses, provided
you have those empty spawn points around. On this branch Dodger feels *very*
different when the minion wave is fresh, covering all those spawn points, and
when the minion count is getting low. Be careful not to get cornered by enemies
when the wave moves. As usual, actually sacrificing a minion is reserved for
those times it saves your life.

**Alternate Blue: Soul Siphon**

*Perform an action of a card in the discard of an enemy hero in radius; apply no
item bonuses.*

Oooh!! Whether it’s used to copy your enemy’s best skills or just to get an
extra attack in the round, this skill is always so cool. Of course, that card
has to end up in their discard somehow before you can copy it, and people
usually hold on to their best effects to use themself. One way to make it happen
is for your friend to attack them with a good strong Red and force them to
defend with their own Red, giving it over to you.

#### Red

**Primary Red: Middlefinger of Death**

*Target a unit in range. +3 Attack if you target a hero with a card in the
discard. This Turn: The target cannot retrieve cards.*

No targeting restrictions on a range 3 attack? Yes, please! Without the bonus
damage it’s only a little attack for 2, but like with Dodger’s Gold an attack of
any strength can put in work. Find a wounded target and that bonus kicks in an
astounding 5 strength attack, unmatched for an attack that threatens so many
spaces. That “can’t retrieve cards” part on the Tier 3 is often overlooked, but
it makes this the perfect first hit against enemies who had hoped a heal would
save them from all your discard antics.

**Alternate Red: Blazing Skull**

*Target a unit in range. Before the attack: Push every enemy unit adjacent to
the target 1 space away from the target’s space.*

Dodger’s Alternate Red gives up a little bit of that range, falling back to just
range 2. In exchange it has a solid defense value and always has that big 5
attack strength. It also pushes minions off their spawn points to power up your
abilities. At Tier 3 that even happens early enough to separate your target from
their helpful minions, making it harder to defend.

#### Ultimate

**Tide of Darkness**

*Whenever you perform an action, all spaces count as if they are in a battle
zone and have a minion spawn point.*

Ultimate Dodger has drawn in enough power to not need fresh corpses any more.
She fuels her dark powers herself. Constantly having the full strength of her
Gold and Primary branch abilities makes her a terrifying force on the
battlefield.

### The Paths

**The Skullcracker**

This path puts Dodger in the middle of the action, using those empty spawn
points to power up your abilities and get in your enemy’s face. You aren’t quite
as hearty as the natural brawlers of the game, but you’re faster than they are
and can engage at range, so stick and move. You grow more powerful as the wave
drags on, so be sure to let some minions get cleared out before you engage enemy
heroes, and be ready to disengage as the minion wave moves to the next battle
zone.

**At Tier 2**

Primary Green is the natural first choice. The longer you have it, the more
money you can gain. Alternate Red and Primary Blue both do a lot to improve your
defenses and make you comfortable in a brawl. Usually the order depends on
whether the minion wave is moving. If you can rely on those spawn points,
Primary Blue is great; if not, go with the Alternate Red first.

If you aren’t being threatened that much, that attack item from Alternate Blue
can be very appealing. Especially when you’re getting good income and can switch
back to Primary Blue quickly if you start to miss it.

**At Tier 3**

Generally you want your Alternate Red first to increase the radius on your other
cards, expanding your Silver’s threat and making your cards more consistent.
Upgrading Primary Green gives it a heal that can make all the difference in a
brawl, and it has that handy defense item. Upgrading Primary Blue fills in that
attack item and protects you from those turn 4 double-hits.

In some matchups the 3 defense items are enough to not need the Primary Blue’s
ability, and a switch to Alternate Blue and its range item can help find that
last kill or take those last minions to end the game.

**Bloodbath and Beyond**

This path features your discard focused skills and emphasizes your extreme range
and speed. Ask your team to help you get heroes wounded so you can use your
skills and finish them off. Anyone with a discarded card will be running for
their life. But your poor defenses means any healthy enemies are a real threat
to you, so stick close to your teammates to keep some of that pressure off. Or
take up positions farther behind your friends, using your range to support them
and staying ready to come in for some final blows.

**At Tier 2**

Taking Primary Red first puts you well on your way to having the range and speed
you’re looking for. Alternate Green next is a natural choice to continue
supporting that, and delays the upgrade to Alternate Blue to hold on to that
Primary’s defense a little longer. But if the minion wave just moved, or is
about to move, that Primary Blue isn’t going to be helping you out anyway.

Primary Green is a nice option here that can boost your income to level up very
quickly. Getting more attack items helps your Red scare everyone off, too. And
sometimes the extra defense from the item and effect with Primary Blue is too
useful to pass up.

**At Tier 3**

Everything is great at this tier and there are excellent reasons to take them in
any order. The range item with Alternate Blue is maybe the most appealing, and
it lets you copy attacks, too. Upgrading Primary Red makes it faster and
stronger, and that movement item is always welcome. Upgrading Alternate Green
can close out games through that very easy to use respawn effect, or from the
help of that last initiative item in catching heroes and winning minion races.

Usually if you diverge here it’s on Blue or Green, and it’s because you were
just one number off earlier: with just one more attack item, you’d have a kill
there; with just one more defense item, you’d survive that attack. If you do
want Primary Green and its defense item, taking it early gives you some extra
income to get those last levels.

## Follow-up by the author (2022-07-17)

Dodger's always a great teammate to have around. Often what she looks for is
someone big on her side to work alongside and avoid getting ganked, like a
Xargatha or Garrus. And she has a natural synergy with heroes like Cutter or
Whisper who have an easy time making enemy heroes discard. Heroes that place
tokens can be awkward for Dodger when they cover up minion spawn points, but
planning together can avoid that, or even help her instead.

There's a play you can make when the minion lane is about to push, where you
stand on an empty spawn point when the minions come to your zone. This forces
the minions to spawn in an adjacent space, and now when you move that spawn
point is already empty. Because tokens are removed after the minion battle at
the end of the round, when timed right they can be used to open up spawn points.

On the enemy team, Dodger is suited to punishing heroes that are light on
defenses, like Hanu or Trinkets, or who can't move far once they're in trouble,
like Ignatia or NebKher. She doesn't like getting out-ranged, and of course
doesn't like heals or effects that prevent minions dying. Tokens and other
things that cover up spawn points are also bad news depending on how she's
built.
