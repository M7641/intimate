# Swift Character Spotlight

- Author: bcsj, on BoardGameGeek

- Posted: 2022-06-25, edited 2022-06-26

Hello! I am considered among the playtesters as the primary Swift player, to the
point that she has actively been banned from me in our league games, despite her
being considered among the lower tiers on the hero roster. While she isn't the
strongest, she is however a very rewarding hero to play! Here is my spotlight of
her. I might have gone overboard a bit, please *bear* with me. 😄

## Introduction

[Image 6932242 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6932242)

Swift is the household sniper of Guards of Atlantis II. If you love to harass
your enemies from across the board and rocket jumping around the map, Swift
might just be the hero for you. As *the sniper* Swift has the longest ranged
attack of any hero in the game sporting up to a whopping 7 range on her primary
red attack. At the same time every round for Swift also presents a positional
puzzle due to the nature of her gold card, but more on that later.

Your greatest defense is to stay out of reach and do your business from there,
but beware 'cause leaving your teammate alone like that risks them getting
ganged up on while you are away. For this reason, reading the game and selecting
your build carefully is very important as Swift. Be sure your teammate can
handle themselves before you leave them to snipe away from the fringes of the
board.

Swift have two modes: *Sniper rifle* and *shotgun*, which will affect how you
play. Both branches of blue and green will work with either of these, but will
influence your means of threat and interaction. But let's get to the cards!

### The Basics

**Gold** - Reload! - initiative 5

*Perform the primary action of the resolved card in your previous turn slot.*

Reload! is Swift's most iconic card. It is incredibly versatile, but its main
function is of course to repeat your powerful red attack; this card *is* your
second attack. Because sequencing red into gold is your only way of getting two
attacks in a round you are predictable and a clever enemy will move in to
threaten you on the turn you wish to repeat your red. Make sure that they are
giving up something (e.g. position and/or farm) if they do that.

That said, gold has other possible roles as well. Due to the low initiative and
incredible (*for a gold card*) 2 boots you have two green-level initiatives and
can (at the cost of your second attack) pursue an enemy twice. And finally, we
can of course reload other abilities than our attack. Are you out of position?
Reload your incredible blue jumps to cross the map! Are the enemies considering
coming out into the open, reload your primary green card to threaten discards.

As a last note: remember that you don't even need to shoot with your red to
reload it, you can use the 4 boots on red to get into the perfect position and
then still have an attack with a red level of power next turn!

**Silver** - Bounce - initiative 13

*Swap this card with a red or a gold card in your hand or discard. If you do,
you may push an enemy unit adjacent to you 1 space, then move 2 spaces in a
straight line, if able.*

Your silver is your gold. This card is your high speed escape. How do other
heroes sidestep an incoming attack, which is faster then their blue initiative?
They play gold! Swift plays silver. But be aware, like any other hero, if you
have used up both your attacks there is no dodging that fast attack anymore. But
is this just a dodge? Well, not entirely!

In my opinion Bounce has 3 modes of use. I already mentioned fast
sidestep/escape (*and note how the push + 2 spaces often will put more space
between you and the enemy than their green movement can cover*). The two other
modes are: recovery of your lost attack, and disruption, both comes with fairly
high risk though, so be careful! I could write a full page on just silver, but
I'll try to keep it short here.

Recovery: If you get hit early in a round and you defend with red, note that
your silver can swap with your red card from the discard pile, hence bringing it
into a turn slot and allowing you to reload it.

Disruption: With the push you can push enemies about to take a minion out of
position. The card is fast enough that you can even catch Tigerclaw with this if
you have the tiebreaker.

Note: As it is a question which comes up, I should point out that you don't get
any of the effects on gold, nor red, when you swap silver with them. No high
speed shooting here.

### The Branches

#### Red

As mentioned you have either *sniper rifle* or a *shotgun*. The sniper rifle has
incredible range but comes with a straight line restriction and typically leaves
you low on the defensive side. The shotgun is your "*up-in-their-face*" weapon,
sporting an attack bonus on adjacent enemies and scatter damage forcing discards
on enemies adjacent to your target.

**Primary Red** - Killshot - range 6

*Target a non-adjacent unit in a stright line.*

Nothing special here but a straight line attack all the way to the moon. Note
that an enemy can safely walk next to you, which will often spell your death if
you try to reload. Far away enemies have to respect you when you are on this
branch. The biggest threat against this card is NebKher, who can take away your
target (*silver*), shrug off your attack (*with blue; sometimes even green*) and
copy your snipe back in your face (*gold*), which will almost always kill you.
Against a NebKher you will switch out of this 95% of the time.

**Alternate Red** - Super-Shotgun - attack 4+

*+3 Attack if you target an adjacent unit. Before the attack: Up to one enemy
hero adjacent to the target discards a card, or is defeated.*

This is the red you want if you want nearby enemies to respect you. In melee
range you hit like a truck, as hard as the strongest brawlers, and you can even
chip away at them with discards by hitting adjacent minions if they should try
to bolster their defense in that way. This branch also comes with extra defense
items meaning your blue and green cards will provide more defense than usual.
Note that the discarding hero doesn't need to be in range.

#### Blue

Blue is your mobility suit. Jump in straight lines along an axis or do askew
off-axis jumps. You can play either branch with either red so it will often come
down to what items you desire, what the situation calls for, or what you just
enjoy the most.

**Primary Blue** - Drop Trooper - radius 4

*Place yourself into a space in a straight line in radius. You may then push up
to two enemy units adjacent to you 1 space.*

Do you need to cross the map fast, this is you tool. And you can even reload it
if the first jump didn't get you all the way. The push is nice too and, like
silver, can (if correctly timed and positioned) disrupt enemy attacks and
skills. It is fast enough that with initiative items you can out-speed most
heroes' gold card if they haven't build initiative themselves.

**Alternate Blue** - Stream Valkyrie - radius 3

*You may place yourself into a space in radius not in a straight line from you.
You may then fast travel once, if able.*

The fact that this card is a defense/skill is huge as it can let you escape out
of reach when someone moves in and hits you. The fast travel means that you can
often jump out of the beach and into the throne and immediately fast travel up
the jungle to be ready to receive a returning push. The dream is to defend with
this while you have your shotgun red on the board and then jump adjacent to
another enemy hero for the point blank shot.

#### Green

You green is either your area denial or your crutches.

**Primary Green** - Killing Ground - radius 4

*An enemy hero in radius, not adjacent to you and not adjacent to terrain, or a
token, discards a card, or is defeated.*

The effectiveness of this card goes up and down. It can deny some serious area
and force enemy heroes to cling to terrain for dear life, but there will also be
games where the branch is utterly useless. Tokens are the biggest bane of this
card as they quickly swallow up the spaces you can target. Trinkets is
particularly nasty as not only does he place tokens, his turret also counts as
terrain and protects him. Observe that it doesn't matter if the tokens are your
teammate's, so pairing with a token user will also often take you out of this
branch.

**Alternate Green** - Fog of War - movement action

***Next turn:** Before or After you perform an action, you may swap any two of
your resolved cards.*

This is your safety crutches, this is Swift on "*easy mode*". Is it impossible
to find the save spot to shoot and reload? Fog of War has got your back. This
card allows you to swap your red card into a new turn slot position from which
you can reload it. Not only does this allow you to reposition between shooting,
the card itself is a movement action. The fact that it comes with your range
item is huge too.

#### Ultimate

Bullet Time

*After you perform any action on your gold card, you may perform an action on
your gold card again; it can be the same action. Cannot target the same enemy
hero twice.*

Ultimate Swift is incredibly scary! Because you can now take up to two steps
with the movement action on your gold card and then immediately follow up with
its primary action, the coverage of your reds (*when reloaded*) multiply to the
extreme. Not only that, but your gold by itself can also reload your red twice
and allow you to leisurely take two minions with gold instead when the enemy
heroes have fled out of the battle zone to escape their otherwise inevitable
death.

The dream is of course to defend an attack with gold the turn after your shotgun
red or alternate green to immediately counter kill them. But I've yet to see
that happen!

### The Paths

**Disclaimer:** These builds are merely examples and there is no guarantee they
will work for you in your game. Read the flow of your game to decide upon the
best path for yourself.

**Fast and hard, from a million miles away**

[Image 6932352 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6932352)

You goal is to stay out of the fights and threaten to kill anyone who dares to
use their red and blue for anything but defense.

**Variant:** Trade out speed for more power: Going alt green instead will give
you +1 Attack and +1 Range for the maximum possible 7 range at the cost of
losing two initiative items.

Weaknesses:

You have no defense, so powerful ranged attacks like Brogan's spear with a few
attack items will plow through your measly 6 defense if you don't respect it.

As mentioned in primary red NebKher is your natural born enemy. If you want to
snipe in a game against him, you have 2 choices: with range item you can
outrange his copy of your attack on tier II and on tier III you should keep him
at a minimum range of 5-6 spaces away, depending on if he has a range item.

**Shotgun brawler**

[Image 6932362 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6932362)

This build gives you maximal defense to get up close and brawl. This is how you
tell your enemies that they have entered your turf.

This build has a lot of flex in your blue and green choices; mix and match as
you like. Be aware that the alternate blue branch loses a bit of its potency
without the radius upgrade that comes with the tier III sniper rifle. On the
other hand the shotgun doesn't need the alternate green as much as most heroes
will think twice about getting close to you when your red is on the table.

Weaknesses:

Tigerclaw's poison is your biggest weakness in this build. With 3 defense items
it will effectively render you defenseless and most gold cards will snap you
like a twig.

You should also respect heroes who may be able to poke you without getting into
your shotgun range.
