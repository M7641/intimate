# Xargatha Hero Spotlight (1st printing)

- Hero: Xargatha the Changed
- Author: GoatRogan, on BoardGameGeek
- Source: <https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2897093/xargatha-hero-spotlight-1st-printing>
- Posted: 2022-07-09, edited 2025-02-09

**NOTE: This guide is for the first printing version of Xargatha so may be
outdated based on which printing you are playing with.**

## Introduction

[Image 6954337 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6954337)

With all the excellent guides being released for some of the higher complexity
champs, I thought it would be a good time to take a step back and revist the 7
core heroes. While it's tempting to crack open Guards and immediately grab the
giant tree flinging ape, or the mummy master of mind control, there is a lot of
amazing goodness in that 1 star roster waiting to be discovered. And for my
money, you can't do better than that slithering snake Randy Ort...uh...I mean
Xargatha.

Xargatha the Changed is an excellent choice of starter hero for anyone who
enjoys a good brawl, but with the added benefit of being a very capable zoner.
She mixes some of the heavy hitting goodness of a Brogan charge with the thinky
movement disruption and displacement of Wasp, all coiled up into a superb,
scaley package.

Her strengths are her ability to brawl with the best of them, her respectable
push options, great movement, and her enticing crowd control (CC). The snake
thrives when paired with a faster initiator to either setup or close kills, or
with heroes that can support her from a distance while she brawls in the middle
of a battlezone, either through additional minion manipulation or heals.

Xargatha can struggle against higher range and higher initiative heroes, as they
can often escape her shennanigans and/or ping her from afar. She excels when she
can plant in the middle of the action and fire off her CC abilities, so any
character that can turn those off through dispels, or stay far away from her
while still having an impact on the fight are ones to look out for.

With our introductions out of the way, let's take a closer look at her kit.

### The Basics

**Gold: Backlash**

*Target a unit adjacent to you. After the attack: This Turn: After you discard a
card for any reason, perform the primary action on an attack card in your
discard, if able. (First complete the action causing the discard)*

The card that proves you shouldn't mess with a snake if you don't want to get
bit. After attacking an adjacent unit, this card serves as an extreme deterrent
to any nearby hero who wanted to take a potshot at you. Depending on which Red
you choose, this can threaten adjacent units, or units up to 2 spaces away (3
with a ranged item.)

Take note that the wording on this card doesn't mean you have to discard your
Red to trigger it's ability, it just has to be in your discard after the action
that caused the discard has been completed. Theoretically this means that you
could get even more than 2 attacks on the turn you played Gold (though if an
enemy team enables you to do this...they probably deserve what's coming).

The biggest obstacle with this card is that your initiative is low-to-middling,
meaning a good amount of Golds will go before you can trigger it. To get the
most of this card's threat potential, you may want to consider grabbing an
initiative item or two.

**Silver: Siren's Call**

*Target the nearest non-immune enemy unit in range not adjacent to you; move
that unit up to 2 spaces along the shortest valid path towards you. May repeat
once.*

*Initiative - 1*

*Range - 3*

Cool... so Xargatha can potentially triple strike in one turn: but did you know
she also has a lovely singing voice? At least your enemies think so, and they
just can't wait to walk up and get her autograph.

The first of Xargatha's CC options -- her Silver -- excels at zone control. The
fact that this card has such a low initiative is a strength, not a weakness.
When played effectively, this card can really make enemies think twice before
they walk next to you. As we will see later, once Xargatha has you in her grasp,
she can make it very difficult to get away.

For the most part, Xargatha doesn't mind being next to enemies. In fact, she
often prefers it. Not only can this card set up attacks on enemy units without
sacrificing your position, it also pairs incredibly well with cards like your
Primary Green and Primary Red (which we'll get to in a bit).

For a bit of extra utility, note that the pull is "up to 2 spaces." While you
likely will want to pull your targets the full distance most of the time (thus
getting them adjacent to you) you can also use this to pull enemy heroes 1 or 2
spaces out of position, potentially denying them a shot on a friendly unit. This
is an especially good option if you already have a card in the discard and don't
want to risk another hit, or if you want to set up a target for your ranged
Alternate Red.

### The Branches

#### Green

One of Xargatha's strengths is her great mobility, and her greens are a large
part of why that is. Not only are they both movement abilities, meaning you'll
have an easy time triggering their effects, they each create a zone of threat
that your enemies would do well to respect.

**Primary Green: Aura of Domination**

*This round: All enemy minions in radius, including the immune ones, count as
friendly when calculating the defense totals.*

I've already revealed my bias for Xargatha as a hero, so I might as well double
down on it and say not only is this one of my favorite cards in the game, I
believe it is also one of the strongest.

As has been mentioned in other strategy articles, the individual stats on Guards
are balanced on a knife's edge. Any shift in value can drastically shape the
outcome of a round, which is one of the reasons why minions, and your position
in relation to them, is so important. By turning your enemies' minions against
them, you are making the battlezone an infinitely more threatening place to be.

Xargatha works best when she is in the thick of the action, and this card makes
it a lot easier to do so. It boosts her Primary Red (which we will talk about
later) to absurd degrees, and also supports her teammates.

I can't really think of any negatives or caveats to include with this card. It's
just that good.

**Alternate Green: Deadly Embrace**

*Next Turn: Enemy heroes adjacent to you cannot move, except when performing a
movement action on a gold card.*

*Movement - 2*

Further proof that Xargatha is the CC queen, your Alternate Green is another
strong tool used to prevent your enemy from getting anywhere they wanted to
go...at least not without a cost. The most obvious strength of this card is that
next turn, any enemies who start their action adjacent to you have a pretty
difficult decision to make: stay and fight, or waste their precious attacks to
retreat.

Either way, they are at your mercy, so use that power to your advantage. Based
on how you think they will respond to the threat next turn, you can play your
Gold or Red if anticipating an attack, play Silver to draw them back in when
they thought they could get away, or further lock them down with your Primary
Blue.

A nifty trick with this card is that you don't even need be next to your target
on the turn you play it. The card's effect lasts for the entirety of the
subsequent turn, so playing your fast Blue when the effect actually kicks in can
help you close the remaining distance between you and your prey. You could use
your Red to do this as well, but I think that's the less enticing option.

It's also worth picking through the wording a bit to fully understand this
card's power. This not only blocks movement actions on non Gold/Red cards, it
prevents any part of a primary ablity that allow one to "move" in the card's
text.

#### Blue

Xargatha's Blue branches offer some flexibility in how she manages the zone she
is in. Both serve to compliment her desire to remain in the thick of it, but
through very different means. While one adds yet another CC tool to her toolkit,
the other helps her stay in the fray.

**Primary Blue: Turn Into Statues**

*Enemy heroes in radius are immune, cannot perform a movement action, and count
as terrain.*

The high initiative control card that compliments her slow Silver, this card can
pack a wallop. When timed right, your Primary Blue can lock an opponent (or
opponents) down and hold them in place for a turn. Stun cards of this magnitude
are in short supply in Guards, but can be incredibly powerful in setting up hero
kills or disrupting enemy plans for the round.

On top of everything, Xargatha normally isn't super bothered by taking a turn to
stand still. Your Silver can still bring enemies to you later in the round, and
your great Red movement, specifically on your Primary, can easily be used to
play catch up if needed.

That doesn't mean this card doesn't come with some caveats though. Enemies who
read your intent to play this can still setup to play attacks and skills, as
they can still perform primary actions. They also can't be targeted by anything
(from either friendly or enemy heroes) for the turn once you've turned them to
stone, so you have to be careful you aren't denying your ally a sneaky kill or
discard. Finally, you are only preventing movement actions, not other kinds of
mobility. Primary skills/attacks that let a hero move (like Brogan's charge) can
still fire, as well as any place/swap effects.

In addition, earlier tiers have a restriction of "nearest enemy," which can
change mid-turn. If you initially freeze an enemy 2 spaces away who played
Green, and another enemy who is acting quicker moves to a space adjacent to you,
that previous target is no longer nearest, and is thus freed from their stone
prison before having to resolve their card.

**Alternate Blue: Devoted Followers**

*If you are adjacent to a minion, you may retrieve a discarded card and This
Turn: You are immune to attack actions.*

Complimenting Xargatha's side gimmick of liking to be adjacent to other units,
this card is a great option for when you find yourself consistently at the
center of the action in a battlezone.

Not only do you have the option to heal yourself, you get a full turn's worth of
immunity to attack actions on TIII. Because the criteria is only that you be
next to the minion, I think this card can be equally good whether you want to
stay in a fight, or shrug off some hits while you focus on the push.

#### Red

Similar to other heroes, the biggest difference in Xargatha's Reds is that one
is melee and the other is ranged. On a hero who has a melee gold, it can often
be more difficult to compete for the push win condition, so having the option
between big damage or more targets is always nice . However, there is also a
very interesting overlap in that both reward you for being next to enemies in
different ways.

**Primary Red: Lethal Spin**

*Target a unit adjacent to you. +3 Attack for every enemy unit adjacent to you.*

*Attack - 2+*

I've mentioned several times how Xargatha doesn't mind being next to her
enemies. This is why. On TIII, this card is guaranteed to do at least 5 damage,
which is ok, but not anything mind blowing. However, throw just one more enemy
minion into the equation and you are suddenly a part of the same weight class as
your Brogans and Ursafars. You might also already be thinking about the synergy
with Primary Green.

Like her Gold, the main obstacle to this card is it's lower Red Initiative.
Chances are your opponent will have 3 opportunities to dodge this (Red, Blue,
and Gold) so know that this will often be more of a threat to get an enemy to
run. However, things like your Alternate Green can help you land this more
reliably.

**Alternate Red: Rapid Thrusts**

*Target a unit in range, not adjacent to you. After the attack: If you are
adjacent to an enemy hero, you may repeat once on a different target unit.*

*Range - 2*

This card is a weird one because to make the best use of it, you want an enemy
hero next to you to enable some respectable ranged damage on a non-adjacent
hero, contribute to the push, or a mix of both. The downside is you can't
actually hit that adjacent hero with this.

Still, like a lot of Xargatha's cards, there is power in forcing your opponents
to make the tough decision. Do they stay and fight you, risking a minion double
take or worse? Do they back away only to now be in range of your Red attack?
Because of this conundrum you can create for your enemies, this is definitely
the "thinkier" Red branch. It shines brightest when you are able to correctly
read your opponent's actions and capitalize on there misplays.

The biggest downsides to this card are that you are giving up a significant
defense advantage (defense drops by an insane 3 from your Primary), and your
movement bumps down from 5 to 4. At TIII, you also lose your movement item.

#### Ultimate: Metamorphosis

*You may move through obstacles. Before you perform an action, one enemy hero
adjacent to you discards a card, if able.*

I hope that I've already convinced you Xargatha likes being next to enemies, but
if not, surely this Ultimate will. With all her ways to lock an enemy in place,
this card lets you fully embrace your inner boa constrictor, squeezing an enemy
hero of cards before delivering the final blow.

Depending on match-ups, your lower initiative might mean that this effect
doesn't fire as much as you would like, but it can still be used to threaten
enemy heroes away from you which has a value of it's own.

### The Paths

Others have brought it up before, but it's worth mentioning that suggested
builds are the biggest "take it with a grain of salt" sections in any of these
guides. Guards can vary so much from game to game, that there is really no way
to anticipate what you might need. Some games, items are going to be more
important than card text. Other times, it will be the reverse. If nothing else,
I hope to just point out a couple fun synergies and highlight the benefit of
taking certain cards in a specific order. First and foremost, go with your gut.
If a build isn't working for you, consider pivoting to something else.
Flexibility is the biggest benefit offered by Guard's leveling system.

**The Green Anaconda**

[Image 6954338 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6954338)

The ultimate goal for this build is to reach your maximum CC potential. By game
end, the hope is that you can latch onto any opponent and never have to let them
go. However, since we aren't picking up any initiative until later in the build,
know that we plan on making the most out of her slower cards here. We'll often
be threatening people away from things at the cost of a needle drop of a Red
attack, or locking them down with the rest of our kit.

**At Tier 2**

I think Xargatha, especially when she sticks to melee, can be a pretty forgiving
hero, so I don't know that order matters too much here. However, I'd be hard
pressed to find a reason to upgrade something other than Green for your first
level. It expands the scope of the effect to ranged minions, hurts all nearby
enemies, and helps all nearby heroes. The move also supports our upgrade to
Primary Red, and since we are already planning on being next to things, we get
our heal (though this is mainly for the attack item).

**At Tier 3**

I feel a little more attached to the order here, but again, builds in Guards are
so match dependent, there's no way I can predict what you might need in any
given scenario. My main justification for a quick upgrade to Red is the movement
item because it makes your already great movement even better. We then switch to
Alt Green because we actually want to start landing our Red and Gold on heroes
more, and the one ini item we also gain hopefully helps with that. Finally, we
get a ranged item on our melee? Yes, because as I said, we are going for max
disruption and a Range 4 Silver does exactly that. Also, this Primary Blue is
just killer.

**The Spitting Cobra**

[Image 6954341 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6954341)

I'm not going to lie, we are getting weird with this one. Already you can see we
are going with our ranged Red and not picking up a ranged item which is... a
choice. However, I think there is a lot to like here as far as how we intend to
build up her threat radius and hopefully not be too scared of enemy attacks.

**At Tier 2**

The first upgrade is primarily for the defense item, which gives us some
coverage when we grab her Alt Red. The Alt Green is a little awkward with this
build, but we really want the extra initiative to be able to shoot fast. You can
also still use it to get in an enemy's face, threaten with Gold, and take a
potshot at something in range if they decide to attack you back.

**At Tier 3**

First, we go back to our Primary Green. This helps both on the defense front (we
get a defense item and positive modifiers from enemy minions), while also
maintaining some offensive momentum (again by turning enemy minions against
their own heroes.) We then grab our TIII Alt Red for the potential double
attack, and for the Radius upgrade -- which currently benefits our new Green and
TII Primary Blue. I haven't forgotten about that first level up! We are however
going to switch Blues for our penultimate level, which gives us an attack item
(bringing our Red up to a sizable 6 damage at Range 2), as well as a nice buffer
against incoming attacks. The Blues are pretty flexible here though, and I can
see sticking with the Primary (and getting the range) being just as viable an
option.

With that, I hope this provides a good overview of Xargatha! She is one of the
quirkier core heroes in my opinion, but I think she is an absolute blast to
play. Let me know if you have any questions, and I would also be interested to
here of any recent snake highlights people have encountered in their games!

### EDITS

*7/10/2022* - Removed passages describing "Fast Travel" as an allowable action
with Xargatha's Primary Blue and Alt Green as this was incorrect. Both block
Fast Travel because even though it is described in the rules as a replacement
for the movement action, you still have to be in a position to perform a
movement action to enable it. As Primary Blue prevents movement actions, you
can't replace said action to fast travel. The same with Xargatha's green (except
for Gold/Red on TII and Gold on TIII). There is also another corner case where
Silver Arrow can still Fast Travel with Hit and Run as she's doing it through a
Primary Action.
