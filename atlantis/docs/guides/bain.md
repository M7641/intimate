# Bain Hero Spotlight (1st printing)

- Hero: Bain the Bountyhunter
- Author: GoatRogan, on BoardGameGeek
- Source: <https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2904317/bain-hero-spotlight-1st-printing>
- Posted: 2022-07-23, edited 2025-02-09

**This guide is for the first printing version of Bain so may be outdated based
on which printing you are playing with.**

## Introduction

[Image 6974951 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6974951)

I'm going to let you in on a little secret. Despite trying to trick you with
it's talk of "minions" and "pushing the lane," there is really only one
objective in Guards. And that objective is to kill your friends. I'm sorry it
took us so long to break it to you, but it is what it is.

Every hero has the ability to do this to some degree, but only one has "Kapow!"
tattoo'd on his arm and eats a bowl of life counters for breakfast: the smooth
talkin', crossbow slingin', steampunk cowboy called Bain.

Bain is great at getting quick discards, locking down opponents, and threatening
key areas of the board. He also has some solid support options. His main
weaknesses are that he only has one full attack (meaning his gold can't kill
other heroes), he can struggle on farming, and a couple of his big cards
introduce a line-of-sight restriction that can muck up your plans if not
careful.

Bain can be an absolute terror when paired with other kill oriented heroes with
a mix of discard options, high initiative, and range. He can also enable heavy
hitting but slower brawlers if he sets up to use his Silver correctly. However,
he can struggle against high initiative characters who can sidestep his
shenanigans, easily trade discards with him, and/or can place or swap themselves
versus moving.

### The Basics

**Gold: Dead or Alive**

*You may give a hero in radius a bounty marker. The hero with a bounty marker
spends 1 additional life counter when defeated. You may then choose one --*

*- Defeat an adjacent enemy minion.*

*- Adjacent enemy hero discards a card, if able.*

*Initiative - 11*

*Radius - 3*

Just in reading this ability, we can see the "or Alive" portion of the title
isn't really applicable. You trigger this ability to signal to your team that
you want a hero brought down come hell or high water. However, while this card
seems straightforward, it's important to really break down the wording to see
all that you can do with it.

First, as we mentioned, you can give someone a bounty marker for the round. If
killed, that hero will cost their team one additional life counter. This is
already huge. Only two cards do this in the game -- Ursafar's Alt Red is the
second -- and this is by far the easier of the two to setup. The choices to
follow are optional, so while it's not always the best use of this card, know
that you can just give someone the marker and be done. This could potentially be
worthwhile on T3 or T4 when an enemy hero is going to die anyway and you just
want to add insult (and another life counter) to injury.

The first of the options presented after the main text is to defeat an enemy
minion. I know we talked about how Bain sure does love killin', but don't
discount this option. He is not the most reliable pusher, so the fact that this
card allows him to pursue two endgame goals at once is pretty great.

The second option obviously has a lot more synergy with it's primary text in
getting an adjacent enemy to discard a card. The most obvious use is to hit your
bounty target with an immediate discard, but the text only says the enemy hero
has to be adjacent. You can also get a discard on a different target provided
they are next to you. It may be that a non-adjacent enemy is going to bite the
bullet by the end of the round so you slap them with the marker, but get a
discard on the adjacent enemy to help you with your more immediate situation.
After all, why settle for one hero kill in a round if you can go for two?

**Silver: Get over here!**

*Target a unit in a straight line, with no obstacles between you. Then, if able,
move that unit towards you by the shortest valid path, until it is adjacent.
This Turn: If it's an enemy unit, it can't move.*

*Initiative - 12*

*Range - 4*

The fastest card in Bain's kit, this is your most reliable way to either get
someone close enough to slap a bounty on them, or prevent your bounty from
getting away. It also introduces Bain's "line-of-sight" sub-gimmick, one present
in his Primary Red as well.

This card is also great in setting up a nearby hero to get a free attack on the
target you pull, and can even enable some of the slower, insane damage melee
Reds that normally scare people away before they trigger (Bain has your back,
Ursafar!!!)

And remember when I said Bain is a support hero? Well here's a big reason why.
This card can help a friend get out of a bad spot fast, without having to deal
with the "can't move" portion of the card text. If you are feeling spicy, you
can also look out for ways to use this offensively. Maybe pulling your friend
Whisper over to some juicy empty minion spawns and letting him barrel towards an
enemy with his Primary Red.

In addition, this also interacts with both enemy and friendly minions in
valuable ways. Even if Bain sometimes struggles getting a minion kill, he can
always pull one of his bros out of the line of fire to deny an enemy hero. He
can also pull an enemy minion, setting himself up for an easy Gold target next
turn.

### The Branches

#### Green

**Primary Green: Perfect Getaway**

*Block the attack and move up to 3 spaces in a straight line. The attacker may
swap their attack card with a card in their hand.*

Bain's get out of jail (relatively) free card. With how specific his toolkit can
be, Bain really wants to trigger the card text on a number of his cards to reach
his max potential. This means that he can often be standing still for several
turns at a time. He's also a single full attack hero, which increases the value
of his Red pretty significantly so he really doesn't have to defend with it if
he can help it. As such, this card is a great option to relieve some of that
pressure in having to play your Blue and Red more often, thus removing your
highest defense options. The movement is also huge, as Bain often requires very
precise positioning with his Silver and Primary Red.

The obvious caveat is that the enemy that hits you has the option of retrieving
their attack card, which rarely feels good. A silver lining is that this swap
happens before any "after the attack" effects trigger, which means you can block
things like a Misa gold gain on her Primary Red. It also would fizzle any active
effects that might trigger like Ursafar becoming enraged.

**Alternate Green: High Ground**

*You may ignore obstacles. Next turn: You may ignore restrictions to have no
obstacles between you and the target when performing attacks and skills.*

*Movement - 2*

If you aren't as worried about taking hits, but are worried about lining up your
straight line hooks and crossbow shots, this card is for you. The first part of
the card text is pretty powerful, potentially letting you close the gap to a
target when there are some obstacles in the way, or letting you escape a hero
through a wall of minions they are unable to chase you through. However, the
next turn effect is where you will probably be getting the most utility.

I've made mention of it before, but like his Silver, Bain's Primary Red includes
a line of sight gimmick baked into the card text. In playing this card, you free
yourself of that pesky restriction, allowing you to fire a hook or crossbow bolt
through any number of obstacles. This can lead to some insane Silver hooks as it
allows you to pull targets around walls, minions, and whatever else is standing
between you and them.

#### Blue

**Primary Blue: We're Not Done Yet**

*An enemy hero in radius with 2 or more cards in hand discards a card facedown.
Name a color and reveal that card. If it's not the color you named, gain 2 coins
and the enemy hero may retrieve their card. If they do, repeat once.*

This card, in my opinion, is the biggest crowd pleaser in Guards. Why just get a
discard on someone when you can make a game out of it? While high Initiative
discards are pretty powerful, your opponent has some agency in trying to get you
to whiff. Believe me, the mind games are real when this card text triggers as
you and your opponent shoot each other thousand yard stares. Queue The Good, The
Bad, and the Ugly soundtrack...

We've already established that we want hero kills, so it's easy to see why we'd
want to guess correctly to get that discard. However, the other option is
incredibly beneficial to Bain as well. As I've mentioned, Bain can sometimes
struggle with pushing, which means if he's not careful he can fall behind on
leveling. This card provides a cushion that lets you lean into those hero kills
and still walk away with some change even if you weren't able to close out the
kill.

A more limited, but still important utility is that even if you guess
incorrectly, the opponent still has to reveal the card before taking it back in
hand. This can give you and your team much needed info on if they potentially
did any sneak level ups, or if you needed to know a specific value on the card
they revealed.

**Alternate Blue: Another One!**

*A hero in radius may retrieve a discarded card. This turn and next turn: Enemy
heroes in radius may not move to a space in a straight line from them with a
movement action.*

If you find that your team is taking a bit of a beating from round to round,
this card is a worthy consideration to pivot towards. It's a Blue Initiative
heal without any restrictions to land which is strong in it's own right.
However, it comes with extra utility in restricting the movement of your
enemies. Even if they don't get the heal, Bain is nice enough to let each of the
enemy heroes in radius have a drink, or two....or three. The downside is they
get a little wobbly and are unable to walk in a straight line with a movement
action. This is pretty conditional, and doesn't block stuff like a Brogan charge
or anything that uses the keyword "move" in the card text. It also doesn't
prevent any place or swap actions.

A couple more things to point out about the wording: first, the target does not
actually need to retrieve a discarded card to receive the benefits of the active
effect. Second, you can target any hero with this heal, not just friendlies.
While the utility might be limited, you could theoretically use this to disrupt
heroes like Tali and Garrus who often want a card in their discard to enable
other abilities.

#### Red

**Primary Red: Impaler**

*Target a unit in a straight line, with no friendly units, or terrain between
you. Before the attack: Up to one enemy hero between you and the target discards
a card, or is defeated.*

As mentioned previously, this is the other card in Bain's kit that shares the
line of sight restriction present in his Silver. For the most part the
restriction remains consistent, but be aware that on TI and TII the restriction
encompasses all units and terrain between you and the target. TIII is just
friendly units and terrain.

With the restrictions out of the way, this is a beast of a ranged card. On TIII
it is Initiative 9, Range 5, and Attack 6. Even without items, this card could
potentially decimate a hero if they aren't careful. Also note the potential for
hitting a hero silly enough to be between you and your target.

The trickiest thing with this card is obviously it's restrictions. People
already don't want to be in straight lines from you because of your Silver, so
you need to be able to pick your shots carefully. Normally this is done by
playing Green (either one, but especially your Alt Green) to setup as many
targets for your crossbow as possible.

**Alternate Red: Hunter-Seeker**

*+2 Range if you target a hero with a bounty marker. If you do, may repeat once
on a different adjacent unit.*

*Range - 1*

This card is for when you absolutely, positively, at any cost, want the target
of your bounty dead. We've already established that it is fairly easy to get a
bounty on someone if you want it, so enabling that +2 Range shouldn't be too
tricky. And if you can tag the bounty, you also get to attack something next to
you on TIII, which can let you split between hero killing and pushing (or
potentially just more hero killing).

When not paired with the bounty, the base Range of 1 is pretty brutal, but the
card still has a respectable Initiative and Attack, and comes with some added
Defense (both on the card and in the form of items). This can free you up from
needing to rely so much on your Primary Green to soak up attacks.

#### Ultimate: A Complicated Profession

*After you perform a basic skill, an adjacent enemy hero discards a card, or is
defeated.*

A pretty simple to understand Ultimate that just makes Bain better at what he's
very good at doing. You got hooked? Discard. You got a bounty on you? Discard.
Now check under your seat. It's a another discard! Everybody. Gets. Discards!

### The Paths

**Vlad the Initiator**

[Image 6979466 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6979466)

Our goal with this build is to create a high initiative instigator who can all
but guarantee his hooks and Gold discard as well as threaten an incredibly scary
ranged attack.

**At Tier 2**

Our first level up, we want to get rid of the additional restrictions on our TI
Red while also grabbing a welcome Initiative item to speed up our Gold and
Silver. Next we upgrade our Blue not only to make our Red Attack value a little
scarier, but also to up the potential gold gain we get from Primary Blue. This
will hopefully let us speed up on our other levels. Finally we get our Primary
Green for the added Initiative. Also, because we haven't taken any Defense, we
want to keep it as a potential escape.

**At Tier 3**

I think both Red and Green can be swapped here depending on your needs. I chose
Red first due to the upgrade you get across all your stats. The Green upgrade
gets you that final Initiative item though, and gives you some extra movement
when you block to potentially help you line up more straight line shots. Finally
we get your Blue to make your Red Attack value extra scary and increase your
discard potential.

**The Man In Black**

[Image 6979470 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6979470)

We played it safe with that last build by only sticking to primary cards, so I
wanted to mix it up with this one. Our main goal here is to build the best
bounty hunter we can. Someone who can hone in on a target and shut them down
either by landing the kill or forcing them to skedaddle.

**At Tier 2**

We level up to our Alt Green first to get a handy attack item and free up our
Red and Silver of those line of sight restrictions. Next we get our Primary Red
both for the one Initiative we plan on picking up, and to pair with the Green we
just took. While we intend to switch over to Alternate Red later, having at
least one Initiative on Bain can really open up his Gold and Silver, and this
was the best place I saw to grab it. Next we grab our Primary Blue mainly for
the Attack item, but also to hopefully speed us into our next couple levels with
the added coin gain.

**At Tier 3**

For our first level we switch to our Alternate Red. This gives us a bit more
defense, and is an unrestricted Range 3, 7 Damage hit on our target which feels
pretty good. We then go with our Alt Blue for the Ranged item to further help us
pursue our bounty as well as bump up our base Range by 1. Finally we get our Alt
Green to help us set up some choice 13 Ini hook shots. It also gives us us an
extra Radius which we can use to cover more spaces with our Gold and Blue.
