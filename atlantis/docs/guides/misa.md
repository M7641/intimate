# Misa Hero Spotlight

- Hero: Misa the Samurai
- Author: Panetierre, on BoardGameGeek

## Introduction

Are you ready to turn your Guards of Atlantis world upside down? Then you’ve got
to give Misa a try. Misa is an extremely mobile hero with strong disruption
tools. Her abilities reward good reads and creative planning. And you’ll need to
be ready to think on your feet, since you only have one attack card in hand and
nothing else that can defeat a minion. Despite that glaring issue, I might be
more scared sitting across from a Misa than I am any other hero in the game.

### The Basics

**Gold: Watch Me Soar**

*You may move through obstacles. This Turn: You are immune to non-ranged attacks
and skills. Ignore the effects of tokens. Other units may move through you.*

Misa’s signature move isn’t an attack, doesn’t defeat minions and doesn’t cause
discards. Instead it’s an incredible movement ability that flies across the map
and makes you immune to attacks and skills that can’t point up. There’s no such
thing as catching Misa out of position when she has Gold in hand. She will
happily seize any opportunity to be had anywhere on the board.

However, with only 9 initiative you can’t use your own Gold to escape your
enemy’s Gold attack like you might be used to. It's slower even than some
heroes' Red attacks.

**Silver: Swoop In**

*Place yourself into a space in radius, adjacent to an enemy hero. This Turn:
Heroes in radius must attack you, if able, when performing an attack action. The
first time you discard a card, gain 2 coins.*

Lacking a Gold that can defeat minions could make winning waves difficult for
your team. That’s where your Silver comes in. Denying your enemy farm or even a
hero kill is always a powerful move, and pocketing coins if they want to take a
swing at you sweetens the deal. After that play you have the mobility to make a
getaway, or with your solid defenses you can simply take the hit and keep
fighting.

Because it changes your position at 12 initiative this can fill in as an escape
card, provided there’s another enemy hero nearby for you to jump next to. Notice
that the taunt part of this Silver doesn’t work if you aren’t an eligible target
for their attack. There are unfortunately many heroes with options to ignore
your taunt, whether it’s because they get to move and then attack, or they can’t
target adjacent units, or they simply can’t target heroes.

### The Branches

#### Green

Misa’s Greens also make up for the missing attack on her Gold. Which Green
you’re using says even more about your game plan than whether your Red is melee
or ranged. At Tier 3 your Greens activate when you defend with them, so your
enemies have an even harder choice to make when you catch them with Silver. Oh,
and to make that work they've taken the defense values Red cards typically have.
So you do need to be careful about when you play them, they way you're used to
being careful as other heroes with their Red's defense.

**Primary Green: Mastery**

*Next Turn: After you resolve an action, you may repeat it once. Cannot attack
the same hero twice this way.*

The most direct way to make up for being down an attack card is to use that
attack twice. It’s naturally easier to set up to hit two targets with a ranged
attack than a melee. But there are places to cut down two minions with a sword,
and only a hero with a death wish would get next to you when you’re signaling
that strike.

Because of all the setup time between revealing your Green this turn and
resolving your card next turn, don’t be surprised if the enemy heroes aren’t
around any more when it’s time to repeat your action. And don’t forget that it
repeats more than just attacks. Moving 10 spaces with your Gold or repeating
your Blue skill can get *a lot* of work done.

**Alternate Green: Death From Above**

*Next Turn: The first enemy hero you move through this turn discards a card, or
is defeated. If a hero discards a card this way, gain 2 coins.*

Your Alternate Green is pure hero aggression. Few heroes will ever be out of
your reach when you play Gold, so this discard and income are practically
guaranteed. Land next to them to finish the job later, or in a teamfight fly
through the enemy and land far away out on your own mission, leaving that enemy
for your teammate to finish off.

#### Blue

Misa’s Blue cards are all about rearranging the board. Use these to tear down
your enemy’s plan, or to set up your own. They have enormous potential for combo
plays with your allies.

**Primary Blue: Crushing Squall**

*Move up to 4 spaces in a straight line, ignoring obstacles. You may place 1
unit moved through into a space adjacent to you.*

On Primary Blue, Misa takes to the skies again for a dramatic reposition effect.
Bring a target to a friend, or bring your friend to a target. Your friends that
need to set up straight lines will love you for playing this card. Or you can
pull someone out of position after they had the perfect set up for a kill or
their eye on a minion to ruin their day. If nothing else, ignoring obstacles
during a move is always a welcome ability.

**Alternate Blue: Storm Spirit**

*Swap with an enemy unit in radius, not adjacent to you, or swap two enemy units
in radius, not adjacent to you.*

On Alternate Blue, Misa has an even wilder reposition, albeit more restricted.
Teleporting someone six spaces away is sure to send them reeling. You can move
an enemy on one side of the battle zone way off to the other side, where they
can’t do any harm to your teammates and minions, and at the same time bring that
distant minion over to your friends to be farmed. Or your ally may have been
planning to attack a minion with their Red card, but wouldn’t they rather land
that big attack on an enemy hero instead?

#### Red

Misa’s Reds are strange for having 1s and 2s for defense and just 1 movement,
like a Gold might have. Other heroes have to be careful about using Red to avoid
being defenseless; Misa only has to be careful about spending the one attack she
gets per round. This leaves her free to choose a ranged attack without much
consequence to her defenses, but it’s hard to pass on the undisputed top melee
attack of the game.

**Primary Red: Worthy Opponent**

*Target a unit adjacent to you. If you target a hero, that hero cannot choose to
not defend. After the attack: If the target defends successfully, gain 4 coins.*

On her sword path Primary Red, Misa pairs the strongest attack a Red can have
with the fastest initiative a Red can have. Nobody one-shots a hero like melee
Misa. And while a hero who's dying anyway can usually decline to defend to avoid
discarding a card, keeping that card for after they respawn, against Misa
they’re forced to discard a card to try to defend and then respawn wounded.

On the outside chance someone manages to defend themself, not to worry. You
still get a payday, and without their best defense card they’ll be easy for your
teammate to go and pick off.

**Alternate Red: Thunder Shot**

*Target a unit in range. After the attack: If the target is not adjacent to you,
you may move it 1 space to a space farther away from you, up to two times.*

Misa’s Alternate Red is a short range bow that packs a good punch itself. Great
for taking out wounded targets, or occasionally making the first hit and moving
the target over to your ally for the finishing blow. Most often if moving the
target accomplishes something it’s sending them away from the action, protecting
your team.

#### Ultimate

**Power Overwhelming**

*You may move through obstacles. Your actions with ”Next Turn” active effects
count as if they had a ”This Round” active effect instead.*

As Ultimate Misa you get to charge up with your Green at the top of the round
and menace the board with double actions, or with yet another discard, on every
turn. Terror will come from every direction as you dismantle the enemy team and
seal their doom.

### The Paths

**Coo-rosawa's Revenge**

This path is about using the repeat from Primary Green to clear enemy units off
the board. It’s too much to expect an enemy hero to stay still while you charge
up Green and swing your sword, so we’re picking up the bow and a ranged item to
control as much space as possible. This Misa pushes the minion wave very well,
as you have the potential to kill two minions and save two of your own. And you
can play an initiator-style role with a different flavor given your very strong
ranged attack.

**At Tier 2**

It’s natural to take your Green first so you can catch up on farming ability,
followed by your ranged attack to make the most of your double attack
capability. In some situations your melee attack might not find a target in the
second round, so taking Alternate Red before Primary Green does happen. Then
Alternate Blue can give you a chance to swap out some targets for your bow, if
your target of choice is just out of range.

That defense item from Blue can be important since you’re taking a defense
penalty on this Green option, but if you want that extra attack item instead,
Primary Blue is a fine choice. Similarly, you can opt for an additional
initiative item by giving up an attack item and using the Alternate Green for
Tier 2 before switching back at Tier 3.

**At Tier 3**

The order here depends on what you’ve been needing to get your job done.
Typically you want the skill upgrade and range item you get from Alternate Blue
first. Sometimes the effective +2 initiative upgrade to the Alternate Red will
matter more. And the Primary Green upgrade adding the defense card type can make
a huge difference in some matchups, as can the attack item it gives you.

If you went for the Alternate Green at Tier 2 variant, keep it while it’s
working and switch out when you spot a good opportunity. Or if it’s not working
for you, upgrade your Green first.

**Omae Wa Mou Shindeiru**

This path tries to make the most of your discard Green ability. In many team
comps, your goal will be to put a discard on one enemy, setting up a kill for
your ally, as you fly off to find another target to one-shot with your awesome
sword. In other games your Silver, Primary Blue, and stacked defense will take a
starring role, tanking hits and giving your teammates space to do their thing.

**At Tier 2**

As before, picking up a new Green turns on your aggression and lets you start
playing your game plan. Then there’s some flexibility on this tier. By default I
recommend picking up both defense items so you aren’t too fragile when you want
to play Green into Gold, but in some matchups you’ll need the initiative item to
land the Tier 3 sword, and in other matchups you’ll need the attack item to make
the sword break through their defenses.

If you’re upgrading to Primary Blue it’s best to take it second so you can hold
onto your Gold more often. If you upgrade Primary Red and Alternate Blue, it’s
often best to take the Red first, then Blue, and hope to upgrade out of that
Blue quickly.

**At Tier 3**

These are three excellent upgrades and the order is up to you. If you’re on
Alternate Blue then Primary Blue first makes sense, for synergy with your Green
and a movement item. Otherwise, taking the Alternate Green first is awesome
because it adds a defeat clause so you can last hit with it, and it lets you
threaten to activate it when anyone attacks you. Or if you’ve been wishing you
had just a little more initiative to land your attack, when you upgrade Primary
Red it gets a handy initiative boost, and the radius item helps your Silver
bring you next to your target.
