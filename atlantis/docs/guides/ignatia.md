### Introduction

Every MOBA has a hero like Ignatia. Yasuo. Genji. Invoker. The promise of an
immense power limited only by your skill as a player. We’ve all played against
masters of these heroes and fought alongside dunces. I’m null, your friendly
neighborhood Ignatia main, here to introduce you to one of the most unique and
rewarding heroes to learn in Guards.

Ignatia is a tanky and high damage ranged character. The downside? Every card
has two completely opposite sets of card text, and which text you use depends on
which team’s side the tiebreaker is on. Are you only allowed to hit enemies in a
straight line, or are you only allowed to hit enemies NOT in a straight line?
Are you allowed to swap with an enemy unit, or a friendly unit? Only the
tiebreaker color can decide your fate.

**Pros:**

Very high damage ranged reds

Highest damage ranged gold attack in the game

Ability to secure the tiebreaker in clutch situations

Powerful utility

Great defense values

**Cons:**

Tiebreaker flipping when you don’t expect it can effectively cause you to lose a
turn

No healing makes her vulnerable to card kills

Bad initiatives exacerbate both of these weaknesses

### That Pesky Tiebreaker

Before we even begin the game, there are a couple strategy considerations.
Ignatia is the only hero in the game that plays differently on red or blue team.
Good opponents will try to tie with you and mess you up. If you win a tie, you
will do the effect of your team’s color and then the tiebreaker will flip. If
you lose a tie, the other team will go, flip the tiebreaker to your team’s side,
and you will do the effect of your team’s color. This is important to keep in
mind.

Which heroes will tie you most often? Other tanky heroes with bad initiatives:
Brogan, Wuk, etc. Against these heroes, it is even more likely that you will be
playing the effects of your own team color through ties. Sometimes the opposite
will happen, where ties will be very rare, phasing in when one hero grabs ini,
and phasing out when a different hero grabs it.

It's important to remember that at a turn by turn level, **the tiebreaker can
only flip if a tie happens before or with your card.** This seems self
explanatory, but let me explain how I quickly narrow down if I think the
tiebreaker will flip or not and keep myself from exploding my brain comparing
all potential initiatives in the game. This is also a really useful skill to
have on any hero, not just Ignatia. There are several things you can look at to
predict tiebreaker flips, and it all comes with practice. Let's pretend, just
for the simplicity of the example, that you are playing your red at 7 initiative
and you're in a 2v2. (Note: These examples will go kind of quickly and assume
some familiarity with the game, but I will answer questions in this thread.)

-Did your teammate play gold and blue already? Is their silver slow? No ties.
Have the enemies played blue, and have zero reason to play gold (no legal
targets, no immediate danger to dodge)? No ties unless they're trying to troll
you (in which case shake up your card order and watch them waste their gold
attack trying to tie and throw you off).

-Are both heroes on the enemy team generally faster than you and your teammate,
or is your teammate generally faster than both enemies? Probably no ties. They
can tie with themselves all they want, but if they don't tie with you or your
teammate, no coinflipping.

-Can you identify any card that any hero might want to play? Did someone spend
last turn moving into position to take a minion this turn, or is someone looking
to use a skill to deny you or your teammate a minion? Is your teammate likely to
tie that card? No? You can make a pretty informed decision based on this info.

-At the end of the day, if you can't be sure of which side the tiebreaker will
be on next turn, use this turn to position so you will have a target regardless
of which side the tiebreaker ends up on.

These are just a few of the ways I cross off tie scenarios like I'm doing third
grade multiplication problems. Eventually you get a feel for tiebreaker strategy
outside of individual turns. For example, let's say you're in the jungle, not in
any real danger, and you want to fast travel. You've decided it doesn't really
matter which card you play since you're planning on taking a safe uncontested
minion. Try to grab the tiebreaker back!

### The cards!

**Gold:**

[Image 6927440 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927440)

*Target a non-adjacent minion in range.*

[Image 6927442 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927442)

*Target a non-adjacent hero in range.*

Gold is the best example of the tiebreaker issue. Red can only target
non-adjacent heroes, and blue can only target non-adjacent minions. Many heroes
have 11 initiative on their gold, and if they tie you, you will be stuck with
whatever your team color is. Oftentimes this means that blue team Ignatia is a
little more reliable at farming, and red team Ignatia is a little more reliant
on murder.

**Silver:**

*Flip the Tie Breaker coin to a side of your choice. This turn and next turn:
The coin is not flipped after a tie.*

Ignatia has one of the most niche but powerful silvers in the game. It allows
you to set the tiebreaker token for two whole turns, and while this card is
active, it cannot flip back. This can obviously make sure the tiebreaker doesn’t
flip on you, but generally you will find that as you get more used to Ignatia,
you will not need to use this crutch as often. The other thing this does is
allow the team of your choice to win ties. This means that even in 3 or 4+ way
ties, all members of the team you choose will go before all members of the other
team. Use this to help your teammates win their slapfights with a crucial
tiebreaker flip, or force the other team to move first and allow a teammate to
take a better position after.

**Reds:**

Primary:

[Image 6927440 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927440)

*Target a unit in a straight line.*

[Image 6927442 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927442)

*Target a unit not in a straight line.*

3 range, either hits in a straight line or not a straight line, and on T3, the
straight line option can hit an additional hero after your first target. This is
generally the safer option, as T2 comes with a defense item, it has better range
than the alternate, and it is generally pretty easy to stand in a spot where you
have a target for both modes of the card.

Alternate:

[Image 6927440 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927440)

*Target a unit at maximum range.*

[Image 6927442 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927442)

*Target an adjacent unit.*

2 range, either hits adjacent targets or targets at max range (2, or 3 with a
range item). This is the scary “in your face” red. It comes with more attack and
more defense on the red itself, as well as an initiative item at T2 and a boot
item at T3. At T3, the adjacent option can take an extra minion after your
initial target, offering you the option of taking two minions should the enemies
flee from your terrifying presence.

**Blues:**

Primary:

[Image 6927440 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927440)

*An enemy hero in radius, adjacent to a token, or minion, discards a card, if
able.*

[Image 6927442 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927442)

*An adjacent enemy hero discards a card, if able.*

A discard on either a hero next to you, or one in radius next to a token/minion.
T3 can defeat heroes, which is insane. This also comes with a defense item at
T2, so usually take this branch if you are up against a beefy tank or two who
are building lots of defense. It will help you survive big attacks if they close
in, and the discard will help get around their pesky high defense numbers.

Alternate:

[Image 6927440 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927440)

*Move an adjacent enemy unit up to 3 spaces.*

[Image 6927442 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927442)

*Move a non-adjacent enemy unit in radius up to 1 space.*

Insane utility. Blue side moving an adjacent hero 3 or 4 spaces, repeating even
at T3? Against many heroes, this can undo the entire movement action they spent
last turn rolling up to you. Better yet, throw them into range of a teammate
waiting with a big red attack. Red side moving a non-adjacent enemy hero a space
or two is just as powerful. Nudge a brogan so he can’t charge, nudge an enemy
hero who is expecting to shoot in a straight line, the potential annoyances are
limitless!

**Greens:**

Primary:

[Image 6927440 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927440)

*Move up to 2 spaces in a straight line. Place a magma token in each empty space
you moved through, or out of.*

[Image 6927442 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927442)

*Place up to 2 magma tokens in radius adjacent to another magma token, if able.*

Speaking of annoyances, this card is, once again, one of the most powerful token
placement tools in the game. It allows you to either move in a straight line AND
place tokens, or just place tokens how you’d like. Even at T2 you can block off
half the beach with 3 tokens, and at T4 you get 4.

Alternate:

[Image 6927440 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927440)

*You swap with a friendly unit in radius.*

[Image 6927442 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927442)

*You swap with an enemy unit in radius.*

Swap with an enemy, or swap with a friendly! This is a very restrictive but
powerful positioning tool. It takes an especially long time to get used to
positioning so that you are in a good spot to swap with both an enemy and a
friendly, but it is very flexible and can save you from a lot of sticky
situations, especially with the radius upgrade. Also, T3 is a defense/skill, so
you can surprise your enemies with a mid-turn swap if they attack you.

**Ultimate:**

*After you perform a primary action, you may flip the Tie Breaker token to the
other side and repeat that action once, if able.*

What can I say, it’s one of the best ultimates in the game, as long as you can
line up your targets. Perform the primary action on your card, flip the
tiebreaker to the other side, perform that action too? Like everything else with
Ignatia, at its best it is insane (8 actions a round), at its worst, it does
nothing (you get no extra actions if you can’t do the primary action in the
first place).

### Some Builds!

**The Speedy Fire Truck**

[Image 6927268 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927268)

The best defense is seeing your enemies fry before you. The secret champion of
this build is having a 6 attack 13 initiative range 3 gold attack. Chase your
enemies down with your boot item. If they play their blue/red for lots of
movement, their hand will be lacking in defense for your gold. If they hold
their blue/red, it will let you catch up to them and slap them with a nasty 9
attack red that hits like a truck. You can also always grab a range item instead
of the 9th attack, depending on if it’s overkill or not.

**The Utility Build**

[Image 6927267 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6927267)

This build is all about displacement, swapping, and beefy ranged attacks. Radius
and range help you reach far away targets, although if the 3 range seems like it
is enough, you can always swap it out for another attack item.
