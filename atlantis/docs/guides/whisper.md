## Introduction

[Image 6930149 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6930149)

Whisper the Outcast is a strange beast. He’s a good example of high ceiling/low
floor hero. The first time you’ll play him you might think he’s as useful as a
chocolate teapot, but in the right hands he will wreak havoc on the enemy team
and be the first ban option. This is where I should give you comparables with
popular online MOBAs heroes, having never played those I’ll leave that to my
colleagues in the comments. Whisper is in my book the ultimate aggressive
support hero. No one character in Guards of Atlantis can do it all on their own,
but more than most Whisper relies on teamwork to be at his best. He can be a
great initiator, he can be a great finisher, he can even be a great pusher, but
he certainly can’t be all at the same time so talk with your teammates and make
sure you’re all on the same page about who’s doing what. Depending on branch
choices, your power level will wane and wax with the minion waves, the more
empty spawn points there are, the more potent you’ll be. There are two ways of
emptying those spawn points, kill them (everyone can do this) or move the
minions around, some characters are better at doing this than others, they are
Whisper’s best friends.

### The Basics

**Gold**

*Сhoose one —*

*• An enemy hero in range discards a card,if able; then, if able, that hero
moves the*

*number of spaces equal to that card’s printed movement value in a straight
line.*

*• Defeat a minion adjacent to you.*

High initiative (12), long range, unrestricted discard on the opponent, that’s
all good. Giving the opponent the chance to run away and escape, that’s less
good. Also note that you can’t use it to kill a hero, even if the opponent has
no card to discard, that’s an issue. The key to getting most out of this card is
to be in a position to use it early in the round (so that your or your teammate
can chase the wounded victim) and/or catching an opponent that really doesn’t
want to move that particular turn, or even better that has nowhere to run,
forcing them to stay within reach of you or your teammates for a later kill.

**Silver**

*If an enemy hero in radius has a card in the discard, choose one —*

*• This turn: You are immune to attacks.*

*• You may retrieve a discarded card.*

At a whopping 13 initiative, this is your get out of jail for free card. It
allows you to be aggressive early, even tank an attack with your best card and
be able to retrieve it later or become immune and laugh at your opponent who
just wasted an attack. Just make sure you or a teammate made them discard first,
otherwise they’ll be the one laughing.

### The Branches

#### Green

Both of these options will give you mobility but with different restriction.
High mobility on slow initiative is a great thing, you can see where the
opponents go before deciding where you’ll move. Most of the time my choice on
this branch will depend more on what item I need than what ability I want. This
is more often my last tier I to tier II upgrade.

**Primary Green**

*Place yourself into an empty minion spawn point in range in the battle zone,
**or into a space in range adjacent to that spawn point.***

You won’t get much value out of this card early in the waves, but after a while
it will let you get to a lot of places. ‘’Placing’’ also let you ignore
obstacles to get there, which is always nice.

**Alternate Green**

*If an enemy hero in radius has a card in the discard, +X Movement.*

This will also require some set up to be useful, but note that this a primary
movement card, so you can move with it and activate the text which always feels
like a nice bonus.

#### Blue

Both branches have their wow moments. Both can also be total duds. Which one to
choose will depend on the items you need and your choices with other colors.

**Primary Blue**

*Move up to 3 enemy minions in radius up to X spaces in the same direction; they
can be moved through obstacles.*

This one will let you enable your primary green and primary red, which is good.
If you can do that and move those minions to a spot where they are easier to
kill for you or your teammates, that’s even better.

**Alternate Blue**

*An enemy hero in range chooses one —*

*• That hero discards a card, if able.*

*• You defeat a minion adjacent to you.*

I really like this card for 2 reasons. For one it forces the opponent to pick
between two bad options of them, and their choice might reveal their plans to
you. In addition to that it is basically a third pseudo attack card and can
enable your alt red card and/or give you extra farming.

#### Red

Both of your attacks are pseudo range, but with different restrictions, kind of
like your greens. Fortunately, the rest of your kit and your teammates can help
you with that.

**Primary Red**

*Before the attack: Move up to X space for every empty minion spawn point in
radius*

*in the battle zone, up to a maximum of Y spaces, then target a unit adjacent to
you*

I love this card because it’s just as good at killing minions to push the wave
as it is at chasing enemy heroes and killing them. Its drawback is it’s a low
damage melee only attack if there are no empty spawn points. But later in the
wave, as long as it’s in your hand, there is a huge area that enemies need to
avoid if they don’t want to get hit, especially if you have a radius upgrade.
You might not hit heroes that often with it, but minions are a nice consolation
prize, and the opponent will have to play around it and it will cause them
issues.

**Alternate Red**

*Target a hero in range with a card in the discard, or a unit adjacent to you.*

This branch is really only an option if the game plan is to win by kills and not
care too much about the minions, or you really need those items. On tier 3 it
does come with a movement item, and you can repeat it on a hero, which lets you
attack 2 targets with one card.

#### Ultimate

*After you perform a basic action, you may repeat it once, choosing the same, or
a different option.*

Nothing too flashy here, but having  more actions is always great, especially
when one of them is a long range pseudo-attack.

### The Path

**Big caveat:** I don’t believe there is a single character in this game that
has a clear upgrade path that is objectively better than any other. It’s all
about adapting to the opponents, your teammate, the game state, and all sorts of
things. SOmetimes abilities are more important, sometimes items are more
important. This is more like a couple of default builds I aim for until
something makes me want to change my mind.

**The rolling tank build**

[Image 6930150 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6930150)

The goal here is to tank and be scary, and then take minions after the opponents
have ran away with their tails between their legs

**At Tier 2**

I usually upgrade red first and then go from there. I most often pick my blue
and green based on items more than card text here, I want to match the opponents
defense items with my attack items and vice-versa

**At Tier 3**

You’ll want to upgrade blue first, to get either Radius or Range upgrade, which
will give you a nice boost to many other cards

**The let’s murder them build**

[Image 6930151 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6930151)

This is the ultimate initiator/finisher build. For this to work you’ll need
support from your teammates, no one gets hero kills by themselves in this game
