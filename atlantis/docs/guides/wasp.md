### Introduction

[Image 6964158 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6964158)

Wasp is the “jack of all trades” hero in Guards. She can adapt to any situation,
and no matter what playstyle you enjoy, she has something for you. At her best,
she morphs into the perfect warrior, disrupting her enemies with every move. At
her worst, she gets bullied by more dedicated heroes in their roles, never
seeming to be the hero you need.

### The cards

**Gold:**

Target a unit adjacent to you. After the attack: Cancel active effects on skill
cards of enemy heroes adjacent to you. This turn: Enemy heroes adjacent to you
cannot perform skill actions.

Wasp has very “above-the-curve” basic cards. Gold is an amazing threat just
because of its blend of speed and power, being faster than most other melee gold
attacks, and more powerful than most ranged ones. On top of that, though, you
turn off active effects, meaning heroes that need to set their effects up have
to be careful that you don’t walk up to them on the setup turn, just to turn
their effect back off with a speedy attack. If that wasn’t enough, skills can’t
be performed by adjacent enemies either; you don’t even need to hit a hero for
this. Blue speed escape skills turn into just normal movement, those pesky
active effects get denied, plans you never anticipated will end before they
begin.

**Silver:**

This turn: Enemy units in radius cannot move or fast travel out of radius. Enemy
units outside of radius cannot move or fast travel into radius.

Silver is another card that when timed right will shut your enemies down.
Enemies can’t move in on you, and enemies that are next to you can’t move out.
Note that place effects aren’t effected; people can still teleport in and out,
and while an enemy can’t move through the barrier themselves, someone else can
push/move them in or out with a card effect. This card has so many uses. In
addition to the area denial, it can be used to pin heroes if they’re already in
a tight space, turn a big movement into just a couple spaces, or cut off an
escape route. Whether you prefer enemies to stay out or stay in will also depend
heavily on your build; more on that later.

**Primary Red:**

Target a unit adjacent to you. Before the attack: Up to one enemy hero in
radius, except the target, discards a card, if able.

This is a beefy red that comes with a lot of defense, both on an item at tier 2,
and on the card itself. Combine it with some initiative for a scary punch, or
play it when adjacent to an enemy hero and enemy minion to threaten the big hit,
or a possible discard if you take the minion. This red will help you hold your
ground in an area, and punish enemy heroes for being close by but ignoring you.

**Alternate Red:**

Target a unit in range, not in a straight line.

This is a great ranged red that has great range. You sacrifice a lot of attack
and defense on the card in exchange for being faster and ranged. This makes
farming a lot easier, and while the lower attack means it’s much harder to punch
through a good defense, it makes it easier to either get a discard on a hero
early for a kill later, or catch a hero defenseless late in the round if most of
their good defense has been played out of their hand. Tier 3 it also comes with
a boot item, helping you kite brawlers while making it harder for squishy
targets to escape you.

**Primary Blue:**

Move a unit or a token not adjacent to you and in radius, up to 1 space, without
moving it away from you, or closer to you.

This is a great reposition tool. It’s one of the few effects that can move
friendly heroes, it can nudge an enemy to mess with their positioning, and
sometimes it makes sense to nudge minions as well. Depending on how fast you are
and how slow the enemy heroes are, you can even catch slow golds, or if you’re
playing a slow build, you can reposition someone after they’ve done a blue move.
It comes with attack over defense, so holding it to defend is always good too if
you can’t find a good use for it.

**Alternate Blue:**

Push every enemy unit adjacent to you 2 spaces; each enemy hero that was stopped
by an obstacle discards a card, if able.

This is great for those pesky brawlers that try to get into your face, and can
be used for some cheeky minion passing. It also comes with defense over attack,
so in general this helps keep you safe at the cost of offense. Note that you can
push 0 spaces if the enemy is backed up against an obstacle to get a discard if
you feel you are equipped to fight them in melee.

**Primary Green:**

If the attacker is not adjacent to you, block a ranged attack.

A great tool against those pesky ranged attackers. It helps you win a shooty
duel if you’re on the ranged red, and makes them think twice about bullying you
from afar if you’re melee. On T2 and 3 it also makes them discard, so if they’re
not careful, you can catch them with your quick gold later in the round. Comes
with initiative.

**Alternate Green:**

Place a unit or a token in range, which is not in a straight line, into a space
adjacent to you.

This is a very special reposition tool. Usually when these repositions are fast,
they disrupt the enemy plans, but since this one is slow, it acts much more as
an area denial. If you’re a scary brawling wasp, it adds an attack. Play this to
cover certain spots and turn them into dangerous places for your enemies to end
their movement; if they are successfully deterred, you can always choose to move
when it gets to your turn. This can also be very useful to move minions around;
and if only your friendly heavy remains, this can grab it and place it safely
out of range of enemies. Generally the difficulty in getting this skill to fire
is that you need to have a positioning advantage; you have to already be
standing where your enemies want to move into.

### Some builds...?

**Disclaimer:** I really believe, more than any other hero, Wasp is very build
flexible. I could put just about any combination of items here and it would be
viable. Wasp will teach you how to adapt and build to meet the needs of every
specific match more quickly than any other hero in my opinion. Here are a couple
"staple" builds, with a custom building section after.

**I want to safely farm!**

[Image 6964144 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6964144)

This build has no attack items, and lots of speed and mobility. Be ready to take
a shot at a hero if the enemy slips up, but for the most part this build likes
to keep its distance. The speed will help prevent pesky enemies from disrupting
your farming, but be careful of hard hitting tanks; if they set up somewhere, it
will be dangerous for you to go in. When this build gets set up with boots and
range, tanks won't be able to catch up to you and ranged heroes that attack you
will eat a discard from your block green and will have to run or get hit by your
very fast attacks later in the round. Lastly, being speedy means you go
undisrupted, but be sure to really read all the cards on the table and position
carefully, because once you pick a spot, everyone else will be able to react
accordingly.

**I want to be a scary brawler!**

[Image 6964145 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6964145)

This build likes to set up shop in a nice spot and stay there. Triple defense
means sometimes even your green and silver can block some weaker reds leaving
you free to greedily position with the higher movement blue, and a 9 ini 8
attack red is a huge threat. Set up shop by a high concentration of minions, and
your red will either put a huge dent in a hero, or take a minion and force
someone nearby to discard. Do you think they will play a heal for the discard or
nudge you away from the minion when you play red? Surprise them with a
telekinesis and watch as they scramble. Will you follow up with red, meaning
they should run? Or will you silver, trapping them from escaping?

**I want to adapt!**

So you've played wasp a few times, and you're ready to make custom builds. If
you're not sure what you need to be adapting to, take a look at the stats on the
enemy player boards. They will give you an idea of the stats the enemy is
generally capable of. Then, take a look at what the enemy heroes are trying to
do as you play, see what you can do to counter them, but be **efficient** about
it. You may need 2 defense items before you can even start to go toe to toe with
Ursafar. In that scenario, consider kiting him with range instead and seeing if
your teammate can help. Maybe take the push blue and work to keep it just barely
faster than his red so you only need to worry about gold. On the other end of
the spectrum, you can counter an infinite number of swift attack items by taking
your ranged block. This gives you the space to build towards helping with
swift's teammates. You want to be building items and cards that will give your
enemies a hard time, without pushing yourself too hard to try and counter the
natural advantages of your enemies that they aren't having to invest in
buildwise. It's a tricky skill to learn, but it will help you on any hero.
