# Sabina Hero Spotlight

- Hero: Sabina the Gunslinger
- Author: Panetierre, on BoardGameGeek
- Source: <https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2889623/sabina-hero-spotlight>
- Posted: 2022-06-26

## Introduction

Sabina the Gunslinger is a short range hero who’s all about her friendly
minions. When she’s out alone on the map she’s fragile and has to tread
carefully, but when she’s standing next to her minions she’s a force to be
reckoned with. With her ability to reposition and supercharge her minions, or
drop into the back line and threaten to take the push single-handedly, Sabina’s
a great hero for the tacticians in the room.

### The Basics

**Gold: Point Blank Shot**

Target a unit in range. After the attack: Push the target 1 space if it is
adjacent to you.

Most heroes can only hit or escape with their Gold. Against a melee foe Sabina
gets to have her cake and eat it, too. Razzle ‘em with the hit, dazzle ‘em with
the push, and try to stifle your haughty laughter. 🤭

It’s a weak attack and only at range 1, but you can always upgrade those parts
later. Overall this is a nice Gold attack: quick, reliable, gets the job done.

**Silver: Back To Back**

You may swap with a friendly minion adjacent to you. This Turn: You and friendly
heroes in radius gain +1 Defense if adjacent to one or more friendly minions.

A second escape option on your Silver card? Now that’s a neat trick! Getting out
of a tight spot without giving up one of your precious attacks will always feel
good. Of course, if they were going to hit you they’ll probably take that minion
you just put in front of them instead. But note that you get that extra defense
whether or not you swap; maybe you don’t have to sacrifice them after all.

In practice, this Silver is difficult to put to good use. Sometimes it’s because
the swap isn’t getting you out of range, other times it’s that +1 Defense isn’t
quite enough. I still try to play it most rounds because I don’t want to be
forced to defend with it.

### The Branches

#### Green

**Primary Green: Tactical Advantage**

Move any 1 friendly minion in radius up to 3 spaces; ignore heavy minion
immunity.

May repeat once.

Moving other units is always great, especially when going last. A lot of your
kit asks you to be next to a friendly minion, so use this to stay adjacent and
keep your abilities up. That also keeps the minions in the fights, boosting your
defense and reducing your enemy’s. Or you can simply keep your minion out of
harm’s way. Sabina’s Primary Green is a very versatile tool you’ll always find a
use for.

**Alternate Green: Plot Armor**

If you are adjacent to a friendly minion, block the attack and This Turn: You
are immune to all other enemy actions.

Sabina’s Alternate Green isn’t so versatile, but it might be the best defense
card in the game. All you have to do is stand with your minions like you already
want to and it’ll block any attack. Keep this option in mind when you see your
enemies playing big scary attacks.

#### Blue

**Primary Blue: Field Promotion**

This Round: When you or a friendly hero perform an attack, count all friendly
minions in radius, including the immune ones, as both melee and ranged.

(This can reduce the defense total by up to 2 per minion.)

Does that say -2 Defense per minion? Talk about a kill zone! Move into an area
where your minions are clustered and even the weakest attacks will find kills.
An amazing effect that your enemies have to respect. If they get scared off,
your minions are staying up and you have room to farm so that should be an easy
minion battle.

Occasionally you get the opportunity to bring your active effect into a team
fight where the enemy thought they were safe. But most of the time your Primary
Blue is about setting up a strong defensive position and zoning your enemies
out.

**Alternate Blue: Covering Fire**

If you are adjacent to a friendly minion, an enemy hero in radius discards a
card, or is defeated.

Your Alternate Blue is the more aggressive tool that takes the fight to your
enemies. Like your Alternate Green, you’re getting a great effect with few
strings attached. Everyone wishes they could force a discard early in the turn
order, and you get to do it from a safe distance. A great start to a fight, and
at Tier 3 it gives you a long range finishing blow. Take this when your team
needs a strong initiator.

#### Red

**Primary Red: Dead Shot**

Target a unit in range. +2 Attack if the target hero played an attack card this
turn

Games teach us over and over: melee attacks hit harder and ranged attacks hit
easier. Well, when Sabina has the read on her enemy you can just throw that out.
Even the biggest baddest tanks will drop what they’re doing and run when they
see you’ll catch them with this attack. Take Primary Red when you need big
numbers to deal with big melee heroes. Those lugs are easier to predict, too. 😉

**Alternate Red: Bullet Hell**

Target a unit in range. After the attack: If you defeated a minion with this
attack, and there is no enemy hero in range, you may remove a non-heavy enemy
minion in range.

Ooooh! 🤩 Even at Tier 2 this attack lets you take out an extra minion, a
powerful move other heroes have to wait until Tier 3 to have. Notice that the
text only “removes” that minion, so it’s not providing extra income.

Of course, after your enemies see you do it once they aren’t going to let you do
it again. In reality, this is another card that puts your enemies in a tough
spot, like your Primary Blue. Do they let you get ahead on minions? Or do they
let you kill them?

Often this attack only wounds the enemy who stepped in. Still, firing this
attack early in the round sets your team up to finish them off later. Or your
team can wound them first so you can play this at the end of the round to really
put them in a bind. Again, try to stifle that laugh, but I’ll understand if it
comes out anyway.

#### Ultimate

**Big Sodding Gun**

Your basic attack has +2 Range and +2 Attack. If you push an enemy hero, that
hero discards a card or is defeated.

As if you weren’t scary enough already! Good luck escaping a Gold attack with
this kind of range or surviving the punch its packing. Even if you could, a
Point Blank Shot on turn four with a gun this big is a sure kill no matter what
kind of defenses they’re holding. 😎

### The Paths

**Field General**

This build is all about using your Primary Blue. Having Blue active and in
position is just as good as having those attack items, freeing up your item
slots to upgrade everything else and have it all. Use your Green to reshape the
battlefield and force enemies to come into your fortified position. And a big
Red attack will make the most of your empowered minions, if you’re brave enough
to play both of your good defense cards in the same round. A versatile,
well-rounded build.

**At Tier 2**

First upgrade is naturally the Blue. After that Red and Green can come in either
order. And you can mix it up with the other Red; the plan is to stand with your
minions and that can make up for the lost defense item. It’s an exciting and
risky move. If you go for it, be careful not to get separated from your minions
when the wave pushes to a new battle zone.

**At Tier 3**

Again that Blue upgrade is just too good to wait on, and it comes with a range
upgrade to make you extra dangerous. Again the Green and Red come in either
order. Usually it’s Red first for the movement and finally getting to hit
adjacent units with it, sometimes it’s Green for the initiative item and extra
control over your minions.

The build’s open to using the other Green or the other Red if it feels like a
better counter to your enemies. Having a block card as well as all 3 defense
items can help make Red a safe move after Blue. And extra radius feels great
paired with Primary Blue, just bear in mind that the Alternate Red doesn’t
always like having extra range.

**Pressure Cooker**

My notorious wave clear Sabina build. Some people say this is a cheese strategy.
I say, is it cheese if it still works when your enemy knows it’s coming? Here
you stack all of the attack items to make it as difficult as possible to survive
diving on your bullet.

**At Tier 2**

Red is typically your first or second upgrade, depending on when you think you
have a good opportunity to make a play with it. Against some heroes getting the
block green can be first priority, against others it can wait to the end. You
can take the other Blue to build some defense; if I skip an attack item it’s
probably that one. I’d only take the other Green if that initiative item is
winning me the game then and there.

**At Tier 3**

Upgrading Blue makes it defeat heroes, letting you easily secure those clean-up
kills on your own, and it gives you your third attack item. It’s the obvious
first choice. Upgrading Red then gives you more flexibility in positioning, and
expands the radius on your Blue. Green here is a flex slot; I default to the
second initiative item for closing out the game, but you can get a defense item
if you’ve noticed it’d make a difference or you can’t afford to lose your block
card. Either way, it’s almost always your last level up.
