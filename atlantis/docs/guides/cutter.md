## Cutter Hero Highlight

Edge-lords, -ladies, & -people rejoice!

Cutter is oriented toward PvP.

She has **many ways to deal damage**, and **many opportunities to position
herself** late in a given turn.

She has powerful options to **counter enemies in combat**.

As a friend once said, “Cutter is a real bag of tricks.”

She can maintain a distance from her target until it’s time to strike.

In fact, she insists on you **keeping threats at arms’ length**.

Cutter has several weaknesses too.

Her abilities require **very precise positioning**, more than usual.

This makes her a little more predictable and naturally weak against tokens.

*If you fancy dancing 'round the battle until the most opportune moment, then
you oughta see the Captain!*

## The Two Basics

#### Silver: Grappling Bolt

*INI 2 • **Defense/Skill:***

*Target an obstacle in range and in a straight line, with no obstacles between
you; ignore immunity. Move any number of spaces in a straight line towards that
obstacle.*

This ability is very strong, producing very different results depending on
whether you use its skill action or defense action.

**As a skill:**

- A Silver card that moves!

- Allows you to position at a very slow 2 initiative, and positioning slowly is
  strong.

*(Remember, that’s 2 initiative from the very beginning of the game. For
comparison, the slowest green cards start at 2 initiative and reach 1 initiative
after upgrading.)*

**As a defense:**

- Move for free.

- Adjust your position and minion defense modifiers before calculating the
  Defense Value.

- Then, possibly, you resolve your played card this turn!

#### Gold: Walk the Plank

*INI 12 • **Skill***

***Choose one —***

*• Push an enemy hero adjacent to you up to 3 spaces; if that hero is pushed
into another zone, that hero discards a card, or is defeated.*

*• Defeat a minion adjacent to you.*

This skill's strength lies in flexibility.

You get to facilitate positional advantage, disruption, or combos, and *still*
you can deal damage or even kill.

But more likely, you will do some of that sometimes, and taking minions other
times.

## The Six Branches

#### Green

For any Cutter build, the Green upgrade is the key to the equation.

Your two options here affect the largest difference in gameplay, so read about
each and weigh out your priorities.

**Primary: Crashland**

*INI 3/3/2 • **Skill***

*Move 3, 4 or 5 spaces in a straight line, ignoring obstacles, to a space
adjacent to an enemy hero. That hero discards a card, if able.*

Facilitates Cutter to dance around and through fights and set up kills.

Nearby enemies can easily find safety from this card by positioning adjacent to
you.

Meanwhile, far-away enemies will find it harder to find safety; they will block
your landing spaces, or move aside, positioning farther from your straight
lines.

Note the targeting restriction.

In order to use this skill and exercise its mobility, you must have a target
adjacent to your destination.

If you don’t have a good target to crash, then it offers a nicely slow movement
action.

This ability on Turn 3 provides great setup for a Turn 4 kill with your Gold
card, especially in Round 1 when people’s opening plays and positioning are the
most predictable.

**Alternate : Pathetic!**

*INI 3/3/2 • **Defense***

*If the attacker has a card in their discard, block the attack. If you do, the
attacker discards a card, or is defeated.*

When an enemy has a massive attack, this card allows you to successfully defend
it regardless of Attack Value.

Once that scary enemy is wounded, your options open up tremendously.

Overall, this card rewards early-round damage and aggressive gameplay.

#### Blue

When you’re upgrading your Blue card, consider the following in this order:

1. Do I need the **primary** Blue to ping heroes and enable on my block Green?

2. Do I need the **alternate** Blue to counter my enemies’ crowd control
effects?

3. Which **item** do I need more: Attack or Defense?

4. Do I need additional **income**?

**Primary: Bombardment**

*INI 10/10/11 • **Skill***

*An enemy hero in radius adjacent to another enemy unit, and not adjacent to
you, discards a card, or is defeated.*

Long-ranged damage that doesn’t follow your usual gimmick.

Catch your enemy off guard, with two restrictions:

1. Your target (an enemy) must be adjacent to another enemy.

2. Your target must not be adjacent to you.

**Alternate : A Fistful of Coins**

*INI 10/10/11 • **Skill***

*Place a coin in up to 2 spaces in radius. End of turn: You may place yourself
in a space with a coin. Heroes in a space with a coin gain 1 coin. Remove the
coins.*

This card gives you a lot of value:

- Your final slow-initiative positioning tool. The turn you play this card, you
  have the final decision on your positioning for the next turn.

- Directly counters some of the strongest crowd control abilities. Note that
  this card *places* you; any restrictions to your *movement* are often
  ineffective on this turn.

- In general, it gets you out of dangerous positions.

- Small income gains for you or your allies!

#### Red

**Primary: Fearless Lunge**

*INI 8/9/9 • **Attack***

*Before the attack: You may move 1, 2 or 3 spaces in a straight line and target
a unit adjacent to you in the direction of the move; if you do, gain +3 Attack.
Otherwise, target a unit adjacent to you.*

Cutter’s most iconic card is a super telegraphed attack capable of high damage,
so you will often hear opponents warn each other when you’ve lined it up.

Since Cutter has only one attack, it’s imperative to get a lot of value for
playing it.

Try these three methods in various combinations:

**1. Disruption**

You will often deny **space** or **timing** rather than land this attack.

Your opponents will see the attack coming and try to prevent it from landing.

If you forced your opponent to move, consider it a good turn.

If you forced them to use their red card to move, consider it a great turn.

Let’s suppose on Turn 1, your enemy Silverarrow has moved into position to take
a minion.

It seems they’re planning to use an attack card next turn, which means **they’ll
stand still next turn.**

Fortunately, you get to go last this turn, so you position yourself to lunge at
Silverarrow.

Now, on Turn 2 you play your attack to threaten them.

*(For simplicity, everyone in this example is Level 1, with all their starting
cards.)*

[Image 6944338 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6944338)

Now Silverarrow must choose:

*Follow through with their plan to defeat the melee minion, and must defend a
powerful attack.*

They take the minion.

If they can’t defend, then “Kapow!” money for me and my homies, and Silverarrow
is in the throne room.

If they defend with Red, they’ve exhausted both attacks this turn. Silverarrow
is not a threat for the rest of this round, and if you wanted, still an easy
kill.

You have **punished** the enemy team for advancing their objective.

*Adjust their plan, and dodge your attack.*

They step aside.

They just spent their Gold card to move.

You have successfully **disrupted** the enemies’ plans.

You move afterwards and set up for the next turn, after Silverarrow again.

*Adjust their plan, and retaliate.*

They tickle you with 1 Attack Value, and you discard any card you like.

You can still strike her.

You should beware of being card-killed on Turn 4, but with careful play you’re
mostly safe.

You have **punished & disrupted**, and perhaps still have your Gold card to
impact the minion wave.

Now, what if there’s an enemy minion adjacent to you?

[Image 6944340 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6944340)

**2. Forks**

In Chess, a fork is when you position your piece to threaten multiple units, and
it presents a difficult decision for your opponents.

Disruption plus farming is an excellent play involving a fork.

**3. Bluffing**

Threatening the lunge early in a round is great. Your target will often play a
fast card to dodge your attack.

If you bluffed, then your opponent has very likely wasted their fast card, while
you still have your powerful attack card.

**Alternate: Thumble Shot**

*INI 8/9/9 • **Attack** • RNG 2*

*Target a unit in range in a straight line, with no other units, or terrain,
between you. After the attack: Move up to 3 spaces in a straight line.*

This card is great for making plays while keeping enemies at a distance. If
you’re struggling to generate value with Cutter, try using this branch and
leveraging your increased initiative in a race to win the push.

A small problem with Tier II:

If you defeat a minion, then *after the attack,* you cannot move 2 spaces away
in a straight line away from the target, since *the target is dead.*

Tier III is super busted.

With it, you're flexible and free to impact whomever you like.

In concert with your kit, Thumble Shot lets you impact a large area and stay
safe doing so.

#### Ultimate: Legend of the Skies

*The first time each turn after you perform a primary action, you may perform
the primary action of a card in the previous turn slot.*

One of the strongest ultimate abilities, this allows you to *theoretically*
perform seven primary actions in a round.

With all of those abilities, you could defeat four minions or win a 2v1
scenario.

But, that takes careful planning and very little disruption from your enemies.

While it would be nice to execute such a play, simply **using the Ultimate one
time is already an incredible value.**

Look for brief moments of impact on certain turns, rather than a full round of
maximum abuse.

Realistically, you can play your Red card to move and then perform the attack
next turn

Or, you can perform primary actions at very unusual initiatives.

If you find yourself in a match where you can reach the Ultimate on Cutter, take
a minute with your team to align your goals.

Don't lose yourself in the endless permutations of this card—most of them are
useless.

Notice, your Silver and alternate Blue are particularly effective at
guaranteeing a primary action to trigger Legend of the Skies.

## The Builds

**Item Building Hot Tip**

No single build is right for most matches of Guards. The key to any good build
is adapting to your opponents and aligning your resources with your teammates’.

With that in mind, check out what you could do.

### Giant Killer

No initiative, all attack, all the positioning skills, and some income along the
way.

You cap out at 10 Attack on your red card. Even the brawlers have to pad their
defense to handle a successful attack from you.

You force them to react in some fashion; perhaps items, minion adjacency,
abilities, and *holding red and blue cards.*

One drawback is that your attack items only boost one card, so make it count
with forks and bluffs!

[Image 6944344 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6944344)

[Image 6944345 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6944345)

**At Tier II**

Upgrading your Red first is a strong move. Playing or holding that card becomes
so much more impactful.

But, as with every hero, your decisions about upgrading your Red card directly
affect your initiative.

If you need an initiative item, but want to keep your primary Red, then purchase
the alternate block Green.

**At Tier III**

*Let’s go hunting! For booty or bounty, it’s sure to be fun.*

If this match goes much longer, you could get your Ultimate.

### Tiny Dancer

This build favors initiative items, as much as you would like. You’ll receive
two for purchasing your alternate Red, and one for your early alternate Green.

You start fights exceptionally well, but struggle to end them.

All cards except for Silver can deal damage or defeat your enemies.

Getting your Gold to kill is difficult, so you must rely on your Blue, your Red,
and your teammates to get the last hit.

[Image 6944346 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6944346)

[Image 6944347 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6944347)

**At Tier II**

If you need your block Green, it comes with initiative.

So if you want to skip an initiative item, consider skipping one here on your
Red.

**At Tier III**

You’re ready to fight to the death! If you play your cards right then you can’t
be killed!

If you need to skip initiative again, consider skipping this Red too.

A range item can be quite valuable on your Silver, even if you do not purchase
the ranged attack.
