# Arien Hero Spotlight

- Hero: Arien the Tidemaster
- Author: Panetierre, on BoardGameGeek
- Source: <https://boardgamegeek.com/thread/2900722/arien-hero-spotlight>
- Posted: 2022-07-16
- Printing: not stated

## Introduction

Arien is a master duelist who boasts the strongest attacks and disruption on the
roster. Many of his abilities subtly shift the board in his team’s favor. Others
do it more dramatically. This watery boy is the classic strategist’s choice,
excelling both at setting up friends and messing with enemies.

### The Basics

**Gold: Noble Blade**

*Target a unit adjacent to you. Before the attack: You may move another unit
that is adjacent to the target 1 space.*

Arien's Gold may not be the fastest, but it’s seriously scary with its
staggering 4 attack and its ability to separate his target from a minion that
would otherwise help them survive. What sends it over the top is the complete
flexibility it gives you in moving that other unit. It can pull your target's
defense off them, or pull your minion away to safety. You can help set up a
straight line for a friend by moving them or their target, or ruin a straight
line for an enemy hero. Maybe you just want to move a minion near where you’re
going, or put one of your minions in a less convenient spot for your enemies.
Consistently sliding units adds up over time, turning the tide of battle.

**Silver: Spell Break**

*This Turn: Enemy heroes in radius cannot perform skills.*

Rude! Catch your enemies with this one and watch their best-laid plans crumble.
It’s a narrow effect that only prevents skill actions, of course, so they can
still move or attack — unless you catch them playing their own Silver and they
have no such option. And it relies on you calling the correct turn; miss and
you’ll be playing a statue that turn for nothing. Some heroes don’t mind missing
their skills sometimes, but for many heroes their entire kit relies on them
using the right skill at the right time. And any hero with a skill on their Gold
card instead of an attack has to be very careful not to let it get denied. These
matchups will turn on how well you can read them and sync up your Silver.

### The Branches

#### Green

**Primary Green: Stranger Tide**

*Place yourself into a space in range without a spawn point.*

An awesome teleport that makes Arien terrifyingly mobile, always ready to enter
the fray. At earlier tiers it’s a little restrictive, more useful early in a
minion wave before they’ve started dying. At Tier 3 the restriction is almost
gone and you’re ready to pop in on your enemies at all the worst moments.

**Alternate Green: Empowered Swap**

*Swap with a unit or a token in range.*

Another trick up Arien’s sleeve to adjust the board to his liking. The most
direct use is to pull your friendly hero or minion out of harm’s way, suddenly
threatening their attacker with your own dangerous abilities. But there are
plenty of devious uses, like standing where your friend would like to have a
target and then giving them one through a swap. When there’s a hero around who
sets up and works through tokens, swapping with that token is a potent enabling
move or a rude disabling one.

#### Blue

**Primary Blue: Master Duelist**

*Ignore all minion defense modifiers. This Turn: You are immune to all other
enemy actions.*

A nice defense effect that gives you the freedom to dive into the enemy line.
Melee heroes really like moving in where there are multiple enemy minions, but
there’s a price to pay in defense modifiers for these useful locations. Hold on
to Primary Blue and you’ll be feeling just fine. It only lasts for one hit, of
course, so don’t wait too long before defeating some of those minions. As a nice
bonus it also protects against those coordinated double-hits, so your enemies
have to give you time to find safety between attacks. Bear in mind that friendly
minions don’t support this card, so if you need help against your enemy’s attack
values it’ll have to come from items.

**Alternate Blue: Deluge**

*This Turn and Next Turn: Enemies in radius cannot move more than 1 space with a
movement action.*

Your Alternate Blue is simply amazing crowd control. Unlike other mass CC
abilities in the game, there’s no little positioning exception that can get your
enemies out of it. If they’re in your radius, they’re going to have a hard time.
And at Tier 3 setting it off a turn before they planned to move is no problem
any more. The effect moves with you, too, so even if they have some kind of
skill action that lets them escape now, you’re ready to follow and slow them
again.

#### Red

**Primary Red: Rogue Wave**

*Target a unit in range. After the attack: Move the target up to 1 space. Push
up to one token, or an enemy unit, adjacent to you 1 space.*

Staying on your Primary Red gives you a short 2 range attack with an unusually
strong punch. Building up attack items will make it difficult for anyone to
defend it, but even if they can defend you get to reposition them. There are few
worse events than an enemy hero moving you how they please. Getting to push
something adjacent to you is a nice bonus when it comes up; as with Alternate
Green, with certain heroes around manipulating tokens is always welcome.

**Alternate Red: Raging Waters**

*Target a unit adjacent to you. Before the attack: One enemy hero in any of the
5 spaces in a straight line directly behind the target discards a card, or is
defeated. May repeat once on a different target.*

Switching to Primary Red leaves you as a melee hero, but that 7 attack! Wow!
Threatening a straight line behind your target is extremely cool but difficult
to land, though just making your enemies avoid those spaces can be enough.
Setting up in a spot where you have multiple targets lets you project that
threat over a lot of spaces at once, and at Tier 3 rocks the double attack you
always love to see.

#### Ultimate

**Living Tsunami**

*After you perform a basic skill, you may trigger a minion battle in the battle
zone you are in.*

Get up on the minion count and an extra wave from your quick Silver can close
out the game all on its own. Hunting down those lone, far off minions usually
gives your enemies time to get back into it; Ultimate Arien throws cold water on
that notion.

### The Paths

**Sneaker Wave**

This path builds up Arien’s defense and speed to match his naturally high attack
strength, making him a well-rounded hero. All those initiative items make it
easy to catch targets with your attacks and disruption skills. The slowing Blue
in particular will make it very difficult for anyone to escape your long-range
Red attack. And this Arien is very effective in the minion battle; any of these
cards can protect your own minions in the right situation.

**At Tier 2**

Primary Red is usually your first upgrade because the additional range gives you
an easier time getting more coins. Arien also likes to upgrade his Green quickly
to get stronger mobility options. The Blue is less pressing, and waiting on it
can let you pick your Blue to match your situation. In some matchups the extra
defense isn’t necessary, and taking attack items instead can make you very
scary. Switching out for Primary Blue or Primary Green, even both, works well on
this path.

**At Tier 3**

These upgrades are more about your current situation. The ranged item you get
upgrading your Alternate Blue is often very attractive. So is the Primary Red’s
printed initiative boost and initiative item, which together bump your Red’s
speed by two. Alternate Green rounds out your defenses and fully opens your
options when swapping.

If you’ve been getting good value out of the slowing Blue or your Silver,
switching to Primary Green can be nice to increase the radius on those effects.
And in some games the Primary Blue’s attack item can mean more to you than
additional range, or its defense ability may close off your enemy’s attack plan.

**King Tide**

Taking up a brawler role as Arien plays up his absurd attack strength to leave
the enemy no working options. You’ll often set up in the midst of enemy minions
to threaten as many straight lines as possible with your Alternate Red’s discard
effect. Or you can simply use the movement item enhanced Green to stalk an enemy
hero and smack them with a big Gold or Red hit.

**At Tier 2**

Much of this tier is set up for later in the game. The upgrade order is flexible
and based on your needs in the moment. Taking Primary Green gives you a big
boost in mobility. When you take Alternate Red you get large swings in its
defense and attack values. And you might see a good opportunity to use the
Alternate Blue’s effect to hold someone out of position.

Because your plan is to stay in melee range of enemy minions, those defense
items from Blue and Red are pretty valuable. But if your defenses aren’t being
challenged, you can take the ranged Primary Red instead and enjoy some easier
farming to level up more quickly and take an initiative item. Or you could go
with Primary Blue to have even stronger attacks.

**At Tier 3**

Typically you want the Alternate Red here as soon as you can, using that
movement item to get next to multiple minions and double take, firing off those
straight lines. Getting back on Primary Blue here gives you an easier time
standing in those dangerous locations next to all those minions. Or if you did
take Alternate Blue, you can hold on to that and upgrade Primary Green first to
boost its radius while you still have it.

Green here is a flex slot. The Primary Green’s teleport can be an excellent tool
for getting into good positions for that multi-attack Red, and it’s great for
making an exit. But sometimes the defense item is important, and the Alternate
Green’s swap can set up good lines for your Red’s effect, as well. And if your
team wants you to focus on heroes, using Alternate Blue along with your movement
item leaves them little hope of escape.

## Follow-up by the author (2022-07-16)

Thinking about team composition and matchups is a little difficult because
Arien's so flexible and his tools so versatile. But he's particularly well
suited to opening up spawn points for Dodger and Whisper. And heroes like Wuk
who work through tokens appreciate how he can move targets to tokens or tokens
to targets.

On the opposite side, the Wuks instead hate to see Arien reshape the map and
take apart their plans. And skill focused heroes like NebKher hate to see  your
Silver. But Arien can be a littte light on defenses, so he has to be careful
around other strong attackers, and ranged heroes can be a real thorn in his
side.
