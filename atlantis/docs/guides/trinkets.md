## Introduction

[Image 6993760 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6993760)

Trinkets the Scavenger; Killer Kobold extraordinaire! Trinkets is a light-weight
(literally, probably), fast, highly-mobile, ranged killer who presents an
enormous danger zone in the Venn Diagram between himself and his Turret. Like
Wuk, Trinkets requires prior setup and battlefield foresight in order to
succeed, but if you're someone who likes to plan ahead AND you enjoy watching
your opponents squirm in discomfort while they puzzle out the best way to
accomplish their goals while trying to avoid you, this hero might be the one for
you.

### The Basics

**Gold - Rapid Deployment**

*Choose one —*

*• You may remove the turret. Move up to 3 spaces and place the Turret into a
space adjacent to you. It counts as terrain.*

*• An enemy hero in range of both you and the Turret discards a card, if able.*

*• Defeat a minion in range of both you and the Turret.*

This card is basically the lynchpin of Trinkets' kit. It offers so much utility,
and at 12-ini to boot! It has 4 base movement, 3-move + turret deployment, AND
can defeat minions and wound/kill heroes \*\* (more on heroes later) as long as
the target is within range 2 of both yourself AND the Turret. The trick to
making Trinkets excel is to reposition the Turret as infrequently as possible.
Try to have on/off rounds where you place the turret (ideally with Silver so you
can still shoot twice) one round, and then spend the whole next round using your
excellent placement. Rinse/repeat as the game progresses. Obviously you won't be
able to follow this pattern ALL the time, but it's a good starting point.
Picking the proper turret placement will help keep you relevant.

**Silver - Salvage Parts**

*Choose one —*

*• Place the Turret into a space adjacent to you. It counts as terrain.*

*• Remove the Turret. If you do, move up to 3 spaces.*

With the exception of Round 1 (in which you can only take a single minion
anyways) this will hopefully be one of the primary ways you reposition your
Turret. I typically try to save Gold for shooting, or escaping, whenever
possible. I will often go several games in a row without needing to use the
second option on this card (remove Turret to move), but it IS an option you can
use if you realize you might die otherwise. But it's normally very bad to remove
your Turret without a very good reason because, unlike Wuk, Trinkets is
virtually useless without his Turret on the board.

The image below is a good reference for ideal Turret placement, assuming
Trinkets is on Blue Team. Extreme Turret placement are for the "Oh Shit!"
moments where you think you have a real chance of winning or losing the game. I
often won't venture too deep onto the enemy beach if it looks like I'll have to
pack up and move back into the center the following round.

### The Branches

#### Green

Everything in Trinkets' kit revolves around the Turret. Your Green branches
focus on Turret manipulation, which is an excellent skill to have at low
initiative.

**Primary Green - Perfected Design**

*Swap yourself, any unit, or a token, in radius of the Turret, with the Turret.*

At base 2-initiative, 3-radius, this skill offers fantastic reposition
opportunities without having to use your Gold card. I love that it works on
virtually any obstacles (heroes, units, tokens). You can use this to pull
minions away from enemy heroes, put your Turret into a forward position without
having to move squishy Trinkets into danger, or to swap friendly heroes into the
fray to secure a kill. The Tier 3 version of this branch also comes with +Range
upgrade, which is a HUGE benefit to double-ranged characters like Trinkets. I
would estimate I take this card about 80% of the time I play Trinkets.

**Alternate Green - Emergency Protocol**

*Enemy heroes in radius of the Turret discard a card, or are defeated. Remove
the Turret.*

In theory, this card could net you 5 heroes kills in a single action. This is
the Trinkets dream. Will it ever happen? No -- probably not. But a Kobold can
fantasize about that KABOOM! any day of the week. Like the Primary branch, this
card comes with base 2-ini, 3-radius. More often than not everyone just runs
away from the Turret before you can blow them up, but if you snag a +Radius
upgrade they might not actually have the movement available to do so. Generally
I'd save this card for Turn 4 to guarantee the kills without wasting the Turret,
but an argument can be made to blow it earlier if you can force 2-3 discards in
a single action and it's looking likely that your teammates can follow-up.

#### Blue

The Blue branches focus on denial, centering around the Turret. Conveniently,
denial works great at high initiative.

**Primary Blue - Disruptor Grid**

*You may cancel skills with active effects of enemies in radius of the Turret.*

*This turn and next turn: Enemies in radius of the Turret cannot perform
skills.*

This is one of the best anti-skill cards in the game. Not only does it cancel
already-active Skill effects, but it also offers a following two-turn disruption
at radius 3 from the Turret. Note -- This does NOT work on active effects from
Attack, Movement, or Defense cards. This is a great way to disrupt certain
heroes with Skill-Gold cards that defeat minions. I take this branch against
Skill-heavy opponents (especially those with Skill defeats), and in situations
where I really need the +Initiative item this comes with.

**Alternate Blue - Deployable Bastion**

*Place up to 3 barrier tokens in radius of the Turret and adjacent to another
barrier token, if able. You and friendly heroes gain +1 Defense for each
adjacent barrier token.*

Barrier tokens function in two distinct ways. Firstly, they can throw up a wall
to block enemy heroes at radius 3 from the Turret. Secondly, they can be placed
adjacent to friendly heroes to a) Prevent an enemy from approaching, or b) Give
friendly heroes (especially Trinkets) +1 Defense for each token. Barrier tokens
must be placed adjacent to each other IF POSSIBLE, so with careful and
thoughtful placement you can sometimes split your Barriers up and get twice as
much value out of one action. I like to take this branch against low-mobility
brawlers because the tokens ruin many of their reposition rounds, and it comes
with a +Defense item.

#### Red

Both of Trinkets' Red branches are built for killing. To be honest, I'm not even
sure which branch is better at it. The Primary Red offers speed and enormous
range on your Gold, which grants flexibility in terms of targeting, but the Alt
Red presents massive damage and bonus discards. Both of these branches come with
base range 3 and are viable upgrade options.

**Primary Red - Repeater Cannon**

*Target a unit in range of both you and the Turret. After the attack: Next turn:
Gain +2 Range; if you target a hero with no cards in their hand with your basic
skill, defeat that hero, instead.*

The Primary Red, on paper, is only marginally weaker in terms of stats compared
to the Alt Red (-1 Atk, -1 Def), however taking this branch at Tiers 2 and 3
provides you with +Ini and +Boot, making this a safe, flexible choice. It also
gives you +Range on your Gold, and allows it to defeat heroes without cards
in-hand. If you also take +Range item from Tier 3 Primary Green, then your Venn
Diagram is a range 4 > 5 Wombo-Combo by playing Red into Gold on Turns 3 and 4.
This will undoubtedly make enemy heroes sweat, because this branch comes with
Initiative, so in addition to creating a huge Death Circle, you're also likely
able to do it before they can act.

**Alternate Red - Flame Belcher**

*Target a unit in range of both you and the Turret. After the attack: One other
enemy hero in range of both you and the Turret discards a card, if able.*

I rarely take Tier 2 Steam Discharge unless I'm worried about being one-shot by
heavy-hitting brawlers. With that being said, it's still a very viable choice if
you want to worry slightly less about Trinkets' position. That extra +Def comes
in handy on super squishy heroes. Tier 3 hits hard, and forces extra discards.
Your results may vary, but this tends to work excellently in 3v3 when the board
is slightly more crowded.

#### Ultimate - Fortified Positions

*Whenever you perform an action, you can count any barrier token, or a friendly
minion, in play as the Turret.*

As far as Ultimates go, this one is okay, but not incredibly game-breaking.
Basically this (nearly) removes the need to reposition your Turret for the
remainder of the game. So, action economy. The Ultimate gets less effective the
closer to the end of the game you get, because friendly minions will dwindle as
teams race to clean up Wave 5. Sometimes being able to do something one turn
sooner is all you need to seal the deal.

### The Paths

**Dakka Dakka**

[Image 6993973 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6993973)

This is a long-range Farming build that is also very good at punishing heroes
who venture into your extremely large Death Circle. You'll be relatively low on
Defense with this build, so in the early game you need to rely on Barrier tokens
and your quick Initiative to keep you alive. The goal for this build is to
maneuver yourself and your Turret early in the round with Green, Silver, Blue,
then try to Red > Gold on Turns 3 and 4 to take your two minions, or kill any
wounded heroes at Initiative 15, range 5.

**At Tier 2**

I almost always pick a Red branch upgrade first with Trinkets. He doesn't truly
have Range 2 with his Tier 1 cards; it's actually more like Range 1.5 because of
the Turret restrictions. The last thing Trinkets wants is to get behind in
levels, so upgrading a longer-range Red lets you safely level up in Rounds 2 and
3. Furthermore, Tier 2 Gattling Gun already allows your Gold card to defeat
heroes, so it doubles your threat potential right out the gate. After picking
Red, I'll most often take Blue next because the +Def and Barrier tokens are very
helpful at staying alive and denying your opponents their minions. I can
sometimes make an argument for taking Green as my second upgrade if it looks
like I need the extra +Ini to race for winning the first minion Wave.

**At Tier 3**

I'd probably estimate 90+% of the time I'll take Primary Green (and +Range Item)
as my first Tier 3 upgrade. Going from Range 2-3 to Range 3-4 vastly improves
your ability to target minions and heroes without having to constantly
reposition the Turret. I strongly recommend this upgrade. Action economy is a
valuable resource in Guards of Atlantis, and this is a huge improvement in that
department. My second Tier 3 upgrade is often Red, because it gives +Boots, +Atk
(base Red value), AND +Range (to your Gold). Again, action economy is important,
and this widens your Death Circle considerably. If you plonk your Turret
somewhere in the middle-ish of the Wave, your opponents are going to have a very
hard time doing anything relevant besides running. My last Tier 3 upgrade is
Blue, and I'll frequently switch between branch options depending on the
initiative values of my opponents. If they also have quick initiatives, I'll
take the 3rd +Ini to keep myself on top. If they're playing slow brawlers and
I'm fast enough anyways, I'll take the extra +Def (and Barriers) to further
disrupt their ability to farm/kill me. This build is especially deadly paired
with other initiators, such as Whisper, Cutter, Dodger, Silverarrow, because
that Range 5 Gold is terrifying on Turn 4.

**Extra Crispy with a side of Ketchup**

[Image 6994017 on BoardGameGeek](https://boardgamegeek.com/image/6994017)

This build transforms squishy Trinkets into a modest Brawler. He'll have Defense
values ranging from 3-8, so you won't have to worry too much about being in the
thick of the action. You'll also have access to Barrier tokens (and their +Def)
at Tier 3 if you're worried about someone punching through and one-shotting you.
The objective for this build is to get set-up in the center of the action, and
push people away as they tremble in fear of your 8-Atk Flame Belcher. You won't
have as much range as the Dakka Dakka build, so you'll need to consider your
position a little more carefully. I find this build works best in 3v3 as you
should be able to force plenty of discards, and may even sneak in a few kills
with an exploding Turret as well. You won't be able to kill heroes with your
Gold card, but I often find a Radius-4 exploding Turret more-than makes up for
that.

**At Tier 2**

As with the other Trinkets build, I will most-often upgrade Red as my first Tier
2 upgrade. The extra range greatly benefits your ability to farm, thus getting
you the remainder of your kit quicker. The Green and Blue upgrades at Tier 2
both net you +Atk, and the order in which you pick them doesn't really matter
too much.

**At Tier 3**

At Tier 3, I bounce between upgrading Alt Green and Alt Red first. Alt Red gives
you +Radius, which helps your exploding Turret force multiple discards, but Tier
2 Green still doesn't defeat heroes. Upgrading Alt Green gives you +Atk and the
ability to defeat heroes, but it's much harder to force extra discards with Tier
2 Steam Discharge. Use your best judgement to pick which of these two upgrades
you want first. I almost always pick Deployable Bastion as my final Tier 3
upgrade (As opposed to Primary Blue) because I often really need the extra
Defense to sit in the middle of the action, and I like to use the Barrier tokens
to trap enemy heroes so that they can't escape my exploding Turret.
