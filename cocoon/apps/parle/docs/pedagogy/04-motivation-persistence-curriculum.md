# Motivation, persistence, practice structure and proficiency frameworks

Part of the [Parle pedagogy survey](README.md).

## 1. Motivation theory

**Gardner's socio-educational model.** Masgoret & Gardner's meta-analysis (75
samples, N = 10,489) found the *motivation* composite correlated more strongly
with achievement than integrativeness or either integrative or instrumental
orientation. Correlations were modest (roughly r = .2–.4). *Reasons* for
learning predict much less than the motivational state itself (effort, desire,
affect).

**Dörnyei's L2 Motivational Self System.** Al-Hoorie's meta-analysis (32
reports, 32,078 learners) found the ideal L2 self predicted *intended effort*
strongly (r = .61) but *objective achievement* only weakly (r = .20). A larger
synthesis (100 studies, ~52,000 participants) replicated this pattern. This gap
between self-reported effort and measured attainment is the single most
important caveat in the L2 motivation literature: most of the evidence base
measures intention, not learning.

*Design implications:* build an explicit vision-elaboration step (who the
learner will be as a speaker, in what concrete situations) but never treat
self-reported motivation as a proxy for progress. Instrument actual behaviour
and actual proficiency.

**Self-Determination Theory (Noels et al.).** Autonomous motivation (intrinsic,
identified regulation) is associated with engagement, persistence and
attainment; controlled motivation with lower achievement, self-confidence and
willingness to communicate. The mechanism is satisfaction of autonomy,
competence and relatedness.

*Design implications:* offer meaningful choice of content and route
(autonomy), calibrate difficulty so success is frequent but non-trivial
(competence), and provide some social channel (relatedness). Avoid controlling
language ("you must", "you've fallen behind").

**Directed Motivational Currents (Dörnyei, Muir & Ibrahim).** Intense,
self-propelling motivational surges built from a salient vision, a structure of
sub-goals and routines, and positive emotionality. Evidence is qualitative and
retrospective; treat as a design heuristic.

*Design implications:* time-bounded, vision-linked projects (e.g. six weeks to
hold a 10-minute conversation about your job) with visible sub-goals, rather
than an undifferentiated infinite feed.

**Mindset (Lou & Noels).** Growth language mindsets are associated with
learning goals and reduced helplessness after setbacks. The wider
mindset-intervention literature has shrunk under replication scrutiny.

*Design implications:* frame errors and plateaus as expected, attribute failure
to strategy and exposure rather than talent.

**Flow (Csikszentmihalyi; Egbert 2003).** Flow occurs in L2 tasks and depends on
challenge/skill balance, attention, interest and control. Sample sizes are very
small.

*Design implications:* adaptive difficulty, uninterrupted task blocks, minimal
UI interruption during production tasks.

## 2. Persistence and attrition

**Base rates.** A survival analysis of 3,319 adult MALL users found 43% dropped
out within the first three months; a three-month dormancy period was the median
before either resumption or permanent dropout. Continued engagement was
predicted by intensity (5+ minutes/session), frequency (4+ sessions/week), and
duration (7+ consecutive active weeks); learners who *paused and resumed* were
more persistent than those who never paused. Sudina, Teimouri & Plonsky (601
Duolingo beginners, 6 months) found only two robust predictors of attrition: L2
grit (perseverance of effort) and age (older learners persisted more).

*Design implications:* design around interruption, not around unbroken chains.
A dormant learner is not a lost learner for ~3 months; that is the
re-engagement window. Design a return path (a short re-orientation session, not
a punishing backlog).

**Gamification.** Sailer & Homner's meta-analysis found small-to-moderate
effects: cognitive *g* = 0.49, motivational *g* = 0.36, behavioural *g* = 0.25.
Only the cognitive effect remained stable in the high-rigour subset. Dichev &
Dicheva's review of 51 papers concluded evidence for long-term benefit is
insufficient. Hanus & Fox's 16-week classroom study found the gamified course
produced *lower* intrinsic motivation, satisfaction and exam scores (two intact
non-randomised courses, so suggestive rather than decisive).

Duolingo's own retention figures are commercially reported, not peer-reviewed,
and measure app retention, not learning.

*Design implications:* extrinsic scaffolds (streaks, points, leagues) reliably
move short-run behaviour and can undermine intrinsic motivation over months.
Prefer competence-signalling feedback (what you can now do) to reward
currencies; if streaks are used, make them forgiving (freezes, weekly targets)
and never the primary progress display.

## 3. Deliberate practice and practice quality

Macnamara, Hambrick & Oswald's meta-analysis found deliberate practice
explained 26% of performance variance in games, 21% in music, 18% in sports,
**4% in education**. Ericsson's rebuttal is that the education studies did not
measure deliberate practice as defined. This remains a definitional dispute.

DeKeyser's skill acquisition theory gives the more usable account: declarative
→ proceduralised → automatised, with practice effects largely
**skill-specific**. Repetitive drilling does not automatically produce skill.

*Design implications:* define "practice quality" operationally as (a)
retrieval rather than recognition, (b) immediate corrective feedback, (c)
practice in the modality and direction the learner needs, (d) individualised
difficulty. Do not promise hour-count outcomes.

## 4. Habits, implementation intentions, goals

Lally et al. (N = 96, 12 weeks) found a median of 66 days to reach
automaticity, range 18–254, and **missing a single day did not measurably
impair habit formation**. Gollwitzer & Sheeran's meta-analysis (94 tests,
>8,000 participants) found implementation intentions ("when X, I will do Y")
improved goal attainment with *d* = .65. Locke & Latham's 400+ studies show
specific, difficult goals beat "do your best", conditional on commitment and
feedback.

*Design implications:* onboarding should elicit a concrete if–then plan
(specific cue, place, time), a specific challenging target, and should
explicitly tell learners that one missed day does not reset progress, which
directly contradicts streak mechanics. Expect 2–8 months before study feels
automatic.

## 5. Session length, frequency and distribution

Cepeda et al. established that optimal inter-study interval scales with
retention interval. In L2 specifically the picture is mixed:

- Serrano (2011) and Serrano & Muñoz (2007): intensive distributions can win on
  immediate posttests, spaced distributions win on delayed retention.
- Rogers (2015): longer lags better for grammar learning.
- Suzuki & DeKeyser (2017): massed practice matched distributed on accuracy and
  produced *faster* utterances; massed may be better for
  proceduralisation/fluency.
- Kasprowicz et al. (2019): little difference for young learners' verb
  morphology.

The Duolingo usage study (n = 287) found total minutes correlated with written
but not oral gains, while frequency was the more dependable correlate.

*Design implications:* separate the two goals. Use widely spaced retrieval for
knowledge retention (vocabulary, forms), and clustered repetition within a
session for automatising a target structure. Prioritise session frequency over
length; ~4 sessions/week at 5+ minutes is a defensible engagement floor, but
this predicts retention, not learning rate.

## 6. Self-regulated learning, strategies and autonomy

Chen's meta-analysis of SRL interventions in L2 reported *g* = 1.39 for
achievement (implausibly large, almost certainly inflated by small-study bias),
0.45 for self-efficacy, 0.40 for strategy use. Plonsky's meta-analysis of L2
strategy instruction found a moderate overall effect he characterised as
modest. Oxford's SILL has been criticised on psychometric grounds. Little
defines autonomy as "a capacity for detachment, critical reflection, decision
making and independent action".

*Design implications:* teach a small number of concrete, verifiable strategies
embedded in tasks, with planning/monitoring/reflection prompts at session and
week boundaries. Give the learner real control over goals, content and pace,
and make progress data legible.

## 7. Proficiency frameworks

The CEFR defines competence as *can-do* action in context (the action-oriented
approach, learner as social agent) rather than as knowledge of forms. The 2020
Companion Volume adds descriptor scales for mediation, online interaction,
plurilingual competence, and revised phonology, and drops the native speaker as
reference point.

Hours estimates: Cambridge's cumulative guided-learning-hours figures are A1
60–80 (elsewhere 90–100), A2 160–200, B1 350–400, B2 500–600, C1 700–800, C2
1,000–1,200. FSI's intensive figures for ILR 3 (≈ CEFR B2/C1) are ~600–750
hours (Category I) to 2,200 hours (Category IV). ACTFL↔ILR↔CEFR alignments are
approximate.

*Design implications:* build the curriculum from can-do descriptors backwards;
make each unit's exit criterion a performable task. Publish honest hour
estimates with a stated language-difficulty category and intensity assumption.

## 8. Curriculum design and existing high-quality courses

Backward design (Wiggins & McTighe): identify desired results, then acceptable
evidence, then instruction. Richards distinguishes forward, central and
backward design; structural, notional-functional, task-based and lexical
syllabuses correspond roughly to these.

Existing courses: Middlebury's immersion research (55 students, 9-week Russian
school) found gains of **zero to three ACTFL sublevels**; note the zero.
Duolingo's beginner courses (Jiang et al., n = 225, self-selected completers)
produced Intermediate Low reading and Novice High listening. Choe's review of
Pimsleur credits genuinely spaced and mixed practice and phonological grounding,
while faulting impoverished scripted input, tightly constrained output, no
negotiation of meaning and no written form. **Michel Thomas, Language Transfer
and Assimil have essentially no peer-reviewed efficacy evidence.**

*Design implications:* backward-design each level from CEFR-style tasks;
combine syllabus types deliberately (lexical/functional core, task-based
performance goals, structural sequencing as scaffolding); adopt Pimsleur's
spacing discipline but replace its scripted output with varied,
learner-relevant production; expect wide individual variance and say so.

## 9. Individual differences

Li's meta-analysis (66 studies) found full-length aptitude tests are strong
predictors of general L2 proficiency but weak predictors of vocabulary learning
and writing; aptitude is largely independent of motivation. LLAMA is free and
popular but has documented internal-validity concerns. Linck et al. (79
samples, 3,707 participants) found working memory correlates with L2 outcomes
at ρ = .255. Suzuki & DeKeyser's aptitude-by-treatment interaction study is the
most actionable finding: the *optimal practice distribution differs by aptitude
profile*.

*Design implications:* don't test aptitude to gate or label learners. Adapt
empirically from in-programme behaviour: shorter retrieval intervals and more
repetition for learners showing weaker phonological/working-memory performance;
more explicit rule presentation for high analytic ability; longer lags for
those retaining well. Tell adult learners that adult learning is slower and
less likely to reach nativelike phonology, but that adults have advantages in
explicit learning and metacognition.

## Genuine disagreements

1. **Massed vs distributed for L2 grammar.** Unresolved; likely depends on
   whether the target outcome is retention or fluency.
2. **Deliberate practice.** Ericsson vs Macnamara/Hambrick, definitional.
3. **Gamification.** Small positive effects vs longitudinal harm.
4. **The L2MSS's validity.** Strong prediction of intended effort, weak
   prediction of achievement.
5. **Critical period shape.** Sharp inflection vs steady decline.
6. **TBLT effectiveness magnitude.**
7. **Strategy research measurement.**
