# Language-learning programmes, apps and self-study methods: evidence and critique

Part of the [Parle pedagogy survey](README.md).

## 1. Efficacy studies of commercial apps

### The Vesselinov & Grego series (industry-funded, non-peer-reviewed)

Almost every headline "X hours = one college semester" claim traces to one
two-person team working under contract to the vendors. The template: recruit
the vendor's own users, administer WebCAPE (written vocabulary/grammar) and/or
an oral test, have them study ~2 months, retest.

| Study | n | Design | Headline |
|---|---|---|---|
| Duolingo (2012) | ~196 recruited; 90 of 156 volunteers reached the 30-hour target | 8 wks, WebCAPE, Spanish | ~34 h ≈ one college semester |
| Babbel (2016) | 325 | 8 wks, WebCAPE, Spanish | ~21 h ≈ one semester |
| busuu (2016) | 144 beginners | 8 wks, WebCAPE + OPIc | ~22.5 h ≈ one semester |
| Pimsleur (2019) | 82 novice Spanish users | 8 wks, oral test only | 83% of Level-1 completers gained ≥1 oral level |
| busuu six-language (2025) | 1,205 | 6 mos, 6 languages | 63.1% improved vocab/grammar; ~15.6 h ≈ first semester |

**Limitations.** Single-group pre/post designs with **no control group**,
samples drawn from existing motivated subscribers, and large non-random
attrition. The 2016 Babbel report concedes that weekly research-team emails
"seemed to stimulate the study process". Krashen's (2019) objection to the
Duolingo study: fewer than 60% of volunteers completed the protocol. The "one
college semester" unit is a WebCAPE placement threshold, not a claim about
communicative ability.

### Independent peer-reviewed studies

- **Loewen et al. (2019, *ReCALL*)**: Duolingo Turkish, **n = 9**, one
  semester. Moderate positive correlation between time on app and gains. A case
  study, as its title says.
- **Loewen et al. (2020, *Foreign Language Annals*)**: Babbel Spanish, **n =
  54**, 12 weeks, ~10 min/day. Gains in receptive vocabulary/grammar **and**
  oral communicative ability; study time was the strongest predictor. The
  strongest independent evidence that a well-structured app can move speaking.
- **Nushi & Eqbali (2017)**: structured app review naming Duolingo's
  weaknesses: no grammatical explanation, unnatural sentences, synthetic voices,
  dependence on a bridge language, lexical knowledge that stays passive.

### Duolingo's own research (in-house, transparent, not independent)

- **Jiang, Rollinson, Plonsky, Gustafson & Pajak (2021)**: **n = 225** learners
  who finished beginner content (to end of A2), ACTFL reading and listening.
  Intermediate Low reading; Novice Mid–High listening; "comparable to
  university students after four semesters."
- **Jiang et al. (2021)**: speaking, **n = 258**, Versant: 66% (Spanish) and
  53% (French) reached A2 speaking.
- **DRR-24-04, Jiang, Hopman, Reuveni & Kittredge (2024)**: re-evaluation
  after the 2022 "path" redesign. **n = 165 Spanish + 92 French**, STAMP 4S all
  four skills. Spanish learners: overall ACTFL **Intermediate Mid** (reading
  Int-High, listening Int-Mid, writing Int-Low, speaking *approaching*
  Int-Low). Cost: **~200 hours and ~2,900 sessions over ~276 days** to finish
  A2 content. Two findings for anyone designing a programme: **session
  accuracy correlated positively** with proficiency, while **total time and
  session count correlated negatively**, and the authors concede "the current
  practice system, although personalized, is not yet optimally efficient".
- **DRR-24-01, Kittredge et al. (2024)**: **n = 140**, conversation-initiation
  tasks after 4–6 weeks; claimed as A1 conversational skill. Tasks are short
  and scripted-prompt based.
- **Settles & Meeder (2016, ACL)**: half-life regression, trained on ~13M
  practice traces. Real, replicable, released with data and code; the single
  most transferable piece of engineering in the commercial space.

**Selection effects across all Duolingo reports:** participants must
self-report beginner status, live outside a target-language country, and use no
other resources. A small and unusual slice of real users.

### Attrition: the number that reframes everything

**Sudina & Plonsky (2025)** followed **787** beginner Duolingo learners: **34%
completion, 66% attrition**. Their earlier work found login *frequency*
predicted outcomes while total learning time did not. Efficacy figures quoted
per-completer describe a minority.

## 2. Systematic reviews and the MALL critique

- **Heil, Wu, Lee & Schmidt (2016)** remains the sharpest structural critique.
  They coded the **50 most popular** iOS/Android language apps. **84%** taught
  vocabulary as isolated units; only 53% assessed vocabulary in context;
  **42%** had no grammar instruction at all; **only 3 of 50 (6%)** gave feedback
  explaining *why* an answer was wrong. Only 24% used the microphone.
  Open-ended production was rare and generally limited to one-word utterances.
  Verdict: **these apps are behaviourist in nature**.
- **Burston (2014, 2015)** attacked the research base: applying a minimum bar
  of 4 weeks and 10 subjects, only ~35 MALL studies qualified, and 74 contained
  no quantitative outcome data at all.
- **Chen, Chen, Jia & An (2020)** meta-analysed 80 experimental MALL studies:
  medium-to-strong advantage over control (vocabulary *d* ≈ 0.67). Almost all
  of the effect sits in **vocabulary recognition**.
- **Shortt et al. (2023)** systematic review of Duolingo's gamification: scores
  well on game mechanics; most included studies are small, short, and measure
  recognition rather than use.
- **Hadi Mogavi et al. (2022, ACM L@S)**: nine years of Duolingo forum content
  plus interviews, documenting **"gamification misuse"**: learners fixating on
  streaks, leagues and XP to the point of choosing easy lessons, grinding
  low-value reps, and reporting distress at streak loss.

## 3. Community and polyglot methods

These have explicit, often sophisticated design rationales and essentially
**no controlled evidence**. Read as practitioner theory plus self-reported
outcome data.

- **ALG / AUA Thai (J. Marvin Brown)**: the ancestor of the input-first camp.
  Silent period, no translation, no conscious study, ~1,000+ hours before
  output. Brown reports the first learners to show natural speech appeared
  only after the course exceeded 1,000 hours. Longitudinal observation by the
  programme's own director, not a controlled comparison.
- **Dreaming Spanish**: a 7-level roadmap denominated in **hours of
  comprehensible input**, speaking recommended only at Level 6. Their FAQ is
  candid that the levels "were initially based on the experiences of thousands
  of students that learned Thai with comprehensible input at AUA". Its real
  innovation is the **hour ledger**: a single, honest, non-gameable progress
  metric.
- **Refold (AJATT/MIA ancestry)**: strict input-first sequencing, **sentence
  mining**, "freeflow" vs "intensive" immersion as separate budgeted
  activities, delayed output. The delayed-output rule is contested inside its
  own community and runs against Swain and the interactionist literature.
- **LingQ (Steve Kaufmann)**: extensive reading with an integrated
  known/unknown word tracker. Nation argues a reading-only diet is unbalanced.
- **Michel Thomas / Language Transfer**: audio-only, teacher-led inductive
  construction from a small kernel with L1 scaffolding. No controlled study.
  Lesson: front-load high-generativity structure. Limitation:
  translation-mediated production, no listening comprehension.
- **Pimsleur**: graduated interval recall, 30-minute audio, prompt-and-respond.
  Choe (2016) credits the distributed, mixed recalls and pronunciation emphasis
  while placing the method in the audio-lingual tradition. No reading, tiny
  lexicon, scripted register.
- **Assimil**: two-wave design (a long passive wave, then an active wave of
  back-translation) is a hand-built spacing and retrieval schedule around graded
  dialogues. No efficacy study.
- **Glossika**: mass sentence repetition with SRS. **Clozemaster**: cloze on
  frequency-ranked sentences. **Anki**: the SRS substrate; imposes no
  curriculum, card quality dominates. **Language Reactor / Migaku**: tooling
  that turns Netflix/YouTube into a mineable, dual-subtitled, one-click-to-card
  corpus; solves the "no path to authentic media" problem more directly than
  any commercial app. **italki / Speechling**: the only widely-used tools
  supplying real interlocutors and human corrective feedback at scale.

## 4. Recent developments (2023–2026): LLM tutors

Adoption has outrun evaluation. Du & Daniel (2024) reviewed AI tutors in
language learning (2017–2023) and concluded empirical studies are scarce,
especially for speaking. Avouris (2025) evaluated AI conversational tutors, but
as an expert UX evaluation, not a learning-outcomes trial. Recurring cautions:
LLM tutors **prioritise fluency over accuracy and miss subtle grammatical
errors**; cultural/pragmatic authenticity is weak; voice-data privacy is a
concern. Duolingo's 2025 report claiming its AI "Video Call" improved speaking
is vendor-run. **As of 2026 there is no independent, adequately powered,
controlled trial showing that an LLM conversation partner produces measurable
proficiency gains.**

## 5. What the best ones actually do well

1. **A single honest progress metric that cannot be gamed.** Dreaming
   Spanish's input-hour ledger and LingQ's known-word count beat XP and
   streaks.
2. **Real scheduling.** Half-life regression / FSRS-class algorithms, Assimil's
   two waves, and Pimsleur's graduated intervals all instantiate spacing and
   retrieval properly.
3. **Language in context, at sentence length or longer.** Almost nobody does
   this; Refold/Migaku sentence mining and Assimil dialogues do.
4. **Explanatory corrective feedback.** 6% of apps provided it in 2016. The
   single largest unclaimed differentiator, and the one place LLMs plausibly
   help if constrained to prioritise accuracy over rapport.
5. **A built-in bridge to authentic media.**
6. **Real interaction with a human at some point** (the 2025 busuu study found
   a threshold: at least 1.25 live lessons/week before any measurable benefit).
7. **Accuracy-oriented practice**: Duolingo's own data show session accuracy
   predicts outcomes positively while raw time predicts negatively.

## 6. Recurring failure modes

- **Gamification substituting for learning**: streak preservation, easy-lesson
  selection, league grinding.
- **Isolated vocabulary, no grammar**, or explicit grammar with no exposure.
- **Translation-drill dependence**, producing L1-mediated production and
  passive lexical knowledge (a structural feature of Duolingo, Pimsleur, and
  Michel Thomas alike).
- **No speaking, or scripted speaking only.** Duolingo's own 2024 data place
  speaking below Intermediate Low after ~200 hours.
- **The A2/B1 plateau and no path to authentic media.** Every commercial app's
  evidence stops at end-of-A2, because that is where the content stops.
- **Attrition**: 66% in the best-documented cohort.
- **Evidence theatre**: single-group pre/post studies on self-selected
  subscribers, commissioned by the vendor.
